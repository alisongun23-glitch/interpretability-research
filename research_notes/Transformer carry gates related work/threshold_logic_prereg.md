## Classic threshold logic and depth-2 threshold constructions of parity / symmetric functions

### Takeaway
The single-sigmoid-neuron gate is the McCulloch-Pitts / linear threshold element. Parity (XOR) is not linearly separable, which is the classic Minsky-Papert result. Symmetric functions such as n-bit parity are computed in depth 2 by count-threshold units [n>=k] combined with alternating-sign weights. This construction goes back to the 1960s magnetic-core threshold-logic literature (Minnick 1961) and is treated systematically by Muroga (1971), by Siu, Roychowdhury and Kailath (1991 paper and 1995 book), and by Parberry (1994). So the MLP's [n>=1] - [n>=2] + [n>=3] decomposition is the textbook depth-2 threshold circuit for 3-bit parity.

### Inferences
- The explicit identity parity(x1..xn) = sum_{k=1..n} (-1)^{k+1} [sum_i x_i >= k] is the standard depth-2 threshold construction implied by Minnick, Muroga, and Siu et al. ch. 3. For n=3 it is exactly [n>=1] - [n>=2] + [n>=3], so the paper can present its finding as "the MLP rediscovered the classical depth-2 threshold circuit for a symmetric function," citing Minnick 1961 and Siu et al. 1995.
- Minnick's floor((n+3)/2) bound shows that cheaper constructions than n count-thresholds exist for symmetric functions. Whether the MLP uses the n-unit construction or a compressed one is a falsifiable distinction the paper could note.

## Noise margins, the static discipline, restoring logic, and von Neumann reliability

### Takeaway
The "outputs within eps of the rails for inputs within eps" criterion is the static discipline of digital design, with noise margins NM_L = V_IL - V_OL and NM_H = V_OH - V_IH. It is the MIT 6.002 / Agarwal & Lang framing of the "digital abstraction." Von Neumann (1956) is the classic citation for building reliable computation from unreliable components through restoring/majority organs.

### Cited Findings
- **Agarwal, A. & Lang, J. H. (2005).** *Foundations of Analog and Digital Electronic Circuits.* Morgan Kaufmann / Elsevier, 1008 pp., ISBN 1558607358 / 9781558607354. The Elsevier page confirms Chapter 5 is "The Digital Abstraction," says the book uses abstraction to bridge physics and computer systems, and says it supports the MIT OpenCourseWare circuits and electronics course (6.002). The static discipline and noise margins are introduced in the digital-abstraction chapter. That chapter-level placement is from background knowledge; the Elsevier page lists the chapter title only. — [Source (Elsevier)](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/foundations-of-analog-and-digital-electronic-circuits/agarwal/978-1-55860-735-4); ISBN via [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/1558607358.json)
- **Static discipline as stated in MIT course material (6.111, Fall 2005, "The digital abstraction" tutorial problems):** for an inverter, "if VIN <= VIL then VOUT >= VOH, and if VIN >= VIH then VOUT <= VOL," with "VOL + noise margin = VIL" and "VOH - noise margin = VIH." This is a citable, concrete statement of the discipline the paper imposes on its sigmoid gates (rails at 0/1, eps-tolerant inputs). — [Source](https://web.mit.edu/6.111/www/f2005/tutprobs/digital_answers.html)
- MIT 6.002 Lecture 4 slides (Spring 2007 OCW) also cover the digital abstraction and static discipline, but the PDF text could not be extracted, so they are not quoted. — [Source](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/f648f9566bf6a6c85b52c43afedf2e20_6002_l4.pdf)
- **von Neumann, J. (1956).** "Probabilistic Logics and the Synthesis of Reliable Organisms From Unreliable Components." In C. E. Shannon & J. McCarthy (eds.), *Automata Studies* (Annals of Mathematics Studies, AM-34), Princeton University Press, pp. 43-98. DOI 10.1515/9781400882618-003. Crossref confirms the title, pages and volume. This is the classic analysis of computing reliably with noisy gates via redundancy and "restoring organs" (majority voting). It motivates why gates with a margin (restoring, signal-regenerating behavior) compose robustly. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400882618-003); verified via [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1515/9781400882618-003). Content: [content from background knowledge, not verified on an opened page]
- Hajnal et al. (1993/1987), above, also analyze circuits of unreliable and imprecise threshold gates, a complexity-theoretic counterpart to the noise-margin view. — [Source](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=title.search:threshold%20circuits%20of%20bounded%20depth)

### Inferences
- A sigmoid neuron trained to logit margin m maps eps-perturbed binary inputs to outputs within sigma(-m') of the rails. This is exactly a gate that satisfies the static discipline with V_OL = sigma(-m'), V_OH = 1 - sigma(-m'), and input thresholds set by eps. The 6.111 inequalities give a direct template for the paper's definition (the paper's eps plays the role of the noise margin).
- The "restoring" property (output margin tighter than input margin) is what lets such gates compose without error accumulation. That is the von Neumann / digital-abstraction argument, and it can be cited for why the margin training criterion is principled.

- I could not extract the exact page or section numbers for the static discipline in Agarwal & Lang. Cite "Ch. 5, The Digital Abstraction" and confirm the section from a physical copy.
- I did not find a source that explicitly connects the static discipline to sigmoid neural networks; none surfaced in this search.

It introduced back-propagation and the learning of internal hidden-unit representations. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/323533a0); verified via [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/323533a0)

## Pre-registration and hypothesis testing in ML and interpretability

### Takeaway
Nosek et al. (2018) is the canonical definition and rationale for pre-registration (separating prediction from postdiction). In ML it was instantiated by the Pre-registration workshops: ICCV 2019, NeurIPS 2020 (proceedings in PMLR vol. 148) and NeurIPS 2021, organized by Bertinetto, Henriques, Albanie and others. Within interpretability, the calls for rigor are Lipton & Steinhardt 2018, Leavitt & Morcos 2020 (falsifiable hypotheses), Räuker et al. 2022/23 (survey criticizing unproductive interpretability work), and the causal-scrubbing method from Redwood Research (2022), which tests precisely stated mechanistic hypotheses.

### Cited Findings
- **Nosek, B. A., Ebersole, C. R., DeHaven, A. C. & Mellor, D. T. (2018).** "The preregistration revolution." *PNAS* 115(11): 2600-2606. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1708274114. Crossref confirms the details (published 12 Mar 2018). It is the canonical statement that pre-registration distinguishes confirmatory (prediction) from exploratory (postdiction) research, which is the justification for the paper's pre-registered predictions. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114); verified via [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1073/pnas.1708274114). Content summary: [content from background knowledge, not verified on an opened page]
- **Bertinetto, L., Henriques, J. F., Albanie, S., Paganini, M. & Varol, G. (eds.) (2021).** *NeurIPS 2020 Workshop on Pre-registration in Machine Learning*, 11 Dec 2020, virtual. Proceedings of Machine Learning Research vol. 148, published 8 Jul 2021. The volume has a preface by the editors and 23 Stage-2 papers. — [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v148/)
- **The Pre-registration Workshop, NeurIPS 2021** (13 Dec 2021, virtual), organized by Albanie, Henriques, Bertinetto, Hernández-García, Doughty and Varol. It was the third edition after ICCV 2019 and NeurIPS 2020. It used a two-stage model: Stage 1 proposals with research question, hypotheses and experimental protocol but no confirmatory results, reviewed and accepted on the plan; Stage 2 full results, reviewed for a good-faith execution of the protocol, for a PMLR volume. The stated rationale is that benchmark-driven review favors "beating the state-of-the-art," while plan-first review makes negative results publishable and "separates hypothesis generation from confirmation." — [Source](https://preregister.science/)
- **Lipton, Z. C. & Steinhardt, J. (2018).** "Troubling Trends in Machine Learning Scholarship." arXiv:1807.03341, presented at ICML 2018: The Debates. It names four trends: failing to separate explanation from speculation, failing to identify the sources of empirical gains, "mathiness," and misuse of language. Each comes with a remedy. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03341)
- **Leavitt, M. L. & Morcos, A. S. (2020).** "Towards falsifiable interpretability research." arXiv:2010.12016 (Oct 2020; no venue listed on arXiv). It argues that interpretability over-relies on intuition, producing illusory progress. Using saliency and single-neuron analyses as case studies, it proposes a framework of "strongly falsifiable" interpretability research in which intuition seeds clear, testable hypotheses. This is the most direct precedent for the paper's pre-registered, falsifiable predictions about unit-level mechanisms. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12016)
- **Räuker, T., Ho, A., Casper, S. & Hadfield-Menell, D. (2022; v6 2023).** "Toward Transparent AI: A Survey on Interpreting the Inner Structures of Deep Neural Networks." arXiv:2207.13243. It surveys over 300 inner-interpretability works with a taxonomy by weights, neurons, subnetworks and representations. It argues that much interpretability research is "largely unproductive" and calls for diagnostics, debugging, adversarial testing and benchmarking. (Published at IEEE SaTML 2023 according to my background knowledge; the arXiv page lists no venue.) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13243)
- **Chan, L., Garriga-Alonso, A., Goldowsky-Dill, N., Greenblatt, R., Nitishinskaya, J., Radhakrishnan, A., Shlegeris, B. & Thomas, N. (2022).** "Causal Scrubbing: a method for rigorously testing interpretability hypotheses [Redwood Research]." AI Alignment Forum / LessWrong, posted 3 Dec 2022. The post says authors are sorted alphabetically; the LessWrong record lists the poster LawrenceC and the co-authors by username, so the full names given here are mapped from those usernames. It proposes testing "precisely stated hypotheses about how a particular neural network implements a behavior on a dataset" through behavior-preserving resampling ablations. It criticizes the ad-hoc evaluation of interpretations and seeks an "interpretability equivalent of property-based testing." — [Source](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN/causal-scrubbing-a-method-for-rigorously-testing)

### Inferences
- The paper's pre-registration fits the Nosek et al. confirmatory/exploratory distinction and the NeurIPS pre-registration workshop's Stage-1 model (hypotheses and protocol fixed before results). Citing preregister.science and PMLR v148 shows that ML has an established, if niche, pre-registration practice.
- Leavitt & Morcos explicitly call for falsifiable hypotheses about single-neuron function. The paper's pre-registered claim that MLP units implement count-thresholds [n>=k] combined with signs (+, -, +) answers that call directly. Causal scrubbing gives a complementary operational test (resample-ablate everything the hypothesis says is irrelevant).
- I found no evidence that pre-registration is common practice in mechanistic interpretability specifically. Apart from the general ML pre-registration workshops, no interpretability-specific pre-registration venue surfaced, which supports the paper's novelty claim but is an absence-of-evidence finding.
