# Causal interventions, linear representations, and probing pitfalls (related work for the carry-gate paper)

Scope: prior work relevant to a paper that finds (1) a label-free principal direction of counterfactual differences is causally used (interchange works 97-100%), (2) a label-trained logistic-regression probe direction reads the variable at high accuracy, yet patching along it has zero effect, (3) probes on randomly initialised models read the variable well above chance, and (4) writing a class-mean value along the causal direction breaks the model, because the variable is read relative to other components.

Verification method: every arXiv entry below was opened through the arXiv export API (title, authors, date, abstract, author comment/journal-ref), and the abstract text is the basis for each summary. Venue information comes from the arXiv comment or journal-ref field, or from the Semantic Scholar record (marked "per S2"). For a few papers I also opened the full HTML and took specific numbers from it (marked "full text"). Author lists of 9 or more names are shortened to "et al."

## 1. Activation patching, causal tracing, interchange interventions, and DAS

### Takeaway
Interchange interventions (activation patching) are the standard test of whether a representation is *used*. They come from causal mediation analysis (Vig et al.) and causal abstraction (Geiger et al.). Distributed Alignment Search (DAS) extended them to learned linear subspaces, which is the closest methodological precedent for patching along a single direction. Best-practice papers (Zhang & Nanda; Heimersheim & Nanda) recommend in-distribution counterfactual corruptions over noise or ablation, which supports the label-free "counterfactual difference" direction in finding (1).

### Cited Findings
- **Vig, Gehrmann, Belinkov, Qian, Nevo, Sakenis, Huang, Singer, Shieber (2020). "Causal Mediation Analysis for Interpreting Neural NLP: The Case of Gender Bias." arXiv:2004.12265.** This paper brings causal mediation analysis into NLP interpretability, treating neurons and attention heads as mediators and measuring direct and indirect effects under interventions. It finds that gender-bias effects are sparse, synergistic, and decomposable into direct and indirect paths. Zhang & Nanda (below) cite it as the origin of activation patching. The arXiv comment says "Expanded version"; I did not verify the NeurIPS 2020 venue on any page I opened. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265)
- **Geiger, Lu, Icard, Potts (2021). "Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks." NeurIPS 2021. arXiv:2106.02997.** This paper aligns neural representations with variables of an interpretable causal model and uses *interchange interventions* to check that the representations have the causal properties of their aligned variables. Its applied case study (MQNLI) shows that a BERT model realises parts of a natural-logic causal structure. It is the foundational reference for "interchange intervention accuracy" as an evaluation metric. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997)
- **Geiger, Wu, Lu, Rozner, Kreiss, Icard, Goodman, Potts (2021/22). "Inducing Causal Structure for Interpretable Neural Networks." ICML (per S2). arXiv:2112.00826.** This paper introduces interchange intervention training (IIT), which trains a network so that setting aligned representations to their values on a source input reproduces the causal model's counterfactual behaviour. It guarantees causal abstraction when the IIT loss is zero. It is relevant because it makes "interchange success" the operational definition of a variable being represented and used. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00826)
- **Geiger, Wu, Potts, Icard, Goodman (2023). "Finding Alignments Between Interpretable Causal Variables and Distributed Neural Representations." CLeaR (per S2). arXiv:2303.02536.** This paper introduces Distributed Alignment Search (DAS), which uses gradient descent to learn a rotation or subspace in which interchange interventions realise a high-level variable. Individual neurons can then play several roles, because the variable lives in a non-standard (distributed) basis. DAS is the closest prior method to patching along a learned direction. The user's label-free principal-direction approach is a cheaper, unsupervised alternative to it. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536)
- **Wu, Geiger, Icard, Potts, Goodman (2023). "Interpretability at Scale: Identifying Causal Mechanisms in Alpaca." NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2305.08809.** This paper introduces Boundless DAS, which replaces the remaining brute-force steps with learned parameters and scales to a 7B model. It finds interpretable boolean variables that are causally used in a numerical reasoning task. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08809)
- **Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov (2022). "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT." NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2202.05262.** This paper develops *causal tracing*: it corrupts the subject with noise, then restores clean hidden states one at a time to find activations that are decisive for factual predictions. It localises recall to mid-layer MLPs at subject tokens and edits facts with ROME. It is the standard reference for "restore clean activation into a corrupted run" (denoising) patching. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)
- **Wang, Variengien, Conmy, Shlegeris, Steinhardt (2022). "Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small." ICLR 2023 (per S2). arXiv:2211.00593.** This paper reverse-engineers a 26-head circuit for indirect object identification (IOI) using causal interventions, including path patching and mean ablation. It evaluates the circuit by faithfulness, completeness, and minimality. The causal-scrubbing post (below) notes that IOI defined circuit behaviour by mean-ablating everything outside the circuit. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593)
- **Zhang & Nanda (2023). "Towards Best Practices of Activation Patching in Language Models: Metrics and Methods." ICLR 2024. arXiv:2309.16042.** The abstract states that activation patching is "also known as causal tracing or interchange intervention" and shows that hyperparameters (metric, corruption method) can produce disparate localisation results. The full text says Gaussian-noise and symmetric-token-replacement corruptions "produce inconsistent discovery results" on IOI. It recommends symmetric token replacement because it "supplies in-distribution corrupted prompts." It also warns that out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs are a challenge for "any intervention techniques that introduce OOD inputs ... including ablations." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16042)
- **Heimersheim & Nanda (2024). "How to use and interpret activation patching." arXiv:2404.15255 (tutorial).** This tutorial separates denoising (clean→corrupt, testing *sufficiency*) from noising (corrupt→clean, testing *necessity*) and says the two "are usually not complements of each other." It calls mean ablation "slightly more principled than zero ablating" and says patching tends to take the model "less out-of-distribution than ablation techniques." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15255)
- **Goldowsky-Dill, MacLeod, Sato, Arora (2023). "Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching." arXiv:2304.05969.** This paper formalises path patching, which tests hypotheses that a behaviour is localised to particular paths between components. It is useful if the paper patches the carry variable only along the path into a specific downstream reader. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05969)
- **Conmy, Mavor-Parker, Lynch, Heimersheim, Garriga-Alonso (2023). "Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability." NeurIPS 2023 (Spotlight). arXiv:2304.14997.** This paper systematises the patching-based circuit-discovery workflow (choose metric and dataset, then patch units) and automates edge selection with ACDC. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997)
- **Geiger, Ibeling, Zur, Chaudhary, Chauhan, Huang, Arora, Wu et al. (2023; JMLR vol. 26, 2025 per S2). "Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability." arXiv:2301.04709.** This paper unifies activation and path patching, causal mediation, causal scrubbing, causal tracing, concept erasure, SAEs, DAS, and steering under causal abstraction. It also formalises the linear representation hypothesis and polysemanticity. It is a good umbrella citation for treating "interchange success" as the criterion for use. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04709)
- **Mueller, Brinkmann, Li, Marks, Pal, Prakash, Rager, Sankaranarayanan et al. (2024). "The Quest for the Right Mediator: Surveying Mechanistic Interpretability Through the Lens of Causal Mediation Analysis." Computational Linguistics (per S2). arXiv:2408.01416.** This survey taxonomises interpretability by the type of mediator (neurons, subspaces, and so on) and by the method used to search over mediators. It discusses when each kind is appropriate. It is useful framing for the paper's choice of a one-dimensional subspace mediator. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01416)

### Inferences
- Finding (1) is a DAS-style interchange result reached without supervision. Citing Geiger et al. 2021/2023 and Wu et al. 2023 places "97-100% interchange accuracy" in the standard metric family. Zhang & Nanda's preference for in-distribution counterfactual corruption supports building the direction from counterfactual-pair differences.
- Because noising and denoising are not complements (Heimersheim & Nanda), the paper should state which direction its 97-100% figure refers to, or report both.

### Gaps
- I did not open a proceedings page confirming Vig et al. at NeurIPS 2020 (the arXiv record only says "Expanded version"). The exact CLeaR year for DAS is also not shown on the pages opened (S2 lists the venue as "CLEaR" and the year as 2023, which reflects the arXiv date).

## 2. Interpretability illusions, ablation choice, and why a class-mean overwrite can break a model (findings 2 and 4)

### Takeaway
Several lines of work show that the *value* written during an intervention matters as much as the subspace. Subspace patches can work through dormant pathways (Makelov et al.). Zero, mean, and resample ablations can "spoof" information and push activations off-distribution (Li & Janson; Chan et al.; Zhang & Nanda). Constant replacement values always carry some information. Together these explain finding (4): writing a fixed class-mean along the causal direction is not a neutral "set variable to class k" operation when downstream readers compare that coordinate against other components.

### Cited Findings
- **Makelov, Lange, Nanda (2023). "Is This the Subspace You Are Looking for? An Interpretability Illusion for Subspace Activation Patching." ICLR 2024 (per S2; arXiv comment: NeurIPS 2023 ATTRIB workshop). arXiv:2311.17030.** This paper shows that a subspace patch can make outputs behave as if a feature changed by "activating a dormant parallel pathway" through a subspace that is causally disconnected from outputs. It demonstrates the effect on IOI and factual recall, and links it to the mismatch between fact localisation and editing. It also shows a success case on IOI and lists the extra evidence needed to argue that a patched subspace is faithful. This is directly relevant: the paper's causal direction should be checked against this illusion, for example by showing that the direction is active on-distribution rather than dormant. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030)
- **Wu, Geiger, Huang, Arora, Icard, Potts, Goodman (2024). "A Reply to Makelov et al. (2023)'s 'Interpretability Illusion' Arguments." arXiv:2401.12631.** This reply argues that even intuitive, desirable explanations qualify as "illusions" under Makelov et al.'s definition. It says the observed illusions are artifacts of their training and evaluation paradigms. Cite it together with Makelov et al. for balance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12631)
- **Chan, Garriga-Alonso, Goldowsky-Dill, Greenblatt, Nitishinskaya, Radhakrishnan, Shlegeris, Thomas (Redwood Research, 3 Dec 2022). "Causal Scrubbing: a method for rigorously testing interpretability hypotheses." LessWrong / AI Alignment Forum post.** This post tests interpretability hypotheses through "behavior-preserving resampling ablations." It says that "unlike the standard zero and mean ablations, we ablate modules by resampling activations from *other* inputs." It notes that the IOI work defined circuit behaviour by mean-ablating everything outside the circuit. The author list comes from the post metadata (poster LawrenceC plus coauthors); "jenny" is assumed to be Jenny Nitishinskaya. — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN/causal-scrubbing-a-method-for-rigorously-testing); [Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN/causal-scrubbing-a-method-for-rigorously-testing)
- **Li & Janson (2024). "Optimal ablation for interpretability." NeurIPS 2024 (per S2). arXiv:2409.09951.** The full text distinguishes *deletion* (information lost) from *spoofing* (information inserted by the replacement value). It says spoofing "dominates the Δ measurements for previous ablation methods," and that zero, mean, or resampled values "could result in an input that was never observed during training." It notes that "every possible value ... conveys at least weak information to the model." Optimal ablation instead uses the constant that minimises expected loss, which is "maximally consistent with information from other components." For median IOI components, the optimal-ablation loss gap is about 33% of the mean-ablation gap. This is the closest formal analogue of finding (4): a class-mean written in isolation is inconsistent with the rest of the residual stream. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09951); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09951)
- **McGrath, Rahtz, Kramar, Mikulik, Legg (2023). "The Hydra Effect: Emergent Self-repair in Language Model Computations." arXiv:2307.15771.** This paper shows that ablating one attention layer can cause another to compensate, and that late MLPs downregulate the maximum-likelihood token. It is a caution that ablation and patching effects are not simple sums of component contributions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771)
- **Bolukbasi, Pearce, Yuan, Coenen, Reif, Viégas, Wattenberg (2021). "An Interpretability Illusion for BERT." arXiv:2104.07143.** This paper shows that neurons and linear combinations of activations can spuriously appear to encode a simple concept on one dataset and something else on another. It traces this to embedding-space geometry and narrow corpora, and argues for testing hypotheses on multiple datasets. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07143)
- **Zhang & Nanda (2023)**, as in section 1. Gaussian-noise corruption "may induce unexpected anomalies in the model behavior" and "could even lead to unreliable or illusory results." OOD interventions are a general hazard. — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16042)
- **Sutter, Minder, Hofmann, Pimentel (2025). "The Non-Linear Representation Dilemma: Is Causal Abstraction Enough for Mechanistic Interpretability?" NeurIPS 2025 (Spotlight). arXiv:2507.08802.** This paper proves that with unrestricted (non-linear) alignment maps, any network can be mapped to any algorithm. Empirically, it gets 100% interchange-intervention accuracy on IOI even in *randomly initialised* language models. It argues that causal abstraction is vacuous without assumptions about how information is encoded, which is a strong argument for restricting to linear or one-dimensional maps as the paper does. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08802)

### Inferences
- Finding (4) fits the Li & Janson spoofing/deletion picture and the Zhang & Nanda and Heimersheim & Nanda OOD warnings. A direction can be causally used under *interchange* (the source value is consistent with the rest of a real activation), yet break under a *fixed class-mean* write, because the readout depends on the coordinate relative to other components. The paper can present this as a concrete mechanism for why mean-style interventions mislead.
- Makelov et al.'s dormant-pathway illusion is the main threat to finding (1). The paper's interchange result is stronger if it shows that the direction carries large variance on-distribution (it is a *principal* direction of counterfactual differences, so it plausibly does), and that the effect does not come from an otherwise-unused subspace.
- Sutter et al. imply that the linearity and one-dimensionality of the paper's direction is a feature, not a limitation. A random-init control for the *interchange* metric (not just probes) would also preempt the concern that a powerful alignment map succeeds trivially.

### Gaps
- I did not open the follow-up causal-scrubbing post that justifies resampling over mean ablation in detail, so its specific arguments are not cited.
- I found no paper that names the "relative readout" failure of a class-mean write along a causal direction exactly as the user describes. Li & Janson's spoofing framing is the closest.

## 3. Linear representation hypothesis, and probe directions versus causal directions (finding 2)

### Takeaway
The linear representation hypothesis (Elhage et al.; Park et al.) motivates searching for single directions. Empirical work shows that the direction a logistic-regression probe finds is often *not* the direction the model uses. Marks & Tegmark and Li et al. (ITI) both found that difference-in-means directions are more causally effective than logistic-regression probe weights. CausalGym and RAVEL show that probe directions are weaker interventions than DAS. This is direct precedent for finding (2).

### Cited Findings
- **Elhage, Hume, Olsson, Schiefer, Henighan, Kravec, Hatfield-Dodds, Lasenby et al. (2022). "Toy Models of Superposition." Transformer Circuits Thread; arXiv:2209.10652.** This paper shows in toy models that networks store more sparse features than dimensions in "superposition," which produces polysemantic neurons. It reports phase changes and a link to uniform polytopes. Superposition implies that features are non-orthogonal, which is the mechanism Marks & Tegmark invoke for why logistic-regression probe directions differ from feature directions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10652); [Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html)
- **Park, Choe, Veitch (2023). "The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models." ICML 2024. arXiv:2311.03658.** This paper formalises "linear representation" through counterfactuals, in an output (word) space tied to probing and an input (sentence) space tied to steering. It identifies a non-Euclidean "causal inner product" that unifies the notions. It is useful for arguing that the *readout* direction (probe) and the *intervention* direction (steering or patching) need not coincide under the Euclidean metric. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658)
- **Li, Hopkins, Bau, Viégas, Pfister, Wattenberg (2022). "Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task." ICLR 2023 (oral). arXiv:2210.13382.** This paper finds an emergent *nonlinear* board-state representation in Othello-GPT using probes, and validates it with interventions that control the output. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382)
- **Nanda, Lee, Wattenberg (2023). "Emergent Linear Representations in World Models of Self-Supervised Sequence Models." BlackboxNLP 2023, pp. 16-30 (ACL 2023.blackboxnlp-1.2, per S2). arXiv:2309.00941.** This paper shows that the Othello board *is* linearly decodable once it is probed in a "my colour vs. opponent's colour" basis, and that simple vector arithmetic along the probe directions controls behaviour. The choice of variable parameterisation matters: a variable that looks nonlinear in one labelling can be linear in a relative one. This is relevant to the "read relative to other components" point in finding (4). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941)
- **Marks & Tegmark (2023). "The Geometry of Truth: Emergent Linear Structure in Large Language Model Representations of True/False Datasets." COLM 2024. arXiv:2310.06824.** The abstract says that simple difference-in-means probes generalise as well as other probes "while identifying directions which are more causally implicated in model outputs." Full text, Table 2: mass-mean beats logistic regression in 7 of 8 conditions. For example, on LLaMA-2-13B trained on cities, the normalised indirect effect (F→T) is 0.13 for logistic regression versus 0.77 for mass-mean. The authors argue that logistic regression converges toward a maximum-margin separator that tilts away from the feature direction to reduce interference from non-orthogonal features (superposition). This is the most direct precedent for finding (2). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06824); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06824)
- **Li, Patel, Viégas, Pfister, Wattenberg (2023). "Inference-Time Intervention: Eliciting Truthful Answers from a Language Model." NeurIPS 2023 (spotlight). arXiv:2306.03341.** Full text, Table 3, LLaMA-7B: shifting along the logistic-probe *weight* direction gives True*Info of 34.8%, barely above the random-direction result (31.2%) and the no-intervention baseline (30.5%). The *mass mean shift* gives 42.3%, and the authors adopt it for all other experiments. This is another case where a linear probe direction classifies well but intervenes weakly. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03341); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03341)
- **Arora, Jurafsky, Potts (2024). "CausalGym: Benchmarking causal interpretability methods on linguistic tasks." ACL 2024 (per S2). arXiv:2402.12560.** This benchmark compares the causal efficacy of directions from DAS, linear probes, difference-in-means, PCA, k-means, LDA, and random directions on Pythia models. Full text, Pythia-6.9B overall odds ratios: DAS about 9.95, probe about 3.42, mean-difference about 2.91, PCA about 1.81, LDA about 0.27. The paper states that a probe "achieving high classification accuracy provides no guarantee" that the model uses those classes. It adapts Hewitt & Liang control tasks to interventions. It also warns that "DAS finds causal effect at all timesteps, even when the model has just been initialised." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12560); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12560)
- **Huang, Wu, Potts, Geva, Geiger (2024). "RAVEL: Evaluating Interpretability Methods on Disentangling Language Model Representations." ACL 2024. arXiv:2402.17700.** This benchmark compares interpretability methods, including probes and DAS variants, on isolating and causally intervening on entity attributes in Llama2-7B. It introduces Multi-task DAS, which achieves the best results. It is further evidence that supervised readout methods are not reliable intervention methods. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17700)
- **Tigges, Hollinsworth, Geiger, Nanda (2023). "Linear Representations of Sentiment in Large Language Models." arXiv:2310.15154.** This paper finds that a single sentiment direction is causally relevant, using causal interventions on toy tasks and SST. It isolates the direction with several methods, including difference-in-means and DAS, and describes a "summarization motif." It is a methodological model for validating a single direction by intervention rather than by probe accuracy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15154)
- **Turner, Thiergart, Leech, Udell, Vazquez, Mini, MacDiarmid (2023). "Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering." arXiv:2308.10248.** This paper introduces ActAdd, which computes steering vectors as activation differences between contrastive prompt pairs, with no optimisation and no labels beyond the pair. It is the closest steering analogue of the paper's label-free counterfactual-difference direction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248)
- **Engels, Michaud, Liao, Gurnee, Tegmark (2024). "Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear." ICLR 2025. arXiv:2405.14860.** This paper finds irreducible multi-dimensional features, such as circular day-of-week and month features, that are used for modular-arithmetic tasks. It is a caveat that one-dimensional linear directions are not universal. It is relevant if the carry variable interacts with cyclic digit representations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14860)
- **Gurnee, Nanda, Pauly, Harvey, Troitskii, Bertsimas (2023). "Finding Neurons in a Haystack: Case Studies with Sparse Probing." arXiv:2305.01610.** This paper uses k-sparse linear probes to localise features to neurons and reports superposition in early layers. It is a typical example of probe-based localisation that the paper's finding (2) argues needs causal validation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610)

### Inferences
- Finding (2) has clear precedent: Marks & Tegmark (logistic regression vs mass-mean causal effect), ITI (probe weight vs mass mean shift), and CausalGym (probe < DAS). The new element is the *extreme* version: high readout accuracy with *zero* patching effect in a small, fully characterised model. Marks & Tegmark's max-margin and superposition explanation is a candidate mechanism the paper could test, for example by measuring the cosine between the logistic-probe and causal directions, or by checking whether the probe exploits correlated non-causal features.
- Park et al.'s causal inner product offers a theoretical reason why a probe direction (Euclidean normal) and a causal intervention direction can diverge.

### Gaps
- I did not extract RAVEL's per-method numbers (only the abstract was opened).

## 4. Probing pitfalls: control tasks, random/untrained baselines, and probing vs usage (findings 2 and 3)

### Takeaway
The NLP probing literature established early that (a) probe accuracy partly reflects probe capacity (control tasks), (b) *randomly initialised* encoders give strikingly strong probe accuracy, and (c) decodability does not imply usage (amnesic probing, usage-based probing, probing the probing paradigm). Finding (3), that probes on random-init models read the variable well above chance, replicates (b) in a mechanistic-interpretability setting. Finding (2) is a sharp case of (c).

### Cited Findings
- **Hewitt & Liang (2019). "Designing and Interpreting Probes with Control Tasks." EMNLP 2019. arXiv:1909.03368.** This paper introduces *control tasks*, which map word types to random labels so that only probe memorisation can solve them, and defines *selectivity* as linguistic-task accuracy minus control-task accuracy. It shows that popular MLP probes on ELMo are not selective and that the more accurate layer can be the less selective one. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368)
- **Zhang & Bowman (2018). "Language Modeling Teaches You More Syntax than Translation Does: Lessons Learned Through Auxiliary Task Analysis." BlackboxNLP Workshop, EMNLP 2018. arXiv:1809.10040.** The abstract reports that "representations from randomly-initialized, frozen LSTMs perform strikingly well on our syntactic auxiliary tasks." It adds that this effect "disappears when the amount of training data for the auxiliary tasks is reduced." This is the canonical random-init probing baseline and the direct precedent for finding (3). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10040)
- **Wieting & Kiela (2019). "No Training Required: Exploring Random Encoders for Sentence Classification." ICLR 2019. arXiv:1901.10444.** This paper shows that sentence encoders with *random* parameters over pre-trained embeddings are strong baselines. Trained sentence encoders gain "surprisingly little" over them. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10444)
- **Voita & Titov (2020). "Information-Theoretic Probing with Minimum Description Length." EMNLP 2020, pp. 183-196 (per S2). arXiv:2003.12298.** The abstract states that probe accuracies "do not substantially favour pretrained representations over randomly initialized ones." It also notes that accuracy can be similar for genuine and random synthetic labels. It proposes MDL (description length) probing, which separates the two. This is a methodological fix the paper could use alongside its random-init probe control. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)
- **Pimentel, Valvoda, Hall Maudslay, Zmigrod, Williams, Cotterell (2020). "Information-Theoretic Probing for Linguistic Structure." ACL 2020. arXiv:2004.03061.** This paper recasts probing as estimating mutual information and argues that one should use the best-performing probe, even a complex one. On this view, probe accuracy measures *information present*, not use. This framing explains why a random network, being an injective-ish function of the input, can contain decodable information about the variable. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03061)
- **Belinkov (2022). "Probing Classifiers: Promises, Shortcomings, and Advances." Computational Linguistics 48(1):207-219 (DOI 10.1162/coli_a_00422; ACL 2022.cl-1.7). arXiv:2102.12452.** This critical review of the probing framework covers controls and baselines and the gap between correlation and causation. The ACL PDF table of contents includes sections titled "Comparisons and Controls" and "Correlation vs. Causation." It is the standard survey citation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12452); [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-1.7.pdf)
- **Ravichander, Belinkov, Hovy (2021). "Probing the Probing Paradigm: Does Probing Accuracy Entail Task Relevance?" EACL 2021. arXiv:2005.00719.** This paper shows that NLI models encode linguistic properties that are not needed for their task, and that pretrained word embeddings drive much of this encoding. In controlled synthetic tasks, models encode properties "considerably above chance-level even when distributed in the data as random noise." This directly supports the claim that probe accuracy does not entail use. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00719)
- **Elazar, Ravfogel, Jacovi, Goldberg (2021). "Amnesic Probing: Behavioral Explanation with Amnesic Counterfactuals." TACL (arXiv comment). arXiv:2006.00995.** This paper removes a property with INLP and measures the behavioural change. It finds that "conventional probing performance is not correlated to task importance" and calls for scrutiny of causal claims drawn from probing. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00995)
- **Lasri, Pimentel, Lenci, Poibeau, Cotterell (2022). "Probing for the Usage of Grammatical Number." ACL 2022. arXiv:2204.08831.** This paper proposes *usage-based* probing: intervene to remove an encoding and test whether a behaviour that requires the property degrades. BERT uses a linear encoding of number for agreement, with separate encodings for nouns and verbs. It is a close conceptual precursor of distinguishing "encoded" from "used" directions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08831)
- **Ravfogel, Prasad, Linzen, Goldberg (2021). "Counterfactual Interventions Reveal the Causal Effect of Relative Clause Representations on Agreement Prediction." CoNLL 2021. arXiv:2105.06965.** This paper introduces AlterRep, which builds counterfactual representations by altering how a feature is encoded along probe-derived subspaces and measures the change in predictions. It shows that BERT uses relative-clause boundary information in a grammar-consistent way. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06965)
- **Ravfogel, Elazar, Gonen, Twiton, Goldberg (2020). "Null It Out: Guarding Protected Attributes by Iterative Nullspace Projection." ACL 2020. arXiv:2004.07667.** This paper introduces INLP, which repeatedly trains linear classifiers and projects onto their nullspace to remove linearly decodable information. It is the erasure tool behind amnesic probing. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07667)
- **Ravfogel, Twiton, Goldberg, Cotterell (2022). "Linear Adversarial Concept Erasure." ICML 2022. arXiv:2201.12091.** This paper formulates linear concept erasure (R-LACE) as a minimax game and shows that INLP-style solutions are generally not optimal. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12091)
- **Belrose, Schneider-Joseph, Ravfogel, Cotterell, Raff, Biderman (2023). "LEACE: Perfect linear concept erasure in closed form." NeurIPS 2023 (per S2). arXiv:2306.03819.** This paper gives a closed-form erasure that provably prevents *all* linear classifiers from detecting a concept with minimal change to the representation. It applies "concept scrubbing" at every layer to measure reliance on POS information. It is relevant as the principled way to test whether the logistic-probe subspace is used: erase it and observe whether behaviour changes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819)
- **Kumar, Tan, Sharma (2022). "Probing Classifiers are Unreliable for Concept Removal and Detection." NeurIPS 2022, vol. 35, pp. 17994-18008. arXiv:2207.04153.** This paper proves that even when concept features alone give 100% accuracy, a probing classifier "is likely to use non-concept features." Probe-based removal can therefore fail or destroy task features. This is a theoretical account of why a high-accuracy logistic probe direction can miss the causally used direction (finding 2). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04153)
- **Canby, Davies, Rastogi, Hockenmaier (2024/2025). "How Reliable are Causal Probing Interventions?" IJCNLP-AACL 2025 (arXiv comment). arXiv:2408.15510.** This paper defines *completeness* and *selectivity* for causal probing interventions and finds a tradeoff between them across methods. It reports that nonlinear interventions are almost always more reliable than linear ones. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15510)
- **Adebayo, Gilmer, Muelly, Goodfellow, Hardt, Kim (2018). "Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps." arXiv:1810.03292 (NeurIPS 2018, venue not verified on the page opened).** This paper introduces the model- and data-randomisation sanity checks and shows that some saliency methods are independent of both the model and the data. It is the general interpretability precedent for using a randomly initialised model as a control. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03292)
- **Zhong & Andreas (2024). "Algorithmic Capabilities of Random Transformers." NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2410.04368.** This paper shows that randomly initialised transformers, with only embeddings trained, can perform modular arithmetic, associative recall, decimal addition, and parenthesis balancing. So random networks already contain substantial task-relevant structure. This helps explain finding (3) for an arithmetic or carry variable. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04368)
- **Sutter et al. (2025)** (section 2) and **CausalGym (2024)** (section 3) extend the random-init caution from probes to *interventions*. Sutter et al. get 100% IIA on randomly initialised LMs with non-linear maps, and CausalGym reports that DAS finds causal effects even at initialisation. — [arXiv 2507.08802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08802); [CausalGym full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12560)

### Inferences
- Finding (3) is a known phenomenon (Zhang & Bowman 2018; Wieting & Kiela 2019; Voita & Titov 2020). The paper should cite these as precedent and present its result as confirming that the classic NLP caution applies to mechanistic analysis of small arithmetic transformers. The Zhong & Andreas result offers a reason: random transformers already compute much task-relevant structure.
- Given CausalGym's and Sutter et al.'s warnings, the strongest version of the paper would report that the label-free direction's *interchange* accuracy is near chance on the random-init model, while the probe stays above chance there. That would show the interchange test, unlike probing, discriminates trained from untrained models. This is a suggestion, not a finding from the literature.
- Kumar et al. (theory) and Marks & Tegmark (max-margin tilt) together give a ready explanation for finding (2) that the paper can cite.
