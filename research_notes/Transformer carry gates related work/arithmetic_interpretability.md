# Mechanistic interpretability of addition (and the carry) in small transformers: related work

Scope note: every entry below was checked by opening its arXiv abstract page (and, where stated, the HTML/full text or a proceedings/conference page). Venue information comes only from what those pages showed; where a venue could not be confirmed it is marked "venue not confirmed". Paraphrases are mine. The key reference for the target paper ("in 1-layer addition transformers the carry is a causally used linear direction rather than a gate-like neuron; attention averages a_k and b_k; the MLP builds 3-input parity from count-threshold units") is in the "Inferences" bullets.

## Q1. Which papers reverse-engineer addition/arithmetic circuits in transformers, and what did they find about carry representation?

### Takeaway
The closest prior work is Quirke & Barez (ICLR 2024; 1-layer n-digit addition) and Kruthoff (2024; 2-layer carry-over algorithm). Quirke & Barez analyse the carry at the level of attention heads and conjecture (without proof) that the MLP is a key-value lookup. Kruthoff locates "carrying" in a set of MLP neurons and notes an SVD axis tied to carrying, and says 1-layer models do *not* implement carrying-over. Neither paper shows a causally used linear carry direction, attention as an a_k + b_k averager feeding a parity MLP, or a count-threshold parity construction. The other circuit papers (Nanda; Zhong; Stolfo; Nikankin; Kantamneni & Tegmark; Zhou et al. Fourier; Hanna; Baeumel) study modular addition or pretrained LLMs, not the carry in tiny from-scratch adders.

### Cited Findings

**Small from-scratch addition transformers (most directly relevant)**

- **Quirke, P. & Barez, F. (2024). "Understanding Addition in Transformers." ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.13121.** Studies a one-layer transformer (3 attention heads by default) trained on n-digit integer addition, with answers emitted most-significant digit first. The task decomposes into per-digit parallel streams built from named sub-tasks: Base Add (BA, digit-pair sum mod 10), Make Carry 1 (MC1), Make Sum 9 (MS9), Use Carry 1 (UC1) and Use Sum 9 (US9). Each head attends to a different digit pair with a time offset (a "double staircase" pattern): one head computes MC1 for one digit, one computes MC1 + MS9 (3 states), one computes BA (10 states). The MLP is described as a key-value memory over a 2x3x10 = 60-key "trigram", which the authors explicitly say they "haven't proven experimentally". The 1-layer model cannot do cascading US9 carries (e.g. 445+555=1000); this is the rare high-loss case. The paper makes no claim about linear directions or single neurons representing carry. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13121); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13121)
- **Quirke, P., Neo, C. & Barez, F. "Understanding Addition and Subtraction in Transformers." arXiv:2402.02619 (v1 Feb 2024; v11 Jul 2026; no venue listed on the arXiv page).** Moves to 2–3 layer models with training data enriched for long carry/borrow chains, reaching >99.999% accuracy on 5–15 digit problems. Across 46 models the accurate ones share a common circuit. Carry is formalised as a three-valued **TriCase** (ST ∈ {0, 1, U}, where U means the pair sums to 9), combined by **TriAdd** into cascade values SV. ST/SA nodes are mostly attention heads, and PCA of ST-head outputs shows three clusters (0/1/U). Evidence comes from ablation and activation patching (interchange interventions). The paper says MLP roles were "not analyzed in detail" and that "the smallest model shape that did reduce loss significantly was 2 layers with 3 attention heads". It reports no linear probes or carry directions. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02619); [arXiv HTML v10](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02619v10)
- **Kruthoff, J. (2024). "Carrying over algorithm in transformers." arXiv:2401.07993 (preprint; comment "Comments welcome!").** Studies 3-digit addition with two-layer encoder-only models. Layer-1 attention ("staircase" patterns) plus skip connections adds same-position digits, and the layer-1 MLP sorts sums into <10 vs ≥10, with sum = 9 handled specially. A layer-2 "decision" head decides whether a carry is needed, and "the final MLP actually adds the carried one". The carry is located in a set of roughly 64–650 neurons picked by activation thresholds. An SVD of pre-activation weights gives a leading axis associated with carrying. One-layer models form the staircase "adder" attention but their residual stream does not group examples by carry need, so they do "not implement the carrying over algorithm". Appendix I maps the model onto a **full adder** (two half adders, each XOR for the sum and AND for the carry): layer 1 ≈ first half adder, layer-2 attention ≈ carry passing, final MLP ≈ "the second XOR gate". Ablating the decision head behaves like a "leaky" XOR. The same mechanism appears in 3-layer and small decoder-only models, with suggestive evidence in three 7B LLMs. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07993); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07993v2)

**Modular addition / grokking (Fourier or "clock" mechanisms, no carry)**

- **Nanda, N., Chan, L., Lieberum, T., Smith, J. & Steinhardt, J. (2023). "Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability." ICLR 2023 (oral). arXiv:2301.05217.** Fully reverse-engineers a small transformer that groks modular addition. It embeds inputs with discrete Fourier features and uses trig identities to turn addition into rotation around a circle. This is confirmed via weights, activations and Fourier-space ablations. The paper defines progress measures that split training into memorisation, circuit formation and cleanup. It is the canonical template for "full mechanistic account of an arithmetic task", but it covers modular (single-token) addition, so there is no carry. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217); [ICLR 2023 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11385)
- **Zhong, Z., Liu, Z., Tegmark, M. & Andreas, J. (2023). "The Clock and the Pizza: Two Stories in Mechanistic Explanation of Neural Networks." NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2306.17844.** On modular addition, small changes to hyperparameters or initialisation lead networks to different algorithms: the "Clock", a new "Pizza" algorithm, or parallel mixtures. Even a simple task has many learnable solutions. This motivates checking that a proposed mechanism is the one actually used, across seeds. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17844)

**Arithmetic in pretrained LLMs**

- **Stolfo, A., Belinkov, Y. & Sachan, M. (2023). "A Mechanistic Interpretation of Arithmetic Reasoning in Language Models using Causal Mediation Analysis." EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2305.15054.** Uses causal mediation (activation interventions) to show that attention moves operand/operator information from early mid-sequence positions to the final token, and late MLPs then write result information into the residual stream. Methodological precedent for "attention routes operands, MLP computes the result". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15054)
- **Nikankin, Y., Reusch, A., Mueller, A. & Belinkov, Y. (2025). "Arithmetic Without Algorithms: Language Models Solve Math With a Bag of Heuristics." ICLR 2025. arXiv:2410.21272.** Finds an arithmetic circuit in LLMs whose key components are a sparse set of MLP neurons. Each neuron implements a simple heuristic, such as firing for an operand in a numerical range. Their union yields most correct answers, which is "neither robust algorithms nor memorization". This is relevant as the counterpoint: neuron-level, gate/threshold-like heuristics rather than a clean algorithm. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21272); [ICLR 2025 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/29843)
- **Kantamneni, S. & Tegmark, M. (2025). "Language Models Use Trigonometry to Do Addition." arXiv:2502.00873 (no venue listed on arXiv page).** In three mid-sized LLMs, numbers are represented as a generalised helix (periodic + linear components). Addition is done by manipulating helices with a "Clock"-style algorithm, supported by analysis of MLPs, heads and individual neurons plus causal interventions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00873)
- **Zhou, T., Fu, D., Sharan, V. & Jia, R. (2024). "Pre-trained Large Language Models Use Fourier Features to Compute Addition." NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2406.03445.** MLPs mainly use low-frequency Fourier features to estimate answer magnitude. Attention mainly uses high-frequency features to do modular addition, e.g. the parity (even/odd) of the sum. Models trained from scratch use only low-frequency features and are less accurate, and pre-trained embeddings rescue them. This is a precedent for attention computing a parity-like/mod component of the sum. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03445); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/2cc8dc30e52798b27d37b795cc153310-Abstract.html)
- **Hanna, M., Liu, O. & Variengien, A. (2023). "How does GPT-2 compute greater-than?: Interpreting mathematical abilities in a pre-trained language model." NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2305.00586.** Finds a GPT-2-small circuit for "the war lasted from 1732 to 17__" in which the final MLPs boost years greater than the start year. The same circuit is reused across related tasks. This is a precedent for MLPs implementing a comparison/threshold over a number representation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00586)
- **Baeumel, T., van Genabith, J. & Ostermann, S. (2025). "The Lookahead Limitation: Why Multi-Operand Addition is Hard for LLMs." BlackboxNLP 2025 (ACL Anthology 2025.blackboxnlp-1.15). arXiv:2502.19981.** LLMs (Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, Llama-3-8B) use a one-digit lookahead heuristic to anticipate carries. This works reasonably for two operands but fails for cascading carries in multi-operand sums. A circuit intervention reveals a carry-detection pathway from the next digit position. Note: the venue and findings come from search-result snippets (ACL Anthology / DFKI listing); I did not open the arXiv page directly. — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19981v1); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix-8040/2025.blackboxnlp-1.15)
- **Baeumel, T., Gurgurov, D., al Ghussin, Y., van Genabith, J. & Ostermann, S. (2025). "Modular Arithmetic: Language Models Solve Math Digit by Digit." arXiv:2508.02513.** Finds digit-position-specific circuits (groups of MLP neurons for units/tens/hundreds) across model sizes and tokenisations. Causal interventions on one circuit change only that digit's prediction. The arXiv page lists no venue; a search snippet attributes it to IJCNLP-AACL 2025 (not confirmed). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02513)

### Inferences
- Positioning of the target paper vs. Quirke & Barez (2024): they use the same 1-layer setting but analyse at head granularity, with the carry as a head-computed MC1 state and the MLP as an unproven 60-key lookup. A paper showing (i) attention averaging a_k and b_k, (ii) a causally used linear carry direction and (iii) an MLP parity construction from count-threshold units would fill the gap they flag ("we haven't proven this experimentally").
- Positioning vs. Kruthoff (2024): he identifies carrying with a neuron set (activation-threshold selection) plus an SVD axis, and explicitly compares the model to XOR/AND gates. This is the nearest "gate-like" framing to argue against or refine. His claim that 1-layer models do *not* implement carrying-over contrasts with Quirke & Barez's 1-layer model handling single carries. The difference is likely due to output order: MSB-first in Quirke & Barez, where the answer token is produced after all inputs are visible. The target paper should state clearly which output order and carry regime it studies.
- Nikankin et al. (neuron-level threshold heuristics) and Hanna et al. (MLP comparisons) support the plausibility of "count-threshold units" as building blocks. Zhou et al. (attention computes mod-2/high-frequency parts of the sum) supports attention delivering sum information that later stages turn into parity.
- Zhong et al. warns that mechanisms vary across seeds and hyperparameters, so claims should be checked across seeds, as Quirke et al. did with 46 models.

### Gaps
- No paper found that explicitly tests "carry = linear direction vs. single gate-like neuron" with causal steering/patching along a direction in a 1-layer addition model. One broad search for carry plus linear-representation work returned nothing specific.
- I did not find a published paper that describes attention in addition models as *averaging* the two operand-digit embeddings (a_k + b_k)/2. Kruthoff's "staircase attention + skip connection acts as an adder" is the closest statement.
- No paper found that describes an MLP building 3-input parity (a XOR b XOR c) from count-threshold neurons in an addition transformer.

## Q2. What did prior work find about digit ordering (reversed / LSB-first outputs), carry and length generalisation?

### Takeaway
Output order and positional information dominate whether small transformers learn addition. LSB-first (reversed) output turns each answer digit into a local function of (a_k, b_k, carry-in) and greatly improves sample efficiency. Length generalisation depends on positional encoding and format: the best results use index hints, relative/no positional encoding, or Abacus embeddings, and they remain fragile across seeds.

### Cited Findings
- **Lee, N., Sreenivasan, K., Lee, J. D., Lee, K. & Papailiopoulos, D. (2024). "Teaching Arithmetic to Small Transformers." ICLR 2024. arXiv:2307.03381.** Plain (MSB-first) output plateaus at a little over 85% even with 10k samples, because the first output digit depends on all operand digits. Reversed (LSB-first) output needs only a local rule (two digits + incoming carry) and learns 3-digit addition perfectly with about 1k–4k samples. The sharp phase transition in sample count is linked to low-rank matrix completion (the addition table is rank 2), though the model generalises in ways matrix completion cannot. Chain-of-thought/scratchpad data further improves accuracy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03381); [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6bf82fdcbd92b6a7793b3894422d2437-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Zhou, H., Bradley, A., Littwin, E., Razin, N., Saremi, O., Susskind, J., Bengio, S. & Nakkiran, P. (2024). "What Algorithms can Transformers Learn? A Study in Length Generalization." ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.16028.** Proposes the RASP-Generalization Conjecture: transformers length-generalise when a short RASP-L program solves the task at all lengths. Format changes guided by this (e.g. for addition and parity) substantially improve length generalisation, and scratchpads can hurt when they make the RASP-L program more complex. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028); [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/45ed1a72597594c097152ef9cc187762-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **McLeish, S., Bansal, A., Stein, A., Jain, N., Kirchenbauer, J., Bartoldson, B. R., Kailkhura, B., Bhatele, A., Geiping, J., Schwarzschild, A. & Goldstein, T. (2024). "Transformers Can Do Arithmetic with the Right Embeddings." NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2405.17399.** Argues the core failure is losing track of each digit's position within a number. "Abacus" embeddings encode position relative to the start of the number. Trained on ≤20-digit numbers, models reach up to 99% accuracy on 100-digit addition (one GPU, one day). Input injection and recurrent layers help further. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17399); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c35986bc1ee29b31c1011481b77fe540-Abstract.html)
- **Shen, R., Bubeck, S., Eldan, R., Lee, Y. T., Li, Y. & Zhang, Y. (2023). "Positional Description Matters for Transformers Arithmetic." arXiv:2311.14737 (no venue on arXiv page).** Transformers over-rely on positional information, which breaks for longer numbers. Modifying positional encoding or the data representation gives 15-digit multiplication (vs. failure at 4 digits for standard training), addition extrapolation from 10 to 12 digits, and natural-language addition up to 5 digits. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14737)
- **Kazemnejad, A., Padhi, I., Ramamurthy, K. N., Das, P. & Reddy, S. (2023). "The Impact of Positional Encoding on Length Generalization in Transformers." NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2305.19466.** On reasoning/math tasks, absolute embeddings, ALiBi and RoPE generalise poorly to longer inputs. No positional encoding (NoPE) does better and in practice learns T5-like relative attention patterns. Scratchpads do not consistently help, and their format matters. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466)
- **Zhou, Y., Alon, U., Chen, X., Wang, X., Agarwal, R. & Zhou, D. (2024). "Transformers Can Achieve Length Generalization But Not Robustly." arXiv:2402.09371 (no venue on arXiv page).** With the right data format and position encoding, standard transformers extrapolate decimal addition to 2.5x the training length, but results vary a lot with seed and data order. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09371)
- **Jelassi, S., d'Ascoli, S., Domingo-Enrich, C., Wu, Y., Li, Y. & Charton, F. (2023). "Length Generalization in Arithmetic Transformers." arXiv:2306.15400 (no venue on arXiv page).** Relative position embeddings let 5-digit-trained models do 15-digit addition but fail for multiplication. "Train set priming" with 10–50 long examples fixes multiplication length generalisation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15400)
- Ordering in the mechanistic papers: Quirke & Barez (2024) and Quirke, Neo & Barez emit MSB-first, so the first answer digit must resolve a carry cascaded from the rightmost digit in one forward pass. This is why cascading US9 cases are hard for the 1-layer model. — [arXiv HTML 2310.13121](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13121); [arXiv HTML 2402.02619v10](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02619v10)

### Inferences
- With LSB-first output, each answer bit/digit is s_k = a_k XOR b_k XOR c_k, a 3-input parity, as Lee et al.'s "local rule" makes explicit. This matches the target paper's "3-input parity in the MLP" framing. If the target uses LSB-first binary addition, Lee et al. is the citation for why that format makes the per-position computation local.
- The length-generalisation literature (McLeish, Shen, Kazemnejad, Zhou et al. ×2, Jelassi) concerns positional bookkeeping, i.e. which a_k and b_k attention retrieves. That is the same attention role the target paper describes as "averaging a_k and b_k", so it gives a mechanistic reason why positional fixes matter.

### Gaps
- No paper found that mechanistically analyses carry *representation* (linear vs. neuron) as a function of output order; the length-generalisation papers are mostly behavioural.
- Most of this literature is decimal; I found no mechanistic study specific to *binary* addition transformers in peer-reviewed venues.

## Q3. Is there work comparing learned components to hand-designed logic gates / Boolean circuits inside networks?

### Takeaway
Yes, in three strands. (1) Kruthoff (2024) explicitly maps a 2-layer addition transformer onto a full adder made of XOR/AND half adders. (2) Theory and constructions show networks emulating Boolean circuits (RASP, Tracr, Hänni et al.'s AND-gates in superposition, Liu et al.'s automata shortcuts). (3) The linear representation hypothesis (Park et al.) and parity-learning theory (Barak et al.) frame "direction vs. neuron" and "parity is hard to learn" respectively.

### Cited Findings
- **Kruthoff (2024), Appendix I**: compares the trained 2-layer model to a full adder (two chained half adders, each an XOR for the sum and an AND for the carry). The final MLP corresponds to "the second XOR gate". MLP ablation ≈ replacing that XOR with a wire, and decision-head ablation ≈ a "leaky" XOR. — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07993v2)
- **Weiss, G., Goldberg, Y. & Yahav, E. (2021). "Thinking Like Transformers." ICML 2021. arXiv:2106.06981.** Introduces RASP, a programming language whose primitives map onto attention (select/aggregate) and feed-forward (elementwise) operations. It is used to write programs (histograms, sorting, Dyck) and relate task difficulty to layers and heads. RASP's aggregate is literally a mean over selected positions, which is a precedent for the "attention averages a_k and b_k" description. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06981)
- **Lindner, D., Kramár, J., Farquhar, S., Rahtz, M., McGrath, T. & Mikulik, V. (2023). "Tracr: Compiled Transformers as a Laboratory for Interpretability." NeurIPS 2023 (Spotlight). arXiv:2301.05062.** Compiles RASP programs into real decoder-only transformer weights with known structure, giving ground-truth "hand-designed" circuits to compare with learned ones and to test interpretability methods. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05062)
- **Hänni, K., Mendel, J., Vaintrob, D. & Chan, L. (2024). "Mathematical Models of Computation in Superposition." ICML 2024 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop. arXiv:2408.05451.** Constructs a 1-layer MLP that computes the pairwise AND of m features (even with inputs in superposition) using about Õ(m^(2/3)) neurons. Generalises to arbitrary sparse low-depth Boolean circuits, with error correction for deep emulation. This is a theoretical reference for MLPs implementing Boolean gates with distributed rather than one-neuron-per-gate representations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05451)
- **Liu, B., Ash, J. T., Goel, S., Krishnamurthy, A. & Zhang, C. "Transformers Learn Shortcuts to Automata." arXiv:2210.10749 (v2 May 2023; the arXiv page lists no venue, so venue not confirmed here).** Low-depth transformers can simulate any finite-state automaton via hierarchical "shortcuts" with fewer layers than steps, analysed with Krohn–Rhodes theory and circuit complexity. Shortcuts are learnable but brittle. Carry propagation is a two-state automaton, so this is the relevant theory for how a shallow model handles a carry. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10749)
- **Barak, B., Edelman, B. L., Goel, S., Kakade, S., Malach, E. & Zhang, C. (2022). "Hidden Progress in Deep Learning: SGD Learns Parities Near the Computational Limit." NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2207.08799.** Networks learn k-sparse parities after about n^O(k) iterations, with sharp phase transitions. Progress is driven by a Fourier gap in the gradient and is hidden from loss curves. This is the background for why the parity (XOR) part of addition is the hard component. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08799)
- **Park, K., Choe, Y. J. & Veitch, V. (2024). "The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models." ICML 2024. arXiv:2311.03658.** Formalises "concepts are directions" via counterfactual definitions and links them to probing (input space) and steering (output space). Uses a causal inner product to build probes and steering vectors. This is the standard citation for claiming the carry is a *linear direction* that is causally used (steerable), not merely probe-decodable. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658)
- **Michaud, E. J., Liao, I., Lad, V., Liu, Z., Mudide, A., Loughridge, C., Guo, Z. C., Kheirkhah, T. R., Vukelić, M. & Tegmark, M. (2024). "Opening the AI black box: program synthesis via mechanistic interpretability." arXiv:2402.05110 (preprint).** MIPS turns trained RNNs into finite-state machines via an integer autoencoder, then applies Boolean/integer symbolic regression to recover Python code. It solves 32 of 62 algorithmic tasks. This is an automated way of extracting Boolean/logic descriptions from learned networks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05110)

### Inferences
- The target paper's "linear direction, not gate-like neuron" claim speaks directly to Kruthoff's full-adder/neuron-set framing and Nikankin et al.'s neuron-heuristic view. Hänni et al. show theoretically that Boolean gates in MLPs need not be one-neuron-per-gate, and Park et al. supply the definitional framework for "causally used linear direction".
- "3-input parity from count-threshold units" is the classic construction of parity from threshold gates: parity depends only on the count a+b+c ∈ {0,1,2,3}, so it can be written as an alternating sum of thresholds 1[count≥1] − 1[count≥2] + 1[count≥3]. Attention averaging a_k and b_k delivers exactly the count (up to scale), which an MLP of ReLU threshold units can turn into parity. I found no prior addition-interpretability paper that states this construction explicitly; Kruthoff's XOR mapping is the nearest.
