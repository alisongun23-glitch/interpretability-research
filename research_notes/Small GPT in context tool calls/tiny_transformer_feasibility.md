# Can a ~1-2M-parameter, 4-layer GPT copy numbers from context, write a short trace, and chain trace steps? Evidence from induction heads, scratchpads, small-transformer arithmetic, TinyStories and compositional-generalization studies

Reader context (from the assignment, not a source): target model is a 4-layer, d_model 128, 4-head GPT, 64-token context, trained from scratch on ~5M tokens of TinyStories plus inserted math/story word problems; every number 0-255 is a single token; held-out story perplexity ~14. Planned redesign: the model writes `<calc> 12 + 5 =`, a gate-level CPU writes `17` into the context, and the model must copy `17` into "now he has 17 apples".

Method note: primary sources were read in full text (arXiv PDFs extracted locally; the two Anthropic pages stripped of images). Where a number is my own arithmetic or extrapolation it is labelled "inference".

## Key question 1: Induction heads and copying — how many layers, when in training, how reliable?

### Takeaway
Copying a token from earlier in context via an induction circuit needs at least two attention layers (a previous-token head feeding a second-layer head by K-composition); one-layer models can only do a weaker "copy tokens that are bigram-plausible" trick. In Anthropic's small models (1-6 layers, d_model 768, 8192 context) induction heads appeared abruptly in a window around 2.5-5 billion training tokens; there is no published number for when they appear in a ~1M-parameter, 5M-token run. Copying is learned reliably on explicit copy tasks by small from-scratch transformers, but "read the right value among distractors" tasks show a persistent long tail of sporadic errors that varies across seeds.

### Cited Findings
- Zero-layer attention-only transformers model bigram statistics; one-layer models are "an ensemble of bigram and 'skip-trigram' (sequences of the form 'A… B C') models"; two-layer models use head composition to build induction heads, which "only develop in models with at least two attention layers" — [Elhage et al. 2021, A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- One-layer copying is crude: "most attention heads in one layer models dedicate an enormous fraction of their capacity to copying," but "tokens are copied, but only to places where bigram-ish statistics make them seem plausible"; in their 12-head one-layer model "10 out of 12 heads are significantly copying" (positive OV eigenvalues) — [Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- One-layer models have factored "skip-trigram bugs": a head that raises "keep… in mind" and "keep… at bay" must also raise "keep… in bay" and "keep… at mind" — [Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- Two-layer mechanism: "the only type of composition is K-composition between a single first layer head and some of the second layer heads"; that first-layer head "primarily attends to the previous token"; "most attention heads are not involved in any substantive composition" (they remain skip-trigram heads) — [Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- Induction-head function: "search over the context for previous examples of the present token. If they don't find it, they attend to the first token … and do nothing. But if they do find it, they then look at the next token and copy it." Contrast: one-layer copying head `[b] … [a] → [b]` vs two-layer induction head `[a][b] … [a] → [b]`; the latter is "less vulnerable to distributional shift" and "can operate on repeated sequences of completely random tokens" — [Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- Elhage et al.'s toy models: attention-only, vocabulary ~50,000, variants with 12 heads × d_head 64 and 32 heads × d_head 128 — [Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- Olsson et al. define induction heads operationally as heads that "increase the likelihood of [B] given [A][B]...[A] when shown a repeated sequence of completely random tokens", scored by a prefix-matching score and a copying score; "induction heads don't occur in 1 layer models, because they require a composition of attention heads in different layers" — [Olsson et al. 2022, In-context Learning and Induction Heads](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)
- Timing: in-context learning (loss at token 500 minus loss at token 50) "develops abruptly in a narrow window early in training (roughly 2.5 to 5 billion tokens) and then is constant"; "Before this window there is less than 0.15 nats of in-context learning, after it there is roughly 0.4 nats … constant across many different model sizes (except for the one layer model where not much in-context learning ever forms)"; induction heads "form abruptly during exactly the same window"; the window is visible as a bump, "the only place in training where the loss is not convex" — [Olsson et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)
- The models behind those numbers: "small models are 1- through 6-layer Transformers … with MLPs and without MLPs … context window of 8192 tokens, a 2^16 token vocabulary, a residual stream dimension d_model=768, and 12 attention heads per layer"; 34 transformers and >50,000 head ablations in total — [Olsson et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)
- Ablation: "almost all the in-context learning in small attention-only models appears to come from these induction heads … ablating most other attention heads appears to increase in-context learning"; the authors call the case "strong" only for small attention-only models and "suggestive" for models with MLPs — [Olsson et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)
- Architecture lever: a "smeared key" (trainable α mixing the current and previous token's key, k_j = σ(α)k_j + (1-σ(α))k_{j-1}) shifts "whether induction heads can form (and when)", and in-context learning shifts "in a precisely matching way" (Argument 2) — [Olsson et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)
- Independent replication: Redwood Research "found induction heads reliably appearing in two-layer attention-only transformers" (comment thread on the paper) — [Olsson et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)
- Copy as a trained task, small from-scratch decoder-only models with learned positional embeddings and loss on all tokens: "copy with unique tokens" length-generalizes to 96% exact match while "copy with repeat tokens" gets 0%; the unique-token case "can be solved by an induction head", and the authors note induction heads "are reliably learned even by simple Transformers" — [Zhou et al. 2023, What Algorithms Can Transformers Learn?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028)
- Copy mechanism with distractors: transformers (~160M params) copy via hashed n-gram lookup; accuracy "starts dropping when the inputs contains duplicate sequences of 5 or more tokens", matching "a 5-gram model" — [Jelassi et al. 2024, Repeat After Me](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)
- Reading a value back from context is glitchy: on flip-flop languages (write/read/ignore a single bit), "Transformers exhibit a long, irregular tail of errors" on both sparse and dense sequences, and error "varies significantly between random seeds … and even between iterates within the same training run"; a 1-layer LSTM (hidden 128, 133K params) "achieves 100% accuracy, on 100 out of 100 runs" with 20× fewer samples; baseline transformer 19M params (6 layers, d 512, 8 heads); sweep L∈{2,4,6,8}, H∈{2,4,8,16}; 16M training sequences; attention sharpening cuts errors "by an order of magnitude" but nothing short of training on o.o.d. data "could fully eliminate attention glitches"; proposed mechanisms include softmax attention being "diluted" with length and brittle tie-breaking — [Liu et al. 2023, Exposing Attention Glitches with Flip-Flop Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00946)
- In 1-layer TinyStories models (16 heads) there is "a clear separation between heads with attention pattern based mainly on the distance between tokens, and heads whose attention pattern has a stronger dependence on the semantic meaning"; several heads attend at fixed relative distances — [Eldan & Li 2023, TinyStories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)

### Inferences
- A 4-layer model clears the only hard architectural bar (≥2 layers). The circuit the target needs — attend from "has" back to the token that follows the most recent "=" (or result marker) and copy it — is an induction-style circuit (previous-token/"tag the slot after =" head in an early layer, copy head in a later layer), so the same two-layer K-composition applies. Inference.
- The 2.5-5B-token window cannot be transferred to a 5M-token run: those models have ~15× the width, 128× the context and a 65k vocabulary, and their induction pressure came from 8192-token documents. With a 64-token context the story text provides little induction pressure (Olsson's metric compares token 500 vs token 50); the inserted calc/copy snippets must supply the pressure directly, as a supervised copy task in Zhou et al.'s sense. Inference.
- The flip-flop result is the closest analogue to "copy 17 when 12 and 5 are nearby" (dense sequences with several candidate values): expect a nonzero tail of wrong-number copies even at 100% in-distribution accuracy, and large seed-to-seed variance; measure across seeds and report an error rate rather than a pass/fail. Inference from Liu et al.
- Jelassi et al. suggest a second failure mode: if the same number appears twice (e.g. "12 apples … 12 + 5 = 17"), n-gram-hash copying can key on the wrong occurrence; a unique cue token immediately before the result (e.g. a dedicated `=`/result token used once per call) reduces this. Inference.
- Olsson's smeared-key trick is a cheap architectural change that makes previous-token information available without spending a head/layer; it could make copy circuits form earlier in a tiny model. Inference.

### Gaps
- No source gives the training-token count or step at which induction heads form in a ≤2M-parameter model trained on a few million tokens; Olsson's appendix sentence on small-model training length was truncated in the stripped page.
- No source quantifies the accuracy of copying a specific number 5-20 tokens back in a 1-2M model; the flip-flop numbers are for 19M-parameter (and larger) models, and Jelassi's for ~160M.
- Olsson's numeric thresholds for the prefix-matching/copying scores used to label a head "induction" were not recovered from the page text.

## Key question 2: Scratchpads (Nye et al. 2021) — what changed, at what sizes, and was anything masked?

### Takeaway
Fine-tuning pretrained decoder-only models (2M-1B params) on 100k addition examples (5k steps, batch 32) with the long-addition steps written into the target made addition solvable "beyond a critical model size", whereas direct-answer models failed even at 1B; the paper reports no loss masking, and an ablation shows the gain is not just from extra supervision. At 10M-parameter scale, Lee et al. found a scratchpad cut the data needed for 3-digit addition to ~1,000 examples, versus ~2,500 for reversed output and a plateau at ~85% for plain format even with 10,000.

### Cited Findings
- Models: "pre-trained dense decoder-only Transformer language models, ranging in size from 2 million to 137 billion parameters … pre-trained on web documents and dialog data" — [Nye et al. 2021, Show Your Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- Addition setup: inputs 1-8 digits, OOD tests at 9 and 10 digits; "fine-tuned on 100k examples for 5k steps with batch size 32"; 10k in-distribution and 1k OOD test examples; sizes "ranging from 2M to 1B parameters"; digits space-separated so "each digit is mapped to a separate token" — [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- Result: "beyond a critical model size, models are able to solve the addition task using the scratchpad, while models trained without a scratchpad fail to do so even at the largest tested scale"; OOD (9-10 digits): no-scratchpad models "completely fail", scratchpad models "show consistent improvement as a function of model size" — [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- Supervision: "We implement the scratchpad by including the intermediate steps of the long addition algorithm in the target"; the paper uses "standard" training on the encoded steps. I found no statement of loss masking anywhere in the paper — [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- Ablation on the extra signal: a model first fine-tuned on the scratchpad and then fine-tuned for direct execution shows "no significant benefits … the extra training-time information seen by the scratchpad model does not seem solely responsible for the scratchpad model's improved performance" — [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- Larger-model results for context: polynomial evaluation used a 137B model few-shot (4 exemplars) and an 8B model fine-tuned 2000 steps; code execution used ~137B; few-shot tracing had a format failure (model "would not assign the variable name" expected), requiring a changed scoring rule — [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- From-scratch 10.6M model: NanoGPT "six self-attention layers, six heads, and an embedding dimension of 384, resulting in approximately 10.6 million parameters", character-level tokens, absolute positions, "standard autoregressive next-token prediction loss" — [Lee et al. 2023, Teaching Arithmetic to Small Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Data efficiency for 3-digit addition: plain format "plateaus at slightly over 85% even with 10,000 samples"; reversed output shows "a notable phase transition between 1000 and 4000 samples" (figure caption: around 2,500); "Simplified Scratchpad data achieves 100% accuracy with only 2000 samples"; "Detailed Scratchpad … perfect addition with just 1000 samples" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Scratchpad token cost: 3,000 detailed-scratchpad examples were 813,510 characters/tokens (≈271 per example) versus 10,000 plain examples at 120,027 (≈12 per example) — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Noisy scratchpads: training with incorrect intermediate values slows learning but "with enough samples, the model ultimately achieves full accuracy" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Scaling the task: for k+1-digit addition, reverse and scratchpad formats need a "relatively consistent" number of extra samples (1,000-5,000) while plain format "requires an increasing number of samples" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)

### Inferences
- The "critical model size" in Nye et al. is read off Figure 3 (x-axis 10^7-10^9 params); the smallest model (2M) is at the bottom of that curve, so Nye et al. provide no positive evidence that a 2M model benefits from a scratchpad when fine-tuned from a pretrained checkpoint. Lee et al. are the relevant positive evidence at 10M, from scratch. Inference.
- The target's trace (`<calc> 12 + 5 = 17`) is ~6 tokens with single-token numbers, far shorter than Lee's 271-token detailed scratchpad, so it fits the 64-token window; the relevant Lee regime is the "simplified scratchpad" (short, ~2,000 examples). Inference.
- Both scratchpad papers trained with the intermediate steps in the loss; neither masked. The target design differs: the "17" is produced by a tool, not by the model, so the Nye/Lee evidence says nothing about whether to mask it (see Q3). Inference.

### Gaps
- Exact crossover model size in Nye et al. Figure 3 could not be read from the extracted text.
- Nye et al. do not report training-token counts or whether the 2M model ever exceeded chance with the scratchpad.
- Lee et al.'s training iteration counts/batch sizes for the from-scratch runs were not recovered from the text (only axis ranges 0-5,000/0-20,000 iterations are visible); the only explicit schedule found ("trained for 4 epochs") refers to a fine-tuning experiment.

## Key question 3: Small transformers and arithmetic — how much data, and memorising vs computing? Will the model do the sum itself if the tool's answer is in its loss?

### Takeaway
Small from-scratch transformers learn addition from thousands, not millions, of examples when the format is friendly (reverse/scratchpad), and ablation tests show they learn a mapping that generalises to unseen operands and even unseen digits-in-position — so it is not pure memorisation, but neither is it a length-general algorithm. With single-token numbers 0-255 the task is a 256×256 lookup; a one-layer d_model 128 model already learns a 113×113 modular-addition table from ~3.8k examples. So if the CPU's answer token carries loss, a 1-2M model has both the capacity and the incentive to learn to predict the sum itself.

### Cited Findings
- Learning map vs matrix completion: learning n-digit addition "from random samples is equivalent to completing a low-rank matrix" (rank 2); for 2-digit addition (n=100) "both NanoGPT and LRMC exhibit a similar phase transition at approximately 1500 samples" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Not pure memorisation: excluding 100/200/500 of the 3-digit operands from training showed "no significant drop in accuracy and in some cases, the performance even improves"; reverse-format models "remain consistently at 100% accuracy"; excluding digit 5 at a fixed position still gives ">66% accuracy on every test" and overall ">85%" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Not a general algorithm: trained on 1- and 3-digit operands only (7,655 samples), "the NanoGPT model is incapable of performing 2-digit and 4-digit additions"; trained on 1-7 digits it "is unable to generalize" to 8 digits; 10-digit binary does not generalise to 11; the authors summarise that models learn "a mapping function constrained to trained digit lengths. While this surpasses mere memorization…" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Mixed text + arithmetic at 10M scale: Shakespeare (1,115,394 chars) with 10,000 plain and 3,000 detailed-scratchpad addition examples inserted "between dialogue chunks", char vocab 80; few-shot prompting lets "plain addition … perform almost perfectly with 40,000 train samples", and accuracy "remains high on plain addition even with the inclusion of a text prompt, given a substantial number of addition examples" — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Prompt sensitivity when five arithmetic tasks are trained jointly: zero-shot addition 84.06%, few-shot with any arithmetic exemplar ~96-97%, with a text exemplar 88.71% ("text prompting shows performance similar to the zero-shot setting") — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Fine-tuning pretrained text models with reversed/non-standard arithmetic formats "can interfere with the model's" existing abilities — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Modular addition in a d_model 128 one-layer model: P=113, "one-layer ReLU transformer, token embeddings with d = 128, learned positional embeddings, 4 attention heads of dimension 32, and n = 512 hidden units in the MLP"; trained on "30% of the 113·113 pairs" with full-batch AdamW, lr 0.001, weight decay 1, 40,000 epochs; test accuracy jumps "after around 10,000 epochs"; phases: memorisation (epochs 0-1.4k), circuit formation (1.4k-9.4k, "the circuit is formed well before grokking occurs"), cleanup (9.4k-14k); the model implements a Fourier/trig algorithm; "our networks do not grok on the modular arithmetic task without weight decay or some other form" of regularisation — [Nanda et al. 2023, Progress Measures for Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)
- Even large LLMs do not learn a clean algorithm: in Llama3-8B/70B, Pythia-6.9B and GPT-J (with numbers 0-1000 as single tokens), arithmetic is answered by "a 'bag of heuristics'" in mid/late MLP neurons (operand-range, modular-pattern, result-pattern neurons); "only 200 neurons (roughly 1.5%) per layer" suffice; the authors conclude models "perform arithmetic using neither robust algorithms nor memorization"; in Pythia checkpoints from 23K to 143K steps the heuristics present at the end "account for an average of 79% of the total heuristics' contribution to accuracy at each checkpoint", i.e. heuristics are the mechanism from early on — [Nikankin et al. 2024, Arithmetic Without Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21272)
- Length generalisation is achievable but fragile: a 25M-parameter model (6 blocks, 512 hidden, FF 2048), 30M examples of lengths 1-40, 50,000 steps, weight decay 0.1, with FIRE + randomised positions + reversed format + index hints reaches >98% on 100-digit addition (2.5× extrapolation) in the best of 10 trials, but generalisation "is fragile, significantly swayed by" weight initialisation and data order; "without index hints, NoPE and FIRE demonstrate poor in-distribution generalization for 40-digit additions" — [Zhou et al. 2024, Transformers Can Achieve Length Generalization But Not Robustly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09371)
- With Abacus (digit-position) embeddings, depth-16 decoder models trained on ≤20-digit operands reach "up to 99% accuracy on 100 digit addition", using 20M training samples; some configurations swing from "near perfect on 100 digit addition to 0% accuracy at 90 digit addition" — [McLeish et al. 2024, Transformers Can Do Arithmetic with the Right Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17399)
- Tool use in pretrained small models: with Toolformer-style self-supervised API insertion (up to 25k examples per API), "the ability to leverage the provided tools only emerges at around 775M parameters: smaller models [GPT-2 124M, 355M] achieve similar performance both with and without tools", except for the easy-to-use Wikipedia search — [Schick et al. 2023, Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)
- From-scratch tiny model wired to a calculator: a "scaled LLaMA with a dimension of 128, 4 transformer heads, 2 key-value heads, and 4 transformer layers" (LLaMA-3 tokenizer, 128,256 vocab), AdamW, batch 32, lr 1e-2, trained on 45,151 natural-language one-step problems ("Add 3 and 4") with a differentiable mod-128 lookup-table calculator attached to the final layer, "Supervision is given only on answers"; "By 62 epochs, the model can use the calculator with 99.2% accuracy on the test set, and by 132 epochs the accuracy is 100%" — [Saldyt & Kambhampati 2024/2025, Augmenting Language Models with Composable Differentiable Libraries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04899)

### Inferences
- Capacity: the target's addition over single-token numbers is a 256×256 table (≈33k pairs with sums ≤255). Nanda's 1-layer, d_model 128 model learned a 113×113 table from 30% coverage (≈3,830 examples; my arithmetic) with a generalising Fourier circuit, and Lee's 10.6M model learned 3-digit addition from ~1-4k examples. A 4-layer d_model 128 model therefore has ample capacity to learn the 0-255 table from a few thousand (a,b,sum) triples. Inference.
- Incentive: if the CPU's `17` carries next-token loss, every calc snippet is a labelled (a,b)→sum example, and gradient will push the model toward predicting the sum from the two operands in context — the model gains no benefit from "waiting for the tool" because it is scored on producing `17` itself. Expect partial internal addition to emerge (likely heuristic/range-based rather than exact, per Nikankin), which then competes with the copy circuit downstream and removes pressure to emit `<calc>` at all. The natural fix is to set loss weight 0 on the CPU-written span only: keep loss on the model's own `<calc> 12 + 5 =` and on the later "has 17" (the copy target must keep loss, otherwise the copy is never trained). Inference; no source tests this at tiny scale.
- Note that Nanda-style grokking needed strong weight decay (λ=1) and ~10k full-batch epochs; in a mixed-corpus run with ordinary weight decay the model is more likely to settle on Nikankin-style heuristics than on a clean circuit. Either way a model that "sort of" adds will produce confident wrong numbers that disagree with the CPU. Inference.
- Toolformer's 775M threshold is not directly comparable (self-supervised insertion into web text, pretrained models, loss-filtered calls), but it is a warning that small models do not spontaneously learn to exploit tool outputs; the target relies on dense direct supervision of the tool-call and copy steps, which Saldyt's 4-layer/d128 result suggests is learnable (parsing operands into a calculator at 99.2% from 45k examples × 62 epochs). Inference.
- Lee's Shakespeare-mix experiment is the closest precedent for "math snippets inside story text" and shows a char-level 10M model can keep arithmetic working inside text, but with a 1.1M-token corpus it needed 10k-40k addition examples (≈0.1-0.5M tokens of arithmetic); the target's ~5M-token budget can afford a comparable number of 20-40-token word-problem snippets. Inference.

### Gaps
- No study trains a ≤2M model on single-token numbers 0-255; all small-model arithmetic work uses digit/character tokens, so the exact sample count for the 0-255 table is extrapolated.
- No source directly tests "tool answer in loss vs masked" for a from-scratch tiny model; Toolformer's masking policy was not recovered from the text.
- Lee et al.'s and Nanda's results do not report wall-clock or token budgets comparable to "5M tokens".

## Key question 4: TinyStories — what 1-10M-parameter models can and cannot do

### Takeaway
At 1-35M parameters (1-8 layers, GPT-Neo architecture, 512-token context, 10k-token vocabulary, ≤30 GPU-hours), TinyStories models produce grammatical, mostly consistent stories; grammar is cheap, consistency appears at hidden size 128, factual recall needs more width, and instruction-following needs ≥2 layers. The paper reports nothing on numbers or arithmetic, and no primary source trains a TinyStories-scale model on inserted structured snippets.

### Cited Findings
- Setup: "GPT-Neo architecture with window size 256 and context length 512 … GPT-Neo tokenizer but only keep the top 10K most common tokens"; models "between roughly 1M and 35M parameters, and whose number of layers range between 1 and 8"; "trained on a single V100 GPU within at most 30 hours"; released as TinyStories-1M/3M/9M/28M/33M/1Layer/2Layer — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Depth vs width: "shallower models perform better in terms of grammar compared to content consistency"; "the ability to generate a completion that is consistent with the beginning of the story emerges when the hidden size of the model increases from 64 to 128"; "models that have only 1 layer seem to struggle quite substantially with following instructions … and 2 layers seem to be sufficient for a certain extent of instruction-following"; instruction-following "depends more heavily on the number of layers", plot coherence on hidden dimension — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Smallest models: "the model with 1M parameters and 8 layers fails to answer any factual prompt correctly, and often generates sentences that do not make sense or do not follow the grammar"; "the model that has only 1 layer does not get any consistency prompt right, but does get some facts right, whereas the model with embedding dimension 64 does not get any fact right, but manages to maintain consistency several times"; GPT-4 consistency scores in the examples: 1M/8L 2/10, 2.5M/8L 3/10, 8.3M/8L 5/10, 28M/8L 9/10, 21M/1L 7/10, 33M/2L 4/10 — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Evaluation is GPT-4 grading of grammar/creativity/consistency/plot (50 instruction sets for the Instruct variant); memorisation checked with Rouge k-gram overlap against the training set, concluding the models "are not doing exact memorization or simple template matching" — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Architecture sweep: layers 2/4/8/12 and hidden 64-2048, plus a study of the number of heads — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Numbers/arithmetic: the paper mentions arithmetic only as an emergent ability of large LLMs; no arithmetic or counting evaluation of TinyStories models is reported — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Anecdotal (model card, not peer-reviewed): a 115M-parameter from-scratch TinyStories model "cannot do arithmetic"; the author notes nothing in TinyStories teaches addition and number words are narrative texture — [chrishayuk/v11-tinystories-115m-base model card](https://huggingface.co/chrishayuk/v11-tinystories-115m-base)
- Anecdotal (model/dataset card): "TinyMathStories" (130M params, 1024 context) embeds grade-school arithmetic inside stories and is described as handling only basic math, not multi-step word problems — [AlgoDriveAI/tinymathstories-130m](https://huggingface.co/AlgoDriveAI/tinymathstories-130m/blob/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- The target (4 layers, d_model 128) sits exactly at the thresholds Eldan & Li identify (hidden 128 for consistency, ≥2 layers for instruction following), which is consistent with its ~14 held-out perplexity being "coherent but shallow". Context tracking over the whole 64-token window should be feasible; factual breadth will be weak. Inference.
- Because TinyStories text itself carries no arithmetic signal, all number behaviour must come from the inserted snippets; the base corpus neither helps nor (per Lee's mixing result) prevents learning them. Inference.

### Gaps
- Dataset size (number of stories/tokens) and per-model training-token counts were not recovered from the paper text.
- No peer-reviewed study of a ≤10M model trained on TinyStories with inserted structured/tool snippets was found; the two Hugging Face cards above are hobby projects at 115-130M parameters.

## Key question 5: Compositional / multi-step generalisation in small transformers

### Takeaway
Small-to-mid transformers trained from scratch on templated data do learn multi-step rules, but (i) implicit two-hop composition only generalises in-distribution and only after grokking far beyond the point of fitting the training data, (ii) it never generalises to facts seen only atomically, because the first and second hop are stored in different layers, and (iii) explicit verbalised steps with sufficient depth do generalise to longer problem chains at ~100M scale. Length generalisation of copy/arithmetic depends on whether an induction-head-style program exists for the format.

### Cited Findings
- Composition (two-hop) grokking: GPT-2-style model "8 layers, 768 hidden dimensions and 12 attention heads", AdamW lr 1e-4, batch 512, weight decay 0.1; with 2,000 entities and inferred/atomic ratio φ=7.2 the ID test accuracy is "merely 9.2%" when training accuracy saturates, then "approaches almost perfect accuracy after extended optimization lasting around 50 times the steps taken to fit the training data"; training fits "within 25K steps"; OOD composition is "never observed" even at 2 million steps — [Wang et al. 2024, Grokked Transformers are Implicit Reasoners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)
- What controls speed: "the speed of improvement in generalization correlates with the ratio between inferred and atomic facts in training, and depends little on the absolute size of the training data"; "larger models converge in fewer optimization steps" (24L/1024 and 36L/1280 tested) without qualitative change — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)
- Mechanism and why OOD fails: lower layers (0-5) retrieve the first-hop fact and store the bridge entity; upper layers (5-8) retrieve the second hop; "the OOD atomic facts are simply not stored in the upper layers when queried during the second hop"; sharing parameters between the first 4 and last 4 layers (Universal-Transformer-like) improves OOD composition; the authors relate this to transformers reducing "compositional reasoning to linearized pattern matching" — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)
- Comparison task generalises OOD via a "parallel circuit" where both facts are retrieved in lower layers and compared in upper layers — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)
- Weight decay matters: the transition from memorising to generalising circuit is driven by "implicit bias of the optimization and explicit regularization such as weight decay", confirmed by varying weight decay — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)
- Length generalisation as a proxy for "algorithm vs template": tasks with short RASP-L programs (count, mode, unique copy, sort, reformatted addition) length-generalise; standard addition and repeat-copy get 0% beyond training length; a scratchpad "unroll[s] a loop, turning a next-token problem that requires n sequential steps" into local steps — [Zhou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028)
- Explicit multi-step with depth (larger scale): a GPT-2-like model (RoPE) pretrained from scratch on synthetic grade-school problems with step-by-step solutions "achieves 99% accuracy" in-distribution and generalises to more operations than seen in training; the model "preprocesses the full set of necessary parameters before it starts any generation"; "The depth of the language model is crucial for its reasoning ability"; the dataset has ">90 trillion solution templates", "much larger than the size of GPT2-small (100M)" — [Ye et al. 2024, Physics of Language Models Part 2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20311)

### Inferences
- The target's "chain" is explicit and verbalised (`<calc> 12 + 5 = 17` … `<calc> 17 - 3 = 14`), so it is closer to Zhou's scratchpad-unrolling and Ye's step-by-step regime than to Wang's implicit two-hop; each step is a short copy-plus-template operation, which is the case small models handle best. Wang's grokking numbers (50× past fitting, 2M steps) are for implicit composition and should not be read as the cost here. Inference.
- Wang's "facts live in specific layers" finding still matters: with 4 layers there is little room to separate "tag the result slot", "copy", and "write the template" stages; expect the model to learn a surface trigger (e.g. "has" → copy the token after the most recent "=") rather than a general variable-binding mechanism, and expect templates that move the cue words to fail. Inference.
- Ye's depth result (reasoning needs layers) at 100M scale is consistent with TinyStories' "instruction following needs layers"; 4 layers is at the low end for two chained steps plus a copy. Inference.

### Gaps
- No study of explicit multi-step tool traces in a ≤2M model; Ye et al.'s model configurations (exact layer/width list) were not recovered from the text, only that the dataset dwarfs a 100M-parameter model.
- No small-model study measures generalisation to new story templates specifically.

## Key question 6: Small models trained from scratch to emit tool tokens or read a value back from context — methods and failure modes

### Takeaway
No source trains a tiny model to emit a tool token into its own stream and copy the tool's answer back. The closest primary results are: a 4-layer/d128 from-scratch LLaMA that learns to parse operands into an attached differentiable calculator from answer-only supervision (99.2% after 62 epochs on 45k examples); Toolformer, where pretrained models below ~775M did not benefit from calculator calls; and the flip-flop and n-gram-copy studies, which document the two expected failure modes — sporadic wrong reads when several candidate values are in context, and keying on the wrong occurrence when a value repeats.

### Cited Findings
- Tiny from-scratch model + calculator: 4 layers, dimension 128, 4 heads, 2 KV heads; dataset 45,151 natural-language one-step mod-128 problems; "Supervision is given only on answers, via cross-entropy"; 99.2% at 62 epochs, 100% at 132 epochs; the calculator is a differentiable lookup table that receives the model's final-layer one-hot "register … and a selected operation", not a token-level call; the authors note "lookup tables are more learnable than circuits" and that answer-only supervision gives "no guarantee that correct tool use behavior will be learned" — [Saldyt & Kambhampati 2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04899)
- Toolformer's size threshold for pretrained models (124M/355M/775M/1.6B GPT-2, GPT-J 6.7B): tool benefit "only emerges at around 775M parameters"; API calls are kept only when they reduce loss on following tokens; up to 25k examples per API — [Schick et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)
- Reading the last written value with distractors (flip-flop): long tail of read errors, seed- and iterate-dependent; 2-layer runs sometimes "non-converged"; the task is solvable by "a 1-layer Transformer with a single attention head which attends sparsely on the most" recent write, yet learned models do not reliably implement that sparse pattern; attention sharpening and more data help by orders of magnitude but do not eliminate errors — [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00946)
- Copying keyed on repeated n-grams fails when the duplicated span is ≥5 tokens; transformers behave like a 5-gram retriever — [Jelassi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)
- Few-shot scratchpad format drift in large models: models "would not assign the variable name" expected by the trace format, forcing a scoring change — [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)
- In mixed text+arithmetic training, what the model does depends on the prompt context: arithmetic exemplars raise accuracy from 84% to ~97% while a text exemplar gives 88.7% — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)

### Inferences
- Expected failure modes for the target, mapped from the above: (a) emitting `<calc>` where no sum is needed, or omitting it — the Lee prompt-sensitivity result suggests the trigger will be learned as a surface pattern of the surrounding story words, so vary the templates and include negative examples (stories with numbers but no arithmetic); (b) copying 12 or 5 instead of 17 — flip-flop-style glitches, worst when the three numbers sit within a few tokens; (c) copying the wrong 17 when the result value also appears earlier — Jelassi-style collision; (d) if the CPU output carries loss, generating its own (possibly wrong) answer and later copying that. All inference.
- Mitigations with some support: a unique result-marker token immediately before the CPU's answer (makes the induction key unique; Elhage/Olsson mechanism), keeping the copy distance short (attention dilution grows with distance, Liu et al.), masking the CPU span from loss (Q3), weight decay plus long training to prefer the generalising circuit (Nanda, Wang), and reporting error rates over several seeds (Liu et al., Zhou 2024). Inference.
- Saldyt's model is the only datapoint at exactly the target's width/depth: 4 layers × d128 can learn "find the operands in a sentence and route them" from ~2.8M example-presentations (45,151 × 62 epochs; my arithmetic). The target's job (also emitting the call and copying back) is harder but has denser supervision. Inference.

### Gaps
- No source reports a from-scratch model that emits a special tool token and consumes an in-context tool result; failure statistics for "emits tool token at wrong place" do not exist in the literature found.
- A single-author arXiv draft claiming arbitrary-length addition in a tiny transformer ([Patriota 2024, arXiv:2406.00075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00075)) surfaced in search but was not read; treat as unverified.

## Key question 7 (synthesis for the reader): expectations for a 4-layer, d_model 128, 64-context model on ~5M tokens

### Takeaway
Feasible on paper: the copy circuit needs 2 layers (have 4), the trace is a few tokens (fits 64), and 10M-scale models learn arithmetic-in-text from a few thousand examples. The main risks are not capacity but (1) the loss design (an unmasked tool answer teaches the model to add, which it can do with d_model 128), (2) a long tail of wrong-number copies that varies by seed, and (3) surface-trigger learning that breaks on unseen templates.

### Cited Findings
- Induction circuits need ≥2 layers and in small two-layer models consist of one previous-token head plus K-composed second-layer heads — [Elhage et al. 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- A d_model 128, 4-head, 1-layer model learns a 113×113 addition table from 30% of pairs (with weight decay 1, ~10k epochs) — [Nanda et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)
- A 10.6M model learns 3-digit addition from ~1,000 (detailed scratchpad) to ~2,500 (reverse) examples, and keeps the ability when the examples are embedded in 1.1M tokens of text — [Lee et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- A 4-layer, d128 from-scratch LLaMA learns operand parsing/routing to a calculator at 99.2% from 45,151 examples × 62 epochs with answer-only supervision — [Saldyt & Kambhampati 2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04899)
- Hidden size 128 and ≥2 layers are the TinyStories thresholds for consistency and instruction following — [Eldan & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Transformers show a seed-dependent long tail of errors when reading a value back from context, even at 19M parameters — [Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00946)
- Pretrained models under ~775M did not benefit from calculator calls under Toolformer's self-supervised scheme — [Schick et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)

### Inferences
- Data budget: at ~30 tokens per snippet, 2,000-10,000 calc/copy snippets cost 0.06-0.3M of the 5M tokens, i.e. the same order as Lee's mixing ratios; several epochs over the corpus (as in Saldyt's 62 epochs and Lee's 5k-20k iterations) are likely needed. Inference.
- Mask the CPU-written span; keep loss on `<calc> a + b =` and on the later copy target; make the result marker unique per call; keep the copy within ~20 tokens; evaluate with held-out operand pairs, held-out templates, and ≥3 seeds, reporting wrong-number rates separately for "nearby distractor" and "repeated value" cases. Inference.
- Expect in-distribution copy accuracy to reach high-90s% quickly, a residual 0.1-few-% glitch rate that does not vanish with more of the same data (flip-flop), and poor transfer to story templates whose cue words differ from training (Lee prompt sensitivity, Wang layer-specific storage). Inference.

### Gaps
- Every number above is extrapolated from models 5-100× larger or from single-task toy settings; no published experiment matches the exact configuration (≤2M params, 64-token context, single-token 0-255 numerals, in-context tool).
