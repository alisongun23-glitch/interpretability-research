## Q2. What does it claim, how was it tested, and what are the key quantitative results?

### Takeaway
The central claim is that **superposition, in the sense of a learning principle that packs unrelated things into a neuron because they rarely interfere, was the wrong explanation for polysemanticity**. The essay says apparent polysemanticity mostly comes from bad tools, bad explanations, bad architectures and "recursive generalization", so a network's own neurons and circuits can be studied directly. Most of the evidence is qualitative and exploratory: re-reading canonical examples, watching neurons form during training, layer-squeezing retraining, SAE comparisons, ablations, and attribution graphs built from neurons only. The headline Qwen3-32B figures (~90% / ~7% / ~2–4%) are explicitly preliminary and depend on thresholds, and their audit method is only partly described.

### Cited Findings
**Core thesis (verbatim)**
- "One main result is that superposition was the wrong explanation for InceptionV1. Architectural problems made later layers overflow. Weak interpretability tools amplified these problems, making the late layers seem incoherent." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- "The canonical vision examples of polysemanticity and superposition were errors too." On the 2019 car→dogs circuit: "It was the only one we showed, and the only one I ever saw… But nothing reads that combination… it was ordinary generalization and composition. To my knowledge, no one has published a second circuit with the right shape." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- The four-step argument, quoted: (1) "Networks use one learning principle, not two"; (2) "With modern models and tools, that science goes much better than most people expect"; (3) "Understanding compounds, so it gets easier as the map fills in"; (4) "Highly speculative: this might be most of what we need, and interpretability might be far more tractable, possibly in the near term, than we've assumed." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Definition of "clean": "a network's parts are built to be read, and built upon, by its downstream readers… That points to a goal for interpretability: become another reader." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**Headline language-model numbers and how hedged they are**
- "In preliminary audits of Qwen3-32B, we estimate that roughly 90% of neuron poles have one main job, often with small related offshoots; another ~7% have two related jobs; and only ~2-4% are not yet understood. Dense and Mixture of Experts models seem roughly similar. We found similar results in models up to trillions of parameters, like Kimi K3, which the companion paper will cover in more detail." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Footnote 1 caveats: the share "varies depending on how you set the threshold for counting weak activations, depth, density of sub-circuits, and what you view as 'one thing'." Also: "considering strong activations, it's closer to 97% non-arbitrary mixtures, but these are relatively small samples." And: "distilling everything into one 'cleanliness' score should be taken as a broad pointer to the regime a neural network is in, rather than something precise." The author adds that a number was included because "early reviewers felt unanchored without even a rough number." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- The essay refers to "1000 neuron papers, detailed investigations into individual neurons, chosen randomly" (an interactive figure). It says "What we see is about 90% of neurons having basically one job… A lot of the remaining ones have two or three. I've never seen a neuron with something like ten unrelated things it does. There's a lot more detail in the other paper." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Baselines it contrasts against: Anthropic's Towards Monosemanticity (2023) "gave the median neuron a 0 on its rubric out of a possible 14". OpenAI's 2023 automated-interpretability project "found about 1.7% of neurons interpretable by our most generous measure." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**Explanation falsification test (appendix, "Explanations That Could Lose")**
- "For 200 random neurons from our gallery, an agent read only each neuron's explanation and wrote five new examples that should drive it and five near misses that shouldn't, before we measured any of them." The figure shows Qwen3-32B neurons, and a "strong response is 30% or more" of the neuron's strongest corpus example — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Results: "Among the 183 whose explanations describe their positive side, the new examples fired their neurons strongly 76% of the time; the near misses, 11%. The explanation won for 164, tied for 15 and lost for 4." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Distant-concept (superposition-style) control: the agent "invent[ed] the most distant concept it could… and wr[o]te five texts for it… Those texts fired their neurons strongly about 1% of the time, and 179 of the 183 neurons never fired on them at all." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Blind-maximum test: "One agent investigates a neuron and writes a short explanation; a second sees only that explanation and writes an example meant to activate it maximally… it can activate the neuron more strongly than any example we found in 10 billion tokens." No aggregate statistic is given in the essay — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Attribution-graph audits: "We had agents review 100,000 attribution graphs, and I inspected thousands… I haven't found a substantial group of neurons whose joint activity remained inexplicable… I regard this as qualitative evidence—I don't trust our current tools enough to turn it into a cleanliness score." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**GPT-2 XL revisited ("Slouching Towards Monosemanticity")**
- In the original OpenAI project, "fewer than 2% of its neurons came out clean." Revisiting it: "We went back and made 500 neuron papers with almost no iteration. Now about 70% look like roughly one thing… The amount of pristine ones are still low, about 5%. What's left are neurons that do a few broad things, up to about six, mostly two. The scoring isn't identical to the original project." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**Vision experiments (the most controlled evidence)**
- Models: "three ConvNeXt models spanning roughly 300× in size: a small one with 92k parameters, a medium one with 7.06M (the same size as InceptionV1), and a large one with 28.6M," plus InceptionV1 and DINOv2 — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- InceptionV1 last-layer legibility estimates: "I put it around 5% with tools of that era, others at ~0%. With better interpretability tools (fixed feature visualization, methods for visualizing attribution etc) it's more like 50% on the same network… Retraining the same architecture with more reasonable modern regularization it's more like 70%. In the ConvNeXt of the same size, it's about 99%." The intro gives "99-100%" for the same-size ConvNeXt's last layer "with… no change to model size or training data." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Layer squeeze, InceptionV1 mixed3b, 480→10 channels (48×), retrained from scratch with no bypass. It reached "58.35% top‑1 and 81.14% top‑5" on 50,000 ImageNet validation images. "The neurons stay interpretable and generalized, but coarser: one 'curves & loops' neuron instead of ten curve detectors, two circle neurons instead of several circle types." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md); [Essay HTML figure details](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)
- SAE on the squeezed layer: "a TopK SAE with 640 features, 2 active at each position… trained on the squeezed layer at 25.6 million positions from 200,000 ImageNet training images. It explains 82% of the variance of held-out positions, and with it spliced in, top‑1 on 10,000 validation images falls from 58.8% to 43.0%." The per-detector matches between the original ten curve detectors and their closest SAE features have shape correlations of 0.38–0.79 and photo correlations of 0.08–0.26 (table). "The essay's SAE of the original layer matches the same detectors with shape correlations of 0.96–1.00." On the squeezed layer, "even the best nonlinear readout of the ten neurons at one position tracks each detector with a correlation of only 0.29–0.48." Other autoencoder settings (1–4 active, 160 or 640 features, a second seed, no auxiliary loss) gave "median shape match 0.47–0.63, photo match at most 0.27." — [Essay HTML figure details](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)
- Last-layer squeeze, medium ConvNeXt: "final layer cut from 1,024 neurons to 13 and trained from scratch". The result is "the same pattern of it broadening." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Residual-stream squeeze, medium ConvNeXt, with a stream "four and eight times narrower, down to just 46 dimensions in the last block". "Accuracy fell from 72% to 60% and 39%," but the map is "quite similar, just a bit messier. Nothing looks cleverly interleaved." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- What SAEs add: "On the last layer, 88% of its activity is near-copies of single neurons, and the rest is faint: removing every dictionary direction that matches no neuron leaves accuracy unchanged." Rotation search lands "back on the neurons, in every block." "SAEs did help with 'what's in this image?' They helped most in the smallest model… At medium size I slightly preferred them; at large, I think I prefer the neurons." An SAE trained on curve activations alone "uses 84k of the 100k items in its dictionary," which the author reads as compositions inflating the apparent number of building blocks — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Weak-activation ablation, medium ConvNeXt, all 18 blocks, baseline 72%. "Removing the weakest 75%: accuracy 72% → 71% · 1 in 10 answers change". "Removing the strongest 7%: accuracy 72% → 4% · 96 in 100 answers change." The two removals are matched for the same total activation, and the changed answers are mostly near-ties (e.g., Eskimo dog → Siberian husky) — [Essay HTML figure](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)
- Memorization (7.06M model taught "umbrella photo = bee"): "In nine training steps, the top guess went from umbrella to maypole to bee. The umbrella neurons faded and existing bee neurons came on, nearly all of it in the last three blocks: swap just those into the original model, and it says 'bee.'" The figure shows the photo's "umbrella" probability going 44.2% → 0.03% and its "bee" probability going 0.07% → 52.8% — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md); [Essay HTML figure](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)
- Adversarial examples: a ConvNeXt triceratops skull is attacked into reading as a jaguar. "Six skull-related neurons fall quiet, while inputs used by a real jaguar switch on at roughly a quarter of their usual strength." In an InceptionV1 robust attack, "Nine of ten neurons selected for their responses to real jaguars become much more active." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Edge motifs: "Factoring 25,000 ImageNet attribution graphs gave us connected circuits of ordinary neurons… These motifs also predicted where ablation would matter much better than comparable groupings of activations." The figure compares 96 edge motifs with 96 activation factors — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Scale and universality of the pose neuron: the same dogs-and-vehicles-by-facing-direction neuron "shows up in DINOv2 and in our large and medium ConvNeXts"; "Our smallest model only has a rough version." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Early generalization: one photo each of ten classes: "It labels about one in five new photos correctly, twice chance… what carries over is mostly color, not shape." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

## Q3. What mechanisms or concepts does it introduce?

### Takeaway
The essay introduces or names several concepts:
- **recursive generalization**, a fractal "branching" growth of neurons that the author says explains most residual polysemanticity, with "conjoined features" from Multimodal Neurons as its precursor
- **neurons as verbs/gates**, meaning control flow with meaningful negative poles
- a **SwiGLU double-negative "echo" regime**
- an **unread saliency dimension** in ConvNet residual streams
- **circuit ecology**, which covers circuit racing, gradient starvation and "semantic gravity"
- **exploratory vs. enumerative interpretability** and **tethered abstraction**
- a set of language-model motifs: the summary motif, "say or just think", "don't say a dot", and generalized pose neurons in vision
- **edge motifs**, also called edge featurizers
- evaluation methods: blind maximum, near misses and distant-concept controls

### Cited Findings
- **Recursive generalization**: "a neuron generalizing along more than one axis, often first along one axis, then sprouting outwards in ways that are easy to follow locally, but stop it from being 'monosemantic' in the end." Example: an "iy" neuron grows to "diy," then "do it yourself," "which doesn't contain 'iy' at all." Training-time evidence: an InceptionV1 neuron starts on rapeseed fields and spreads "into dogs and then broccoli, until the fields are nearly gone", while the matched ConvNeXt neuron "lets them go and stays on the fields." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Precursor: "We first hinted at this in Multimodal Neurons, calling them 'conjoined features.'" — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Superposition, as the essay defines it**: "unrelated things are placed together because they don't interfere… These have opposite predictions: generalization should be local, superposition should be non-local." The essay separates this from "more features than dimensions" (from Toy Models), "which even a fully monosemantic network would satisfy, because its neurons compose." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Five causes of apparent polysemanticity**: "1. Bad tools… 2. Bad explanations… 3. Bad learning… 4. Recursive generalization… 5. Superposition." Superposition should be "the last hypothesis" rather than the null — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Neurons as gates/verbs**: "I used to think of neurons as human-shaped nouns… Now I think of them more as control flow… often its job is to gate a region of the network." "The negative side tells us what distinction matters at this point in the circuit." Example: 4e:55, the "cat/car" neuron, is reinterpreted as a **V-shape neuron** whose negative pole is an O. Its inputs and readers include V-shaped flames, fireboat water jets and fox faces — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Generalized pose neurons**: "InceptionV1 has more than a hundred of these generalized pose neurons, each one its own clean detector of a pose shared across cars, dogs and other objects." These reinterpret the Zoom In car→dog "superposition" circuit — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Feature-visualization reward hacking**: in late InceptionV1 layers, gradient optimization "takes the easiest path, which in InceptionV1 often means dogs." The fix is to bias optimization toward the routes taken by top dataset examples, "often only for the first 64 steps" — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **SwiGLU double-negative "echo"**: "When both [gate and value] are negative, this sends activations into the positive pole, that usually look like the negative pole… for about 4% of neurons, it's the whole top of the list. Ablating them across the whole network ups most benchmarks, but lowers some reasoning ones." In vision SwiGLU, "the value tends to learn broader things, and gate more specific things." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Residual-stream saliency dimension**: ConvNet residual maps "came out as one big arc" because "the network was putting almost all of its activation mass into a single unread dimension, which it can change to scale all the other dimensions that are read through LayerNorm." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Language-model motifs**:
  - Summary motif: attention collects on punctuation. A "polysemantic comma neuron" actually tracked Colorado.
  - "Say or just think": the last layer is "largely used to suppress single tokens."
  - "Don't say a dot": the essay's reinterpretation of the Towards Monosemanticity intro neuron, where "eight of its ten strongest negative logit connections begin with" a dot.
  - Long-context and SEO-spam neurons looked polysemantic only because the data was insufficient.
  - [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **MoE experts as "an anti-superposition architecture"**: "a neuron only fires when its expert is chosen… the router would have to recognize all ten thousand to know when to send a token there, and the router is just one small linear layer." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Circuit ecology / circuit racing / gradient starvation / "semantic gravity"**: generalization wins because "circuits race to capture the remaining error, and the ones that reuse what's already there… [grow fastest]." "circuits have a kind of semantic gravity, and arbitrary packing… has to fight against it." The essay frames this as thinking "more about learning speed than storage capacity." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Exploratory vs. enumerative interpretability; tethered abstraction**: the essay favors question-driven exploration over "a big list of features." It advocates "views that let us zoom out while keeping the larger patterns connected to the neurons and circuits." Probes and sparse dictionaries "belong at the end, not the start." An SAE "fractures" the V neuron "into a car feature and a cat feature." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Evaluation methods**: blind maximum; per-bundle blind maximum after clustering incoming attribution, for example a pet-microchip neuron with three upstream bundles; near misses; and distant-concept controls. The author is skeptical of simulation scores, saying they "often end up measuring where attention puts information." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Edge motifs (edge featurizers)**: "recurring patterns of connections in attribution graphs. If neurons make distinctions, these motifs map how one distinction feeds another." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Vision for a solution**: "a laboratory run by automated scientists who watch the network through training and inference… So maybe all we have to do is plug one into the other." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

### Inferences
- The conceptual core is a shift from asking whether a neuron is monosemantic to asking **what distinction it gates, and from which local origin it grew**. Several "mechanisms" are interpretive reframings rather than new algorithms: the V-neuron, pose neurons, gates and recursive generalization. The SwiGLU echo regime and the residual saliency dimension are concrete, checkable architectural findings.
- The negative-pole-as-gate idea carries the most testable weight in the language-model results, as in the Santa ablation (8 negative-pole neurons → 6% vs. 20 labeled neurons → 76%).

### Gaps
- Technical details of the SwiGLU echo ablation are not given: which model, which benchmarks and effect sizes. The essay defers them, saying "we haven't looked into it deeply."
- The edge-featurizer method is described only briefly, with no released algorithm.

## Q4. What does it leave open, and what would falsify its main claim?

### Takeaway
The author leaves open:
- full audits at the trillion-parameter scale
- attention, which he says has made "less progress"
- whether LM residual streams need a fix like the one in vision
- a trusted semantic test for weak activations
- a few unexplained InceptionV1 neurons
- a rigorous whole-model test of recursive generalization, which needs training checkpoints LLMs lack

He states several falsifiers: one convincing inexplicable group of neurons, a real non-local superposition circuit, or rare facts carried by unrelated neurons. He also concedes that packing does occur in data with no structure, the toy-model world.

### Cited Findings
**Stated limitations and open questions**
- "Language models… aren't close to solved due to sheer size." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Kimi K3: "We haven't begun full audits of this scale yet." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Attention: "We've made less progress on attention than on neurons, and it's mostly outside the scope of this essay." The marginal "explanatory residual" of global attention-head names is guessed at "about 5%". An agent looking at 100 attribution graphs independently also put it at about 5%. "these estimates could be pretty far off." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Residual stream in LMs: "We don't know whether language models need a similar fix." On continuous packing ("manifolds woven through each other"): "Ruling that out entirely may not be possible. But in practice it hasn't been a bottleneck." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Weak activations: "We don't yet have a semantic test for weak activations that we trust… Broader category-based screening gives mixed results, so these spot checks cannot establish what weak activations mean in general." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Unresolved: an InceptionV1 "pinched-center" neuron (bow ties, butterflies, swimsuit tops, plus dress shoes and flip-flops) is "better described as generalization with semantic residue than arbitrary packing, but I don't fully understand these exceptions yet." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Recursive generalization is not proven everywhere: "We haven't thoroughly shown that every case of heavy polysemanticity is recursive generalization… for something like a huge language model we don't have the checkpoints to watch it happen. Testing this rigorously across a whole model isn't our priority right now." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Scale argument: "We're not at all sure about this, and laying out even what we've found so far would take at least another paper." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- The noise-label model was not fully audited: "I didn't fully audit the model." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Limits of the blind maximum: it "tests the neuron's center, not its breadth." Breaking wrong explanations takes "creativity and judgment rather than a summary statistic." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- SwiGLU echo: "We think the answer is probably prosaic… But we haven't looked into it deeply. Architecture designers, we think you should look into fixing this!" — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Alignment caveat: "Alignment is supposed to be hard too, and it may well be." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**Explicit falsifiers and the evidence that would change the author's mind**
- "I would update strongly on even one convincing example" of "a substantial group of neurons whose joint activity remained inexplicable." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- A second real superposition circuit: "To my knowledge, no one has published a second circuit with the right shape." "I think the bar for claiming it should be high." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Developmental test: "recursive generalization and superposition predict nearly opposite things, and you can watch a neuron grow and see which one happens." Specifically: "everything on [a branch] should trace back to the same local origin in an extremely high-dimensional semantic space, and it should grow out from there over training in a particular order." Superposition would show non-local, unrelated additions — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Rare facts: "The prediction is testable: the neurons and heads that carry a rare fact should be related to what the fact is about, not unrelated ones chosen because they rarely collide." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Per-bundle blind maximum: "A bundle that had been packed in rather than grown would show up here as an explanation unrelated to the others that still drives the neuron." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Conceded domain where packing does happen: "memories that really are ends in themselves, related to nothing else. That's the toy's world, and there it does pack, just as the paper found. So I don't think we disagree with the paper about the toy's world. The question is which world real networks are in." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**Explicit predictions**
- "My low-confidence prediction is that attention heads will turn out to be broad and recursively generalized, like neurons in small models, since there are far fewer of them: a few thousand heads in Qwen3-32B, against more than a million neurons." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Scale: "it's why we think superposition wouldn't show up at the next scale." "the closer we look at large language models, the cleaner their neurons get." The essay cites Anthropic's SGTM finding that knowledge becomes more localized — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Bigger models learn narrower neurons that are easier to recognize. Simplified or small models make neurons "learn less well." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Timeline (speculative): automated scientists "may be able to do in months in frontier language models what took humans most of a decade for vision models." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Deception: "Deception might hide from someone reading a model's outputs, but it has no reason to hide from the model's own circuitry… My fear is that deception will be right there, in plain sight, and we won't know how to read it." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

**Implicit predictions (my reading, not the author's words; the anchoring evidence is cited)**
- Squeezing a monosemantic layer should yield coarser but still interpretable neurons, not superposed mixtures, and an SAE should not recover the original fine detectors from it. The essay tested this on InceptionV1 mixed3b and the medium ConvNeXt last layer — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- Ablating weak activations should cost little, and the answers it changes should be near-ties between semantically related classes. Ablating strong activations should be catastrophic — [Essay HTML figure](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/)
- Neuron-only attribution graphs in frontier LMs should ablate as predicted, and negative-pole neurons should often matter more than positively labeled ones — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)

### Inferences
- The thesis is falsifiable at specific points: developmental branching, the rarity of distant-concept firing, rare-fact routing, and a single robust inexplicable neuron group. But the main headline number (~90%) is threshold-dependent by the author's own admission, so disagreements may be partly definitional. Liv Gorton's and Neel Nanda's responses ("more ideas than neurons" is still true) point to this terminology split, which the essay itself flags ("'superposition' is itself in superposition").
- The essay's evidence for "no superposition" is strongest for vision models up to 28.6M parameters and for selected LM case studies. For trillion-parameter models it is explicitly anecdotal so far.

### Gaps
- Quantitative falsification thresholds are not given. For example, the essay does not say what fraction of distant-concept firing, or how many inexplicable groups, would count against the thesis.
- How well the developmental test transfers to LLMs without checkpoints is left open.

## Q6. Minimum context from his earlier Circuits work needed to understand the new paper

### Takeaway
The essay revisits and partly overturns Cammarata's own Distill Circuits-era claims:
- Zoom In's framework of features, circuits and universality, including its polysemantic-neuron and "superposition" examples (4e:55 cat/car; the car→dog circuit he found in 2019)
- Curve Detectors, his flagship monosemanticity case
- Multimodal Neurons' "conjoined features"
- the weight-level circuit reading pioneered in Curve Circuits and Visualizing Weights

### Cited Findings
- **The Distill Circuits thread (OpenAI)**. Cammarata co-authored:
  - Zoom In (Olah, Cammarata, Schubert, Goh, Petrov, Carter)
  - An Overview of Early Vision in InceptionV1
  - Curve Detectors (first author: Cammarata, Goh, Carter, Schubert, Petrov, Olah)
  - Naturally Occurring Equivariance
  - High-Low Frequency Detectors
  - Curve Circuits (first author: Cammarata, Goh, Carter, Voss, Schubert, Olah)
  - Visualizing Weights
  - Branch Specialization
  - Weight Banding
  - The thread page gives 10 Mar 2020 as the thread date.
  - [Distill Circuits thread](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/)
- **Zoom In's three claims**: the article "proposes three claims which, if true, might justify serious inquiry": "the existence of meaningful features, the existence of meaningful circuits between features" and "the universality of those features and circuits." — [Distill Circuits thread](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/)
- **Polysemantic neuron 4e:55**: the essay says this "most famous polysemantic neuron" of InceptionV1 "looked like an arbitrary mixture of cats and cars." It also says Zoom In "said outright that it 'isn't responding to some commonality of cars and cat faces'". In Multimodal Neurons, the team asked whether "perhaps cat eyes and car lights look similar," then concluded it was "genuinely OR(cat, car)." These are Zoom In and Multimodal Neurons quotes as reproduced in the essay — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md); original at [Zoom In](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/zoom-in/)
- **The original "superposition" circuit**: "In 2019, Chris Olah and I found the circuit that was originally introduced as the superposition phenomenon in Zoom In. It showed a car feature appearing to spread into a set of unrelated dog neurons." The car neuron is 4c:447, assembled from "basically monosemantic neurons for car parts like windows, car bodies, and wheels" in 4b. The essay says Zoom In presented the V neuron and this circuit as its "two main examples of why its picture of neurons… might be 'overly rosy'." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Weight-level circuit reading**: Curve Circuits aimed to "reverse engineer a non-trivial learned algorithm from the weights of a neural network". Visualizing Weights presents "techniques for visualizing, contextualizing, and understanding neural network weights." — [Distill Circuits thread](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/)
- Cammarata's first Circuits interface "only had feature visualization and weights connecting their neurons". He says it was this weights-plus-feature-visualization view that led to the superposition reading, because "it didn't prompt diving more into the tools, which would have revealed feature visualization reward hacking." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Curve detectors as the monosemanticity benchmark**: "In Curve Detectors, we wrote forty pages of experiments on ten neurons precisely to identify at least one sure case of a monosemanticity." The ten curve neurons live in InceptionV1 layer 3b, the layer the new paper squeezes from 480 to 10 channels. The essay also says SAE "missing curve detectors" (Gorton 2024) decompose into existing curve-responsive neurons — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md); [Curve Detectors](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/curve-detectors/)
- **Conjoined features → recursive generalization**: the essay credits Multimodal Neurons (2021, feature-properties section) as the first appearance of the idea, renamed here — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md); [Multimodal Neurons](https://distill.pub/2021/multimodal-neurons/#feature-properties)
- **Universality, revisited**: the generalized pose neuron recurs across InceptionV1, DINOv2 (">ten times the size and about ten years of architecture later") and the large and medium ConvNeXts — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **Superposition terminology lineage**: Zoom In introduced the term to circuits work "with a single circuit example". Toy Models of Superposition (2022) "later defined superposition as representing more features than dimensions." The essay adopts the narrower meaning: "neurons learning unrelated things together because they rarely interfere." — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md)
- **2023 OpenAI automated interpretability** (GPT-2 XL, Bills et al.) is the LM baseline (~1.7%, or "fewer than 2%" clean) that the essay revisits — [Essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/essay.md); [OpenAI neuron-explainer paper](https://openaipublic.blob.core.windows.net/neuron-explainer/paper/index.html)

### Inferences
- The new paper is in effect a self-correction of Zoom In's two counterexamples to its "rosy" view of neurons. It keeps the features/circuits/universality program but replaces "superposition" with "recursive generalization" as the default account of polysemanticity. A reader needs only to know what 4e:55, the 4c:447 car→dog circuit, the 3b curve detectors and conjoined features were to follow the argument.

### Gaps
- The Zoom In page was too large to fetch (over 10 MB), so its quotes are taken as reproduced in the new essay rather than checked against the original text.
- The Curve Detectors fetch returned only neuron grids, so its exact date and in-text claims were not re-verified here. The author list was verified from the thread index.
