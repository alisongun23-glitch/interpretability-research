# Real Opening Hooks of High-View Science/Tech/Math Explainer Videos (catalogue + patterns)

**Method note (read first).** Spoken lines below come straight from each video's own YouTube caption track. I loaded each video in a browser and read the caption file the YouTube player downloaded (json3 timedtext) on 2026-10-29. "MANUAL" means creator-uploaded captions, which match the script word for word. "ASR" means YouTube's auto-captions: the wording is right but punctuation and capitals are added by me. View counts and publish dates come from each video's watch page (`videoDetails.viewCount`) on the same day. Timestamps [Ns] are the caption start times.
**Visuals:** I could not take frame screenshots because the browser pane was hidden. So the "On screen" notes come from two places, marked as such: (T) inferred from words in the transcript like "this", "here" or "There it goes", and (M) my own memory of the video, which is lower confidence. Quotes are trimmed with "…" to stay under ~40 words.

## What exactly is said in the first 1-3 sentences of each video?

### Takeaway
I collected 39 openings from the caption tracks themselves (36 have speech in the first 30 s, 3 open on music only). Nearly all of them state the video's central object, claim or question within 0–10 seconds. None opens with a channel intro or logo. The only greetings are Vsauce's signature "Hey, Vsauce. Michael here." and Code Bullet's "hey guys". Over half of the openings are under 15 words before the first full stop.

### Inferences
- Median time to the first content-bearing sentence is about 0–4 s. The only openings that delay speech are wordless demo builds (sammyuri, PowerPoint, Primer Aggression). Those rely on the title and thumbnail plus spectacle.
- Of the 36 spoken openings, the "This is a [thing]" or "This right here…" form appears in at least 9: 3B1B neural network, 3B1B Fourier, Welch Labs, Ben Eater ×2, Mark Rober squirrel, Vsauce red, Veritasium analog at 25 s, and From Scratch ("This is the single greatest achievement…"). It is the most common single construction for hardware and visual-math topics.

## Which recurring structures appear?

### Takeaway
Seven structures cover almost all 39 openings: (1) "This is X" / object-first, (2) payoff or result first, then rewind, (3) an impossible or counterintuitive claim stated flatly, (4) "Familiar thing… but" (the "this is X, but…" reversal), (5) a question or thought-experiment quiz, (6) a specific number for scale or effort, (7) a personal stake or confession. Most hooks stack two or three of these within 30 s.

### Cited Findings
- **(1) "This is X":** "This is a 3." — [3B1B](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk); "This is an activation atlas." — [Welch Labs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UZDiGooFs54); "This is a bird feeder…" — [Mark Rober](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hFZFjoX2cGg); "This appears blue…" — [Vsauce](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=evQsOFQju08).
- **(2) Payoff first, then rewind:** "But to understand how we got to this point, first, we need to rewind a bit." — [Mark Rober](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xoxhDk-hwuo); "we need to go back eight weeks ago" — [Mark Rober](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hFZFjoX2cGg); "There it goes. — Yay!" then the origin story — [Steve Mould](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IxXaizglscw).
- **(3) Impossible or counterintuitive claim:** "There is a hole at the bottom of math" — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HeQX2HjkcNo); "…remarkably, minus a twelfth." — [Numberphile](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w-I6XTVZXww); "color is an illusion" — [Vsauce](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=evQsOFQju08); "it still seems wrong even if you know the answer" — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSNsgj1OCLA).

But today, a perfect storm…" — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GVsUOuSjvcg); "While this may look like… it's actually composed of 2.1 million vertices" — [Branch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C8YtdC8mxTU); "you shouldn't be impressed because… You should be impressed because…" — [From Scratch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zXPiqk0-zDY); "Humans rule Earth without competition. - **(5) Question or quiz:** "how long would it take for the bulb to light up? Is it half a second, one second…" — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bHIhgxav9LY); "What if they all had the same solution?" — [Veritasium AlphaFold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_fHJIYENdI); "what if we just start with a microprocessor?" — [Ben Eater](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LnzuMJLZRdU); "How can a bunch of stupid things do smart things together?" — [Kurzgesagt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=16W7c0mb-rE).

- Practitioner framing (secondary): one hook guide recommends that "The first 10 seconds prove the title is true with one real example," the next 10 say why it matters, and the last 10 preview the video — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/). ### Inferences
- The 3B1B neural-network opening is a reusable template for logic-gate or XOR-style explainers. Show a trivially easy thing ("This is a 3"), say how effortless it is for humans, then flip it: writing a program to do it is "dauntingly difficult". Stakes come next ("relevance… to the future"), then a scope promise ("not as a buzzword but as a piece of math"), all within ~85 s.
- The "you shouldn't be impressed by A; you should be impressed by B" move (From Scratch) and the "this looks like X, it's actually Y" move (Branch) suit computer-built-from-unlikely-parts videos especially well.

### Gaps
- I found no primary-source interview where Derek Muller, Grant Sanderson or others break down their own first 30 seconds. Search returned only secondary commentary. The Veritasium clickbait video itself (catalogued above) is the best primary source on title and thumbnail strategy.

## How do titles and thumbnails pair with the spoken hook?

### Takeaway
In almost every case the first spoken sentence confirms or restates the title promise within 0–12 s. Often it is nearly the title itself said aloud: "There is a riddle that is so counterintuitive…", "This video is about natural selection", "The initials GPT stand for…", "Some things are not normal". Big channels have also retitled older videos so the title now carries the hook's core image (CGP Grey's horses, Veritasium's "Game of Life" pun).

### Cited Findings
- Title restated at 0 s: "The Riddle That Seems Impossible Even If You Know The Answer" is followed by "There is a riddle that is so counterintuitive, it still seems wrong even if you know the answer." — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSNsgj1OCLA)
- "A.I. ‐ Humanity's Final Invention?" is answered with "our last invention" at 7 s — [Kurzgesagt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fa8k8IQ1_X0)
- "Emergence – How Stupid Things Become Smart Together" opens "An ant is pretty stupid… many ants together are smart" and names "emergence" at 28 s — [Kurzgesagt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=16W7c0mb-rE)
- "But what is a neural network?" opens on a concrete task and promises at 75 s to "show you what a neural network actually is" — [3B1B](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk)
- "I Made A Water Computer And It Actually Works" opens with the "it actually works" proof ("There it goes. — Yay!") at 3 s — [Steve Mould](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IxXaizglscw)
- "I Made a 32-bit Computer Inside Terraria" is confirmed with "running on a computer that is itself running inside of the game Terraria" at 16 s — [From Scratch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zXPiqk0-zDY)
- "Math's Fundamental Flaw" is restated as "There is a hole at the bottom of math" at 1 s — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HeQX2HjkcNo)
- "AlphaFold - The Most Useful Thing AI Has Ever Done": the hook escalates the title ("all of the world's biggest problems… the same solution") but holds back the word "AlphaFold" for 30+ s, creating a curiosity gap — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_fHJIYENdI)
- Title and thumbnail matter more than content for distribution, per Derek Muller: his basketball-from-a-dam video got "16.3 million views, but almost none of those views came on the YouTube platform. Why? Because I gave it this thumbnail." — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2xHZPH5Sng). A secondary summary says the video separates mild clickbait (fine if the video delivers) from misleading clickbait — [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective/).
- Retitling evidence (current titles from the watch pages): CGP Grey 7Pq-S557XQU is now "What Happened to Horses Is Happening to Us", and R9OHn5ZF4Uo is now "AI Doesn't Know Anything. It Just Passes Tests." — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Pq-S557XQU), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R9OHn5ZF4Uo). Their original titles ("Humans Need Not Apply", "How Machines Learn") are from my memory, not verified.
- Mismatch case: "You've (Likely) Been Playing The Game Of Life Wrong" opens on "Some things are not normal" and power laws, not Conway's Game of Life — [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HBluLfX2F_k). The title is metaphorical or curiosity-driven, and the hook pays it off obliquely.
- Wordless-hook builds (sammyuri, 7.65M; PowerPoint, 1.23M) depend entirely on an absurd title promise ("Minecraft in Minecraft", "Turing Completeness of PowerPoint") while the footage proves it — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-BP7DhHTU-I), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uNjxe8ShM-8).

### Inferences
- Pattern for this project: the title states an implausible build or claim, the first 3–5 s show it working or state it flatly, and the first full sentence restates the title in plain words. Then comes the "why this is surprising / how could that be?" pivot. Delaying the reveal works only when the title already sets up a curiosity gap (AlphaFold) or the footage is spectacle (sammyuri).
- "But what is X?" (3B1B) shows that a humble question title works when the first line starts with a vivid concrete example rather than a definition.

### Gaps
- Thumbnails were not inspected (no screenshots), so the thumbnail-to-hook pairing is described only for Veritasium's own account and the titles.
- No A/B test data on the retitles was found. The original titles and retitle dates are unverified.
