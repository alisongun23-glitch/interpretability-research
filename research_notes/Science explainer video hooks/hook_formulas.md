# Named Hook Formulas and Templates for Short- and Long-Form Video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, YouTube)

Research note, compiled 2026-10-29. About 18 searches and fetches. **Source-quality warning:** most "hook formula" content online is secondhand. It comes from tool-vendor blogs, AI-generated transcript summaries, and Claude-skill marketplace listings. Primary material from Kallaway, Hormozi, Brendan Kane and Paddy Galloway was mostly not fetchable, so I name the type of every source. **Example status legend:** [GUIDE] = the example is cited in a guide or article, but I did not verify it on the platform. [ANTI] = a counter-example quoted by a source. None of the examples below were verified directly on TikTok or YouTube during this session.

## Key Question 0 (Objective): Taxonomy of hook types, with fill-in-the-blank templates, real examples and when each works

### Takeaway
One fetched guide (vidIQ, Dec 2025) gives verbatim fill-in templates for about 9 of the 16 requested types. Real, attributable examples for each type are scarce in the sources I could reach. The best sourced examples are Veritasium, Johnny Harris, Red Bull, Ian Lauer, Jenny Hoyos ($1 chicken sandwich) and Kallaway. Several requested types ("this shouldn't be possible", "nobody talks about", the "but" turn, "what if") have templates or descriptions only, with no verified viral example found.

### Cited Findings

**Verbatim templates from vidIQ, "9 YouTube Hooks That Keep Viewers Watching" (Uttaran Samaddar, published Dec 15, 2025).** No creator examples are named in the article.
- **Curiosity hook / open loop:** "I thought I knew [topic], until I found out [surprising twist]." Specific works better than vague. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Story in medias res ("Drop Into the Action"):** "It's [time]. I'm [in the middle of problem]. Here's what I did next." Start mid-moment, with stakes. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Audience-centric / negative ("you're doing X wrong"):** "You're doing [common mistake], and it's costing you [pain]. Let's fix it." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Time-promise (number/specificity variant):** "In the next [X] minutes, you'll learn [specific outcome] so you can [benefit]." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Stakes/consequences:** "If you don't fix [problem], you'll keep [bad outcome]. Here's the workaround." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Contrarian / negative ("stop doing X"):** "Stop doing [popular advice]. Do this instead, and here's why." vidIQ warns that it needs logic and proof behind it. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Visual hook / pattern interrupt:** "Show [unexpected visual] first, then say: 'This happened because [reason].'" The visual must tie to the message: "weird is fine, but pointless is fatal." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Shocking stat (number/specificity):** "[Surprising stat]. That's why most people fail at [goal]. Here's the fix." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **"What if" / unanswered question:** "What if [assumption] is wrong, and the real reason is [new idea]?" — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)

**Other templates (Jenny Hoyos, via a vidIQ profile)**

- How the requested types map onto the sourced templates:
  - Contrarian and "stop doing X" are one family (vidIQ #6, Hoyos "I never do THIS").

- "This shouldn't be possible", "nobody talks about this" and "This is X. Nothing special. Except..." are widespread as creator-copy patterns. The report writer should present them as unsourced patterns, or with templates written from scratch (for example "This is [ordinary object]. Nothing special. Except [impossible property]."), and not as cited formulas.
- When each type works, based on the sources:
  - Curiosity, stat and "what if" suit explainer/science content, where the twist is the payoff.
  - Contrarian needs proof in the following seconds, per vidIQ and Kallaway's lock-in zone.
  - Comparison/scale and challenge formats suit repeatable series (Galloway/Colin and Samir, Hoyos content buckets).
  - Visual interrupts fail when they are unrelated to the topic (vidIQ).

## Key Question 1: What templates and frameworks do the named sources give, verbatim where possible?

### Takeaway
The best-documented frameworks are these:
- **MrBeast's leaked doc:** the first minute proves that the thumbnail's promise will be met.
- **Kallaway:** the "triple hook" (text, visual, spoken), then a 5–10 s "lock-in zone", then a hook → set-up → re-hook rhythm.
- **Jenny Hoyos:** a ≤3 s hook that works on mute, with foreshadowing and a but/so conflict chain.

Paddy Galloway, Colin and Samir, Hormozi, Kane and Retention Rabbit are only weakly documented on hooks specifically. Galloway's public material is mostly about packaging.

### Cited Findings

**MrBeast production document**
- Background: a 36-page internal doc posted by Rosanna Pansino on Aug 13, 2024. Two former producers told Passionfruit it is legitimate, and a MrBeast spokesperson declined to comment. It likely dates to about 2022. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/)
- The first minute "is the most important minute of each video", because that is when people click off most. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/)
- "I just need you to make people watch as long as possible." — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/)
- Simon Willison's summary:
  - "The first minute captures the viewer's attention and demonstrates that their expectations from the thumbnail will be met."
  - "Minutes 1-3, 3-6 and 6-end all have their own clearly defined responsibilities as well."
  - "Losing 21 million viewers in the first minute after 60 million initial clicks is considered a reasonably good result!"
  - "The creative process for every video they produce starts with the title and thumbnail."
  - "If a viewer feels their expectations are not being matched, they'll click away."
  - Source: [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- The doc refers to CTR, average view duration (AVD) and average view percentage (AVP). — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/amp/)

**Kallaway (Kane Kallaway)**
- Script shape: "I think of it like a hook, there's a dance, and in that dance, you have context and conflict, which is basically just set up, rehook. Set up, rehook." Then "at the very end, I tried to hook them again with, like, the ending so that they share it." — [Castmagic, "Rhythm, Hooks, and a Billion Views" (Aug 2024)](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)
- He mixes short, medium and long sentences and varies his speaking pace. — [Castmagic](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)
- Triple hook, the lock-in zone and text-hook rules: see Key Question 2. — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)

**Jenny Hoyos**
- A hook is about 3 s: concise, visually compelling, and conveying the gist of the story. "It needs to be so good that you can be watching the video on mute and still know what it's about." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/)
- Foreshadow the ending. Chain conflict → resolution → new conflict using "but" and "so". Pacing runs fast hook, medium middle, fast peak, medium resolution, quick end. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/)
- "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts." She also says that if a premise could work as a long-form title and thumbnail and still get clicks, it will work as a Short. Sources here are secondary or AI summaries of her interviews (YouTube Shorts deep dive, My First Million, Creator Science). — [Demand Curve newsletter #187](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-187); [YouTube Blog Shorts deep dive](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/); [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)

**Paddy Galloway / Colin and Samir**
- "YouTube isn't just about making great videos - it's about winning the click." Their rule: "If they don't click they don't watch." — [Colin and Samir, "The New Rules of YouTube from Paddy Galloway" (©2024)](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- Secondary analysis says Galloway-style intros use "opening storylines and creating intrigue" and leave unanswered questions. — [ScaleViews (Beehiiv)](https://scaleviews.beehiiv.com/p/how-paddy-galloway-forever-broke-the-youtube-algorithm)

**Brendan Kane**
- A "hook point" is the element that grabs attention instantly and signals value. Kane frames his method as data-driven ("it's science, it's about looking at the data"). — [Blinkist summary](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en); [Meio & Mensagem interview](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/websummit/como-chamar-a-atencao-do-publico-em-3-segundos)

### Inferences
- The MrBeast doc and Galloway agree that the hook is defined by the packaging. The title and thumbnail set the promise, and the intro's job is to confirm it fast. That makes "clickbait mismatch" the central long-form failure mode.
- Kallaway's set-up/re-hook loop and Hoyos's but/so chain are the same mechanism: a series of small open loops after the main hook.

## Key Question 2: What are the rules for spoken, text and visual hook alignment?

### Takeaway
Kallaway's "triple hook" is the main source. The text hook (on-screen words, not captions), the visual hook and the spoken hook must each imply the others. Misalignment is a primary cause of failure. Hoyos's "works on mute" rule is the practical test.

### Cited Findings
- **Definitions:**
  - Text hook = words on screen, "not the captions", which he calls the most important component.
  - Visual hook = the visuals shown beyond what is written or said.
  - Spoken hook = the line said out loud.
  - A fourth "caption hook" (the first caption line above the fold) is set aside.
  - Source: [SozAI transcript, Kallaway "The Psychology of Killer Hooks"](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **Alignment test:** do the visuals support the text and speech, does the text support both, and does the speech support both? A viewer should be able to guess the others from any one component. — [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **Text-hook rules:**
  - Include a subject repeater (proper noun or jargon) plus a pain point, a dream outcome or a future-state change.
  - Use specific numbers.
  - Use at most 2 lines, broken at natural phrase breaks.
  - Use no punctuation other than quotes or parentheses, and no transition words ("and/or/that/but/therefore").
  - Keep it on screen about 3 s.
  - Place it where the eye lands first, without covering the face, eyes or mouth.
  - Source: [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **Spoken-hook rules:**
  - Speed means compression (subject plus stakes in as few words as possible), not talking faster.
  - Clarity means only one possible interpretation, with no wordplay.
  - Formats: question, tutorial, problem-solution, and "how to do a bigger task in a shorter time".
  - Use "power word" bundles.
  - Source: [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **Visual-hook categories:** graphic/text overlays, pattern interrupts/visual switching, subject motion, VFX/transitions, and visual selection. The simplest options are moving your body, the visual or the text. Overdone visual hooks look gimmicky. — [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **Mute test:** "It needs to be so good that you can be watching the video on mute and still know what it's about." — Jenny Hoyos via [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/)
- Motion's ad-analysis framework also breaks hooks into spoken hook, visual hook and text overlay. — [Motion hook analysis](https://motionapp.com/library/frameworks/hook-analysis)

### Inferences
- For science explainers, the alignment rule implies all three point at the same subject. For example: the text hook "This metal remembers its shape", the visual of wire springing back in hot water, and the spoken hook stating the claim plus the stakes.

### Gaps
- The SozAI page is an automated transcript or summary. It renders the name as "Callaway" and I did not check it against the original video. Kallaway's own newsletter and course (where "SSSQ" is presumably defined) were not accessed.

## Key Question 3: What do sources say about the first 1, 3 and 5 seconds vs the first 30–60 seconds of long-form?

### Takeaway
On short-form the guidance is about 1 s to stop the scroll (Hoyos), a ≤3–5 s hook (Hoyos, Kallaway), then a 5–10 s "lock-in" proof zone (Kallaway). On long-form, YouTube's Intro metric measures 30 s. Third-party guides target about 70%+ retention at 30 s. MrBeast treats the whole first minute as the most important, and losing about 35% of viewers there counts as "reasonably good".

### Cited Findings
- **About 1 s:** "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts." — Hoyos, via [Demand Curve #187](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-187)
- **About 3 s:** the hook is about 3 seconds. — [vidIQ on Hoyos](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/). Kane's book is subtitled "How to Stand Out in a 3-Second World". — [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- **3–5 s hook, then 5–10 s lock-in:** the hook (roughly the first 3–5 s) must interrupt the scroll. The "lock-in zone" (next 5–10 s, or 2–4 sentences) must confirm the claim and build trust through looks, packaging quality, relatability and proof (the "trust anchor"). — Kallaway via [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **First 5–10 s (long-form):** cut anything that delays value in the first 5–10 s. The first 10 s are the video's "audition". Most creators lose 20% of viewers in the first 15 s. The 20% figure is unsourced in the article. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **30 s (official):** YouTube's Intro retention figure "tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds". — quoted in [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-analytics-improve-video-hooks-intros)
- **30 s benchmarks (third-party, they conflict):**
  - "70%+ retention at 30 seconds is solid. 80%+ is exceptional"; below 50% means the hook needs work. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/what-is-good-youtube-audience-retention)
  - 30–40% of viewers leave within 30 s. — [Boss Wallah](https://bosswallah.com/blog/creator-hub/youtube-retention-in-the-first-30-seconds-how-views-are-won-or-lost/)
- **First minute (MrBeast):** the "most important minute"; 21M of 60M clickers lost in minute one counts as "reasonably good". — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences
- A practical layered model from these sources:
  - frame 0–1 s: visual stop
  - 1–3 s: subject plus stakes (aligned text, visual and spoken hook)
  - 3–10 s: proof / lock-in
  - long-form 10–60 s: deliver on the thumbnail promise and open the next loop
- The MrBeast "first minute" and the YouTube "30 s Intro" metric measure different windows. Benchmarks should not be mixed.

### Gaps
- No platform-official (TikTok or Meta) guidance on first-second retention was retrieved. Third-party benchmark numbers are not methodologically sourced.

## Key Question 4: Which hook mistakes kill retention?

### Takeaway
The best-sourced killers are these:
- Thumbnail or title promises that the intro does not meet (MrBeast doc).
- Slow or long intros that delay value (vidIQ).
- Misaligned text, visual and spoken hooks (Kallaway).
- Vague or clever wording.
- Claims without proof (Kallaway's lock-in).
- Gimmicky visuals unrelated to the message (vidIQ, Kallaway).

"Hey guys" greetings and logo intros are folk wisdom here. I found no fetched source stating them explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- **Expectation mismatch / clickbait:** "If a viewer feels their expectations are not being matched, they'll click away". The first minute must show that the "expectations from the thumbnail will be met". — [Simon Willison on the MrBeast doc](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- **Slow intros:** long-winded intros and intros that delay value. vidIQ attributes early drop-off to the intro, not the content. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Pointless visuals:** "weird is fine, but pointless is fatal." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Kallaway's list:**
  - misaligned text, visual and spoken hooks
  - a spoken hook that is too long, or delivered too fast to absorb
  - vague or clever wording that requires thought
  - removing the text before about 3 s
  - more than 2 lines of text
  - a strong claim without proof in the lock-in zone
  - overdone visual gimmicks
  - Source: [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/)
- **Over-promising:** contrarian hooks need logic and proof behind them. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/)
- **Platform pacing mismatch:** slow-paced podcast clips often fail on TikTok and Instagram. — Kallaway via [Castmagic](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)
- **Weak premise:** "'I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard' is lame and you wouldn't click it." — MrBeast doc via [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences
- For science explainers, the riskiest pattern is an "impossible" or contrarian hook ("this shouldn't be possible") that is not resolved with a real demo or proof within about 10 s. It triggers both the MrBeast mismatch rule and Kallaway's lock-in failure.

### Gaps
- I found no source within this session for "hey guys", channel-logo or branded-intro advice, or for subscribe requests before value. vidIQ explicitly does not mention "hey guys", logos or clickbait.
- I found no quantitative A/B data on any specific mistake.
