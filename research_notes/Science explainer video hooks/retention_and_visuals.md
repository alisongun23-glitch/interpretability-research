# Retention data on video openings (first 3-30 s) and hook/visual pairing: YouTube long-form, Shorts, TikTok, Reels

Research date: 2026-10-29. Vendor numbers are flagged **[vendor]**. Older data is flagged with its year. Source-quality notes are inline.

---

## 1. What share of viewers leave in the first 30 s of long-form YouTube, and what do top channels get?

### Takeaway
YouTube's own "Intro" metric is the share of viewers still watching at the 30-second mark. YouTube publishes no benchmark for it. The best-documented vendor data (vidIQ, from its own channel, 2026) suggests that a good long-form intro keeps about 60-68% of viewers at 30 s, so it loses about 32-40%. Weak intros on the same channel keep under 40%. Most of the drop happens in the first few seconds, after which the curve flattens.

### Cited findings
- **Official definition:** "Intro tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds." YouTube says a high intro percentage may mean the opening matched the thumbnail and title. Its advice for a low intro figure is to change the thumbnail and title to better reflect the video, or to modify the opening 30 s and "experiment with different styles." — [YouTube Help, "Measure key moments for audience retention"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en) (fetched Oct 2026)
- **Other key moments YouTube reports** (same page):
  - "Top moments" are where almost no one dropped off. If they appear late, YouTube suggests moving that content earlier.
  - "Spikes" are segments that were rewatched or shared. A spike can also mean the content was unclear.
  - "Dips" are segments that were skipped or where viewers stopped watching.
  - Moments appear only if detected. The video must be at least 60 s long with at least 100 views, data takes 1-2 days to process, and the report is per video only. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)
- **"Stayed to watch"** is not a long-form intro metric. On long-form it appears in the definition of Average view duration. The Shorts equivalent is "Viewed vs. swiped away" (see Section 2). — [search summary of YouTube Help 1715160](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1715160?hl=en)
- **[vendor] vidIQ targets (page updated 29 Jun 2026):**
  - Keep at least 60% of viewers past 30 s; the strongest hooks exceed 65%.
  - Aim for about 30% average percentage viewed (APV) on a 6-9 min talking-head or educational video.
  - Third-party studies put the platform average APV in the low-to-mid 20s.
  - vidIQ's own long-form APV is 30.3% across 16.5M views; its Shorts APV is 73.6% (12 months to June 2026).
  — [vidIQ, "YouTube Audience Retention: What's a Good Rate"](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)
- **[vendor] vidIQ per-video 30-second retention, same channel (trailing 12 months to June 2026):**

  | Video | Still watching at 30 s | Views |
  |---|---|---|
  | How to Get 1000 Subscribers FASTER | 67.9% | 762K |
  | BIG Mistakes SMALL YouTubers STILL MAKE! | 65.5% | 2.98M |
  | If You're a SMALL YouTube Channel... | 64.6% | 947K |
  | The 9-Minute Algorithm Hack | 62.9% | 890K |
  | He Made $500,000 from Faceless YouTube Channels | 59.9% | 1.28M |
  | YouTube Settings You NEED to Know | 38.1% | 1.95M |

  - On one video, retention fell from about 95% in the opening seconds to about 65% at 30 s, then levelled off.
  - vidIQ names these causes of poor intros: slow or branded cold opens, a hook that doesn't match the title or thumbnail, no clear value promise within about 15 s, and slow pacing.
  — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)
- **[vendor, secondary] Retention Rabbit "2025 State of YouTube Audience Retention" (reportedly May 2025):**
  - Average retention across all videos is 23.7%.
  - 55%+ of viewers are lost in the first 60 s.
  - Only 16.8% of videos (about 1 in 6) exceed 50% average retention.
  - 5-10 min videos average 31.5%.
  - Intros holding more than 65% of viewers correlate with 58% higher average view duration.
  - **Caveat:** I could not reach the original report (the retentionrabbit.com URL I tried returned 404). Secondary blogs disagree on whether the 65% threshold is measured at 30 s or at 1 min. Treat these figures as unverified. — secondary citations surfaced via [subsub.io](https://learn.subsub.io/audience-retention) and [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/what-is-good-youtube-audience-retention)
- **[vendor/blog] Other rules of thumb, weaker sources:**
  - "Most videos lose 20-40% of their audience in the first 15-30 seconds." — [ytshark](https://ytshark.com/?p=288)
  - "~50-70% at 30 s is really solid for videos >5 min." — cited in [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/what-is-good-youtube-audience-retention)
  - Neither source shows its methodology.

### Inferences
- A realistic target for a 5-15 min science explainer is about 60-70% retained at 30 s, meaning roughly 30-40% leave. Below about 50% indicates a mismatch between the opening and the title/thumbnail, or a slow start.
- The intro is the steepest part of the curve. Small gains there compound into average view duration, which is consistent with the reported Retention Rabbit 65%-to-58% AVD correlation, if that figure holds.

### Gaps
- No YouTube-published distribution of Intro scores (median or percentiles) exists.
- There are no category-specific (Education/Science) 30-s benchmarks from a verifiable primary report.
- The Retention Rabbit original report and its methodology were not reachable.

## 2. What are the 3-second hold and swipe benchmarks for Shorts, Reels and TikTok?

### Takeaway
No platform publishes official 3-second hold or swipe benchmarks.
- **Shorts:** YouTube reports "Viewed vs. swiped away." Creator-tool sources call 70-80% viewed good and 50-60% typical for struggling channels. These are vendor-reported and unaudited.
- **Meta:** The only platform-published funnel numbers are Facebook's 2016-era figures. Of people who watch 3 s, 65% reach 10 s and 45% reach 30 s.
- **TikTok:** Its official data is about ad recall in the first 6 s, not hold rates.

### Cited findings
- **YouTube Shorts metric:** Studio > Analytics > Content > Shorts shows "How many chose to view," split into viewed vs. swiped away; the two shares sum to about 100%. — [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics)
- **No official benchmark:** Guides note YouTube publishes no official targets or algorithmic cutoff for viewed vs. swiped away. — [1of10 (2026)](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/); [ByteCap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- **[vendor] Suggested ranges:**
  - 75-80% viewed is good. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-viral)
  - Above 70% viewed is good; most struggling channels sit at 50-60%. — [Joyspace (2026)](https://joyspace.ai/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-growth)
  - One real channel's 90-day figure was 26.6% viewed vs. 73.4% swiped away. Social Media Examiner advises benchmarking against your own channel. — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-analyze-your-youtube-shorts/)
- **Shorts view-count change, 31 Mar 2025:**
  - A Shorts "view" now counts on every start or replay, with no minimum watch time.
  - The old definition was renamed "engaged views" and moved to Advanced Mode.
  - YPP eligibility and revenue still use engaged views.
  - **Implication:** raw view counts include fast swipes, so viewed/swiped (or engaged views ÷ views) is the real hook metric.
  — [TechCrunch, 26 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Tubefilter, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/)
  - **[unverified]** A third-party claim says raw Shorts views jumped 20-30% overnight. — [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- **Meta / Facebook (2016, old):**
  - People spend 1.7 s per piece of content in the mobile News Feed vs. 2.5 s on desktop.
  - Of people who watch the first 3 s of a Facebook video, 65% watch at least 10 s and 45% reach 30 s.
  - Facebook's study of more than 850 video ads (Q4 2014-Q4 2015), "Capturing Attention in Feed," had raters watch the first 10 s with sound off. Facebook recommended putting the key message up front, without relying on audio.
  - Separate research cited by Facebook found mobile feed content recalled at a statistically significant rate after 0.25 s of exposure (Fors Marsh), and video ad recall at 1 s (Nielsen).
  — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818); [Beet.TV, Feb 2016](https://beet.tv/2016/02/br16facebookduggan.html); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/socialtimes/facebook-iq-salesbrain-zestxlabs-mobile-tv-ads/623005)
- **Instagram Reels (Mosseri, Jan 2025, via secondary sources):**
  - The three most important ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach.
  - Likes are "slightly more important for connected content," and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content.
  - Instagram publishes no early-retention cutoff. The widely repeated "1.7 seconds" for Reels traces to the 2016 Facebook feed figure, not to Reels research.
  — [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers); [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm/) (secondary; Mosseri's original posts not retrieved)
- **TikTok (official, ads-focused):**
  - "90% of ad recall impact is captured within the first six seconds" (TikTok Creative Guide 2020, "Value of View" analysis).
  - "Faster scene changes typically draw viewers in early" (Neuro-Insight Global Information Density study, 2022). No effect size is given.
  — [TikTok for Business, "Creative best practices for top-performing ads"](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads) (post undated; cited studies 2020-2022)
- **[vendor, unverified]** "63% of the highest-CTR TikTok videos hook within the first 3 seconds" is widely attributed to TikTok for Business, but I found no primary TikTok source. — e.g., [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/importance-of-tiktok-ad-hooks-first-3-seconds/)
- **[vendor, paid ads only]** Meta-ads "hold rate" (ThruPlays ÷ 3-s plays) bands are 30-35%, 40-50%, and 60%+. These are not organic Reels figures. — [Attribuly](https://attribuly.com/blogs/facebook-ads-hold-rate-shopify-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- For Shorts, about 70%+ "viewed" is a reasonable stretch target and 50-60% is common. Compare against your own Shorts of similar length and topic.
- On every feed platform, the decision window is about 1-3 s. The 2016 Meta data (65% of 3-s viewers reach 10 s) suggests that once the first few seconds are won, attrition slows sharply.

### Gaps
- There is no verifiable, organic, platform-published 3-second hold benchmark for TikTok, Reels or Shorts.
- Socialinsider and OpusClip reports with hold-rate figures were not found or verified.
- TikTok's "2-second" and "6-second" view metrics: no current benchmark found.

## 3. Which visual choices in the first frames measurably help?

### Takeaway
The best evidence is from ad research:
- Put the key message in the first 1-6 s.
- Design for sound-off viewing with text and captions, but use sound for its uplift.
- Faster scene changes help early attention.
- Surprise reduces early zapping, while joy sustains viewing.
- The opening must match the title and thumbnail.

Quantified effects for "motion in frame 1," faces vs. no faces, or cuts per second in organic creator content are scarce. Most numbers are self-reported surveys or vendor claims.

### Cited findings
- **Title/thumbnail consistency (YouTube official):** A high Intro score may mean the opening matched the thumbnail and title. For a low score, YouTube's first suggestion is to change the thumbnail and title to better reflect the video. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)
- **[vendor] vidIQ** links its 38.1% video (vs. 67.9% on the same channel) to the same failure modes: a hook that doesn't match the title or thumbnail, no value promise within about 15 s, and branded cold opens. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)
- **Message up front, sound-off design (Meta, 2016):** Facebook's analysis of 850+ ads, judged with the first 10 s on mute, led it to recommend key messaging up front, ideally without relying on audio. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818)
- **Captions (Verizon Media + Publicis Media, Apr 2019, survey of 5,600+ US consumers, self-reported):**
  - 80% said they are more likely to finish a video when captions are available.
  - 69% watch with sound off in public.
  - 80% of caption users are not hearing-impaired.
  — [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/digital/video-108656/page/2?et_blog); [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)
- **Sound on TikTok:** 88% of TikTok users say sound is vital to the experience (TikTok Marketing Science / Kantar, 2022, self-reported). — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- **Text overlays on TikTok:**
  - Overlays that get people to read "increase view time, drive recall, and make ads more likable" (TikTok/Lumen 2021). No effect size is published.
  - 71% of ads analysed used text overlays (a coding of about 4,000 ads with Kantar, 2021).
  — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads); [TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- **Pacing:** "Faster scene changes typically draw viewers in early" (TikTok, citing Neuro-Insight 2022). — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- **Emotion trajectory (academic, eye-tracking plus zapping):** Teixeira, Wedel & Pieters (2012), *Journal of Marketing Research* 49(2):144-159.
  - Data: 28 internet video ads, 50 viewers, measured every 250 ms.
  - Surprise mainly reduces zapping momentarily but distracts attention.
  - Joy's effect is asymmetric, with bigger gains from increases than losses from decreases.
  - Joy should build over time and end strong.
  — [Tilburg University record](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/emotion-induced-engagement-in-internet-video-ads/); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/creating-online-ads-we-want-to-watch)
- **Faces and handwriting motion (MOOC context):** Talking-head insertions beat slides alone, and the "natural motion of human handwriting" in Khan-style drawing beats static rendered text (see Section 5). — [Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Result first:** Show the payoff, or a striking visual of it, within about 1-3 s on feeds and within about 5-15 s on long-form. The same frame should visibly match the thumbnail's subject.
- **Captions:** Burned-in captions or key-phrase text help sound-off viewers. On TikTok, voice and music still matter.
- **Movement:** Movement and scene change in the first seconds probably help early attention. Use surprise to stop the scroll, and an escalating payoff to hold viewers.

### Gaps
- No rigorous public data on cuts per second vs. retention for organic creator videos. The Neuro-Insight study is cited without effect sizes.
- No quantified organic evidence on faces vs. no faces in frame 1 for Shorts, Reels or TikTok. Face-in-thumbnail claims exist but were not verified in this pass.
- No controlled data on "motion in frame 1" vs. a static first frame, or on high-contrast first frames.

## 4. What does YouTube officially say about intros?

### Takeaway
YouTube's official guidance is the Help-center Intro metric and its advice: align the opening with the title and thumbnail, move compelling moments earlier, and experiment with the first 30 s. I could not retrieve a primary-source quote from Todd Beaupré or Creator Insider on intros in this pass.

### Cited findings
- **Help-center guidance:**
  - Intro measures the share of viewers still watching at 30 s.
  - If top moments occur late, move compelling content earlier, since audiences usually shrink over a video.
  - For low intro retention, change the thumbnail and title to reflect the content, or modify the opening 30 s and experiment with styles.
  — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)
- **Shorts views vs. engaged views (Mar 2025):** YouTube kept the "chose to keep watching" definition as engaged views, the basis for monetization. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)

### Inferences
- YouTube's framing treats intro loss mostly as an expectation mismatch with the title and thumbnail. That mismatch is something the creator can fix with packaging as well as with the edit.

### Gaps
- No direct, dated quotes from Todd Beaupré, Rene Ritchie or the Creator Insider channel about intros or hooks were found; searches returned only third-party blogs. A follow-up should search Creator Insider video transcripts or the "Creator Insider" and "YouTube Liaison" X/YouTube posts.
- YouTube Creator Academy and Creators-site course pages on openings were not retrieved.

## 5. Guidance for animated (no face, voice-over) explainer videos, and data on educational content

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is Guo, Kim & Rubin (2014), which analysed 6.9M edX video sessions (older but still the canonical study). Its findings:
- Median engagement is at most about 6 minutes regardless of video length.
- Khan-style hand-drawn tutorials beat slide or code screencasts.
- Faster, enthusiastic speech is more engaging.
- Tutorials get only 2-3 minutes of engagement.

For a faceless animated explainer, the closest analogue is continuous drawn or animated motion with an energetic narrator. Talking heads help, but animated handwriting motion is also engaging.

### Cited findings (all from [Guo, Kim & Rubin, "How Video Production Affects Student Engagement," ACM Learning@Scale 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf), text extracted from the PDF)
- **Dataset:** 6.9M sessions across 4 courses (MIT 6.00x, Harvard PH207x, Berkeley CS188.1x, MIT 3.091x). The analysis uses 1x-speed plays, which were 76% of sessions.
- **Length:**
  - "Median engagement time is at most 6 minutes, regardless of total video length."
  - Students "often make it less than halfway through videos longer than 9 minutes."
  - The shortest videos (0-3 min) had the highest engagement and much less variance.
  - Recommendation: segment videos into chunks shorter than 6 min.
- **Talking head:** Students usually engaged more with videos that alternated between the instructor's talking head and slides or code. Recommendation: in post-production, show the instructor's head at opportune times. Producers credited the face with a more "intimate and personal" feel that broke up the monotony of slides.
- **Personal feel:** 6.00x (instructor at an office desk, direct eye contact) got nearly 2x the engagement of PH207x (TV studio, podium) for 6-12 min videos, and nearly 3x for videos over 12 min. Recommendation: informal settings, not big-budget studios.
- **Khan-style drawing:**
  - Khan-style tablet-drawing tutorials were more engaging than PowerPoint or code screencasts.
  - The authors cite the "natural motion of human handwriting" as more engaging than static computer-rendered fonts.
  - Recommendation: if slides must be shown, sketch over them with a tablet.
- **Speaking rate:**
  - Rates ranged from 48 to 254 wpm (mean 156, SD 31).
  - Engagement usually rose up to 2x with speaking rate.
  - For 6-12 min videos, the mid bucket (145-165 wpm) was least engaging.
  - Even 254 wpm was understandable when information was also shown visually.
  - Recommendation: coach enthusiasm; instructors need not slow down.
- **Lectures vs. tutorials:** Students engaged with tutorials for only 2-3 minutes regardless of length; tutorials are rewatched and skimmed. Recommendation: for lectures, focus on the first-watch experience.
- **Limitations (authors):** Watch time is a proxy for engagement, and the data are observational and historical.
- **Feed context for faceless voice-over content:** The Meta (2016) and Verizon/Publicis (2019) findings in Section 3 support burning in captions or key text, so a voice-over explainer still works sound-off. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818); [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/digital/video-108656/page/2?et_blog)
- **[vendor] Educational long-form targets:** vidIQ targets about 30% APV for a 6-9 min educational video and at least 60% at 30 s. — [vidIQ (Jun 2026)](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)

### Inferences (for animated, no-face, voice-over science explainers)
- **Visuals:** Replace the "talking head" pattern interrupt with visual state changes, such as a new diagram, a zoom or a reveal. Use "drawn-on" animation (strokes appearing, as in Khan-style handwriting) rather than static slide text.
- **Narration:** Fast, energetic narration (about 165-250 wpm) is likely fine when every claim is shown visually. Avoid flat mid-tempo delivery.
- **Opening:** Lead with the visual payoff or a surprising question matching the thumbnail within about 5-15 s on long-form, and within 1-3 s on Shorts or Reels. Keep the intro free of logos.
- **Length:** For feed or Shorts cuts, keep self-contained segments short. For long-form, structure in chapters of about 6 min or less.
- **Sound-off:** Burn in captions or key terms.

### Gaps
- No platform-published data specific to animated or faceless explainers on YouTube, TikTok or Reels was found.
- Kim et al. 2014 ("Understanding in-video dropouts and interaction peaks," L@S 2014) was not retrieved in this pass; it covers in-video dropout and interaction peaks in MOOCs and is worth a follow-up.
- YouTube Creator Academy or Creators-site guidance on openings for educational channels was not retrieved.
- No data found on faceless vs. face channels' Intro retention.
