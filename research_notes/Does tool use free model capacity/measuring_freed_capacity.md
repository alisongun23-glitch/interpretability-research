# Measuring whether a language model has "freed capacity" after a skill is offloaded to a tool

Setting these notes are written for: a ~1.4M-parameter GPT (few layers, context 64) trained ~7,000 steps in ~30 min on a laptop CPU on TinyStories-style text mixed with small arithmetic word problems. Condition A ("tool") writes `<calc> 12 + 5 = 17 </calc>` and an external calculator fills in the answer, whose tokens are excluded from the loss. Condition B ("no calls") must write the answer itself. Present observation: story perplexity 15.66 (tool) vs 15.33 (no tool) at equal steps, about 0.1 apart at equal story words; the no-call stream has fewer tokens per problem (no call chips), so the two models see different numbers of story words per step.

All cost estimates below are for models of 0.5–10M parameters on a laptop CPU; where a method was only demonstrated at much larger scale, that is stated.

---

## Key question 1: Fair comparison protocols — matched parameters, data, compute and loss masking; which tokens should count in the perplexity

### Takeaway
The published precedents that insert non-loss tokens into a training stream (pause tokens, RETRO neighbours) compute the reported loss only over the real text tokens and match on *total* tokens, while explicitly acknowledging that the augmented model then sees fewer real tokens; precedents that insert calculator calls (GSM8K annotations, Calc-X, Toolformer) mostly keep all tokens in the loss and evaluate "tool off" perplexity on plain text. For the user's question the cleanest comparison is: identical held-out plain-story set, identical tokenizer and context, per-token loss averaged over story tokens only, compared at matched counts of story tokens seen in training (not matched steps).

### Cited Findings
- Toolformer fine-tunes GPT-J (6.7B) on the API-augmented corpus "using a standard language modeling objective"; the paper does not state any masking of API-call or API-result tokens. Its language-modeling check is done with API calls *disabled* on plain text: WikiText perplexity GPT-J 9.9, GPT-J+CC 10.3, Toolformer (disabled) 10.3; CCNet 10.6 / 10.5 / 10.5, and the authors conclude training on the augmented data "does not lead to an increase in perplexity" relative to training on the same plain text. Tool-use benefits "only emerge at around 775M parameters" in a GPT-2 sweep (124M, 355M, 775M, 1.6B); smaller models perform similarly with and without tools. — [Toolformer, arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1)
- Toolformer's filtering criterion weights only the next ~5 tokens after a call (unnormalized weights 1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2, then 0) when deciding whether a call is useful: a call is kept if the loss reduction on those following tokens exceeds a threshold (τ_f = 1.0 by default; 0.5 for the calculator). — [Toolformer, arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1)
- Pause tokens (Goyal et al. 2023): `<pause>` tokens are inserted "randomly at 10% of the sequence length (2048) positions" in pretraining, the sequence is trimmed back to its original length, and the loss is computed "ignoring any loss term that corresponds to predicting the pause tokens themselves" (pause tokens are context, never targets). Models: 130M and 1B decoder-only, pretrained on C4 for 200B tokens in both the standard and the pause variant, i.e. matched on *total* tokens; the paper notes the pause-pretrained model therefore sees only about 90% of the meaningful pretraining tokens. No perplexity comparison is reported. 1B results: SQuAD EM +18%, CommonSenseQA up to +8%, GSM8K 8.5% vs 7.5%. Pause-finetuning a standard-pretrained model gives mixed results (gains on ~5 benchmarks, drops on others including GSM8K). — [Goyal et al., arXiv:2310.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)
- Filler tokens (Pfau, Merrill, Bowman 2024): a 4-layer, 384-wide, 6-head, ~34M-parameter Llama trained from scratch on 3SUM / 2SUM-Transform. "The input vectors have masked labels and so do not contribute to the loss"; the filler dots are one-hot labelled, so filler positions appear to contribute to the loss (not spelled out). 3SUM length 12: no-filler 66% (near random) vs 100% with fillers; 2SUM: chain-of-thought 95.1%, filler 93.6%, no intermediate tokens 78.7%, majority baseline 63%. "Learning to use filler tokens is difficult and requires specific, dense supervision to converge"; training only on filler sequences stayed near the no-filler baseline (~71%); Claude 2 and GPT-3.5 "achieve the same performance with filler tokens" as without. — [Pfau et al., arXiv:2404.15758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15758)
- Quiet-STaR (Mistral 7B): thought tokens are not trained by NLL at all; their generation is trained with REINFORCE (reward = change in likelihood of true future tokens; negative rewards excluded), and a separate NLL loss is applied to the *mixed* next-token prediction (post-thought and base predictions combined by a mixing head). Average perplexity changes are described as small while hard tokens improve disproportionately (Figure 5); no numeric perplexity is given. Training used 8×80GB H100s and "results in a substantial overhead". — [Zelikman et al., arXiv:2403.09629](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09629v1)
- RETRO: the training objective (Eq. 1) sums the log-likelihood over the input tokens only, conditioned on retrieved neighbours; neighbours are context and are never predicted. Evaluation handles leakage by removing training documents with 13-gram MinHash Jaccard ≥ 0.8 to any validation/test document, and by reporting bits-per-byte restricted to evaluation chunks whose longest common substring with their 10 nearest training neighbours is below a threshold α ("Retro models exploit leakage more strongly than baseline models, as indicated by the more negative slope"; "Retro outperforms baseline models at all leakage levels, down to α=12.5%"). — [Borgeaud et al., arXiv:2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04426v3)
- GSM8K calculator annotations (Cobbe et al. 2021): "we train all models to use a calculator by injecting calculation annotations into the training set"; in training there is no special treatment of annotated tokens ("they are all just tokens"), the model minimizes "the cross-entropy loss over all training tokens"; at test time the calculator overwrites "the token(s) directly following '=' and within <<…>>". Models: GPT-3 family, 6B and 175B. Fixing a calculator bug "improves verification test performance by about 1%". — [Cobbe et al., arXiv:2110.14168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)
- Calc-X / Calcformers (EMNLP 2023): format `<gadget id="calculator">32-3-2</gadget><output>27</output>`; training is "standard sequence-to-sequence training with cross-entropy loss on all tokens" (calculator outputs included in the loss); at inference the calculator's output is appended after the closing gadget tag. T5-Large (700M) GSM8K 17.0 → 34.2 with the calculator; T5-XL (3B) 19.2 → 39.6; Flan-XL 24.2 → 39.4; "Calcformers surpass the accuracy of the baseline models 2-3 times" except AQuA-RAT. Smallest model tested is 700M. — [Kadlčík et al., arXiv:2305.15017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15017)
- TinyStories evaluation: the paper reports "While all the evaluation scores are consistently increasing with the decrease of evaluation loss" and "GPT-4 evaluation scores increase as evaluation losses decrease", i.e. at this scale held-out loss tracks quality; context length 512 with attention window 256, GPT-Neo tokenizer trimmed to 10K tokens; models "can be trained on a single V100 GPU within at most 30 hours". — [Eldan & Li, arXiv:2305.07759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)

### Inferences
- The user's "tool" condition is structurally the pause-token / RETRO situation (answer tokens are context but not targets), not the Cobbe / Calc-X situation (answer tokens in the loss). The published practice for the former is: report loss only over real text tokens, state the fraction of non-loss tokens, and match on total tokens while acknowledging the real-token deficit. The pause paper's "sees only ~90% of meaningful tokens" is exactly the user's confound in reverse (the no-call model here sees *more* story words per step).
- "Perplexity on plain stories" should be computed, for both models, as exp of the mean per-token NLL over the *same* held-out set of pure stories (no problems, no chips), with the same tokenizer, the same 64-token windows and the same stride, excluding padding and any special tokens; this mirrors Toolformer's "API calls disabled on WikiText/CCNet" check. Averaging per token (not per sequence) matters because sequence lengths differ between the streams.
- Compare at matched cumulative *story tokens seen* rather than matched steps: log the count of loss-bearing story tokens per step for each run and evaluate at checkpoints matched on that count (the user already did this informally: 0.33 PPL at equal steps shrinks to 0.1 PPL at equal story words). An alternative that makes the streams identical is to pad the no-call stream with the same number of loss-masked filler tokens as the call chips occupy, so both models see identical story words per step; Pfau et al. suggest a from-scratch model will not exploit such fillers for computation without dense supervision, but this is a known potential confound and should be stated.
- A third control condition isolates "having to learn arithmetic" from stream differences: condition C = the no-call token stream (answer written in text, no chips) but with the answer tokens masked from the loss, like the tool condition. B vs C then measures the capacity cost of learning arithmetic on an identical token stream; A vs C measures the cost of the chip tokens and the longer sequences. This is the cleanest two-factor design and costs one extra run per seed and size.
- Matched parameters are straightforward (same architecture); the tool model's vocabulary must include the chip tokens, so the embedding/unembedding rows for those tokens should be present in both models (unused in the no-call model) so parameter counts are identical.

### Gaps
- Toolformer never states whether API result tokens are masked; the paper only says "standard language modeling objective". I found no paper that reports a clean "same model, same data, answer-in-loss vs answer-masked" perplexity comparison.
- No published precedent was found for comparing two pretraining runs at matched counts of a *subset* of tokens (e.g., matched story tokens); the pause paper is the closest (matched total tokens, acknowledged 10% real-token deficit).

## Key question 2: Scaling curves and "effective parameter count" as the capacity measure

### Takeaway
The standard way to turn "freed capacity" into a number is to train both conditions at several sizes, fit a loss-vs-parameters curve per condition, and read off the horizontal shift (how many parameters the tool is "worth"); RETRO, routed-model scaling laws (EPC), Davidson's compute-equivalent gain and Hernandez's effective-data-transferred are the published versions of this idea. At 0.5–10M parameters this is feasible in hours on a laptop, but the detectable shift is bounded by seed noise (see Key question 8).

### Cited Findings
- RETRO trained baseline/Retro pairs at 132M/172M, 368M/425M, 1.3B/1.45B and 7.0B/7.5B parameters; "Retro provides a constant gain for models ranging from 150M to 7B parameters" (Figure 1 caption) and "we observe that improvements do not diminish as we scale the models"; "Retro 7.5B outperforms Jurassic-1 and Gopher on a majority of the test sets" (it underperforms on dm_mathematics and ubuntu_irc). The abstract phrases this as "comparable performance to GPT-3 and Jurassic-1 on the Pile, despite using 25× fewer parameters". — [Borgeaud et al., arXiv:2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04426v3); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)
- Effective Parameter Count (Clark et al., ICML 2022): fit a bilinear law log L(N,E) = a·log N + b·log E + c·log N·log E + d (with a saturating transform on E), then define the EPC N̄ as the size of a dense model with the same loss as a routed model with N dense parameters and E experts: N̄ = N^(α(Ê)/α(E_start)) · (Ê/E_start)^(b/α(E_start)), α(E) = a + c·log E; fitted (s-base) a = −0.082, b = −0.108, c = 0.009, d = 1.104, E_start = 1.847, E_max = 314.478, valid only at the 130B-token budget. "Scaling in terms of N̄ results in a unifying power law: valid for dense and routed language models alike." Dense sizes 15M–1.3B, experts 2–512, "168 different models, including dense baselines", each trained 250,000 steps (~130B tokens). — [Clark et al., arXiv:2202.01169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01169)
- Compute-equivalent gain (Davidson et al. 2023): the CEG of an enhancement is the ratio C'/C of training compute needed, without the enhancement, to match the enhanced model's performance, both trained compute-optimally; it is relative to a model family and benchmark-specific ("a CEG of 5 for a particular benchmark doesn't necessarily imply that the post-training enhancement is as useful overall as increasing the training compute by 5×"); surveyed enhancements typically give CEG 5×–30×. — [Davidson et al., arXiv:2312.07413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07413)
- Chinchilla's parametric fit L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β with E = 1.69, A = 406.4, B = 410.7, α = 0.34, β = 0.28, from "over 400 language models" of 70M to over 16B parameters on 5B to over 400B tokens; the three estimation approaches agree that "the model size and the number of training tokens should be scaled equally"; the IsoFLOP method reports "a clear valley in loss" per compute budget and uses points within the last 15% of each run. — [Hoffmann et al., arXiv:2203.15556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)
- Effective data transferred (Hernandez et al. 2021): D_T is "the amount of additional fine-tuning data that a model of the same size trained only on fine-tuning data would have needed to achieve the same loss on fine-tuning data as a model trained purely on pre-training data"; in the low-data regime it follows D_T = k(D_F)^α(N)^β; "pre-training effectively multiplies the fine-tuning dataset size". — [Hernandez et al., arXiv:2102.01293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293)
- TinyStories trained 1M, 2.5M, 8.3M, 21M (1 layer), 28M and 33M models (hidden 64–1024, 1–8 layers); GPT-4 grades on the "Lucy" prompt (grammar/creativity/consistency): 1M-8L 6/3/2, 2.5M-8L 5/6/3, 8.3M-8L 7/5/5, 28M-8L 9/6/9, 21M-1L 8/3/7, 33M-2L 7/6/4; "model depth is more important for keeping consistent with the content than for generating syntactically correct language". — [Eldan & Li, arXiv:2305.07759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)
- Toolformer's own size sweep found tool use "only emerges at around 775M parameters"; smaller GPT-2 models performed similarly with and without tools. — [Toolformer, arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1)
- Teaching arithmetic to small transformers: a 10.6M-parameter NanoGPT (6 layers, 6 heads, width 384, character-level) learns 3-digit addition almost perfectly from ~2,500 reversed-format examples (phase transition between ~1,000 and 4,000), while plain-format accuracy "plateaus at slightly over 85%" at 10,000 samples; a GPT-2-scale model with twice the layers/heads/width "outperforms in both plain and detailed scratchpad evaluations"; no length generalization beyond trained digit counts. — [Lee et al., arXiv:2307.03381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)

### Inferences
- Recipe at the user's scale: pick 4–6 widths (e.g., 0.5M, 1M, 1.4M, 2.5M, 5M, 10M; TinyStories shows large quality changes across 1M→8M→28M), train conditions A and B (and C if used) at each width, at matched story tokens, with 2–3 seeds; fit L_story(N) = E + A/N^α per condition (Chinchilla form with D fixed); report the "parameter-equivalent" gain as the factor by which N must grow in condition B to match condition A's story loss (the EPC / RETRO logic). With 30-min runs this is 24–54 runs, i.e. 12–27 laptop-hours sequentially, less with parallel runs.
- The parameter-equivalent shift is only meaningful if the loss gap exceeds the seed spread (Key question 8). With single-seed gaps of ~0.1 PPL at 1.4M, the shift may be within noise; the sweep across widths adds power because a consistent sign across sizes is itself evidence (a sign test over 5 widths with 2 seeds each is informative even if each point is not).
- Toolformer's finding that tool benefits emerge only at ~775M is about *learning to call tools from self-generated data*; it does not bear on whether a model that is *taught* to call (as here, with calls in the training text) saves capacity. Lee et al. show 10M-scale models can learn multi-digit addition internally with a few thousand examples, so the no-call model at 1.4M plausibly does spend capacity on arithmetic; whether that is a measurable fraction of its parameters is exactly the open question.
- "Compute-equivalent gain" is the better framing than "parameter-equivalent" if the user compares at equal wall-clock or equal steps, because the tool model spends forward/backward compute on chip tokens that the no-call model does not; both framings should be reported.

### Gaps
- No published scaling curve exists for tool-augmented vs non-tool models at < 100M parameters; RETRO's constant-gain result is the nearest analogue (retrieval, 150M–7B).
- Chinchilla's coefficients cannot be transported to 1.4M-parameter TinyStories models; the exponent α must be fitted locally and I found no published L(N) fit for TinyStories-scale models.

## Key question 3: Loss decomposition by token type

### Takeaway
Decomposing the loss by token type (story words, problem text, operands, answer digits, words after a problem) is cheap and is the quantity the published tool/pause papers implicitly report ("loss on language tokens only"); it also reveals *where* a gap comes from (e.g., tokens right after a problem, which Toolformer weights most heavily when judging a call's usefulness).

### Cited Findings
- Toolformer judges whether a call helps by the weighted loss on the ~5 tokens following the call site (weights 1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2) — i.e., the signal of a tool's value is concentrated in the tokens immediately after the call. — [Toolformer, arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1)
- Quiet-STaR reports that average perplexity changes are small while "difficult tokens improve disproportionately" (Figure 5), i.e. aggregate perplexity can hide a token-type-specific effect. — [Zelikman et al., arXiv:2403.09629](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09629v1)
- Pause tokens: the loss is taken only over real tokens; pause positions are context but never targets. — [Goyal et al., arXiv:2310.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)
- RETRO's Eq. 1 sums log-likelihood over input tokens only, never over retrieved neighbours. — [Borgeaud et al., arXiv:2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04426v3)
- Gurnee et al. use the *loss increase on a specific token class* (e.g., "an average loss increase of 8% per French sequence" when a context neuron is ablated in Pythia-70M, vs 0.2% in 6.9B) as the measurement of what a neuron does. — [Gurnee et al., arXiv:2305.01610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610)
- Signal and Noise (2025): bits-per-byte-style loss metrics have higher signal-to-noise than accuracy metrics on most benchmarks ("Most benchmarks have higher signal-to-noise ratio when using the BPB"; GSM8K SNR 1.2 → 7.0, MBPP 2.0 → 41.8), and loss-type metrics improved decision accuracy for 90% of benchmarks. — [Heineman et al., arXiv:2508.13144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144)

### Inferences
- A per-token-type table for the user's setting: (i) story tokens in pure stories (held-out, both models), (ii) problem-statement tokens, (iii) operand digits, (iv) answer digits (only the no-call model is trained on these; for the tool model this is the "tool-off" test of Key question 5), (v) the first 5 tokens after a problem's answer (Toolformer's window), (vi) story tokens ≥ 10 tokens after a problem. If the tool model's gain is real, it should appear in (i) and (vi); if the gap is driven by (v), it is a local "recovering from the problem" effect, not freed capacity.
- Report these in nats per token with the seed standard deviation, not as perplexities, because the per-type loss differences will be small (0.1 PPL at 15.5 PPL is ≈ 0.0065 nats per token) and SNR is higher for loss metrics.
- Cost: one forward pass over a held-out set; for a 1.4M model, ~2×1.4M FLOPs per token, so a million-token eval set is on the order of 10^12 FLOPs, roughly a minute on a laptop CPU (order-of-magnitude estimate, not a measured figure).

### Gaps
- I found no paper that reports the per-token-type loss decomposition for a tool-augmented model; this is standard interpretability practice (Gurnee-style) rather than a documented protocol.

## Key question 4: Pruning and ablation as capacity probes

### Takeaway
Head/neuron ablation and pruning curves are cheap at 1.4M parameters and have well-established precedents (Michel et al. heads; Gurnee neuron ablations; magnitude-pruning curves), but at small scale magnitude pruning is fragile and zero-ablation pushes the model off-distribution, so mean/resample ablation and a two-axis readout (story loss vs arithmetic loss per removed component) are the recommended form.

### Cited Findings
- Michel, Levy & Neubig 2019 (WMT transformer, BERT): "only 8 (out of 96) heads cause a statistically significant change in performance" when removed individually, "half of which actually result in a higher BLEU score"; "for most layers, one head is indeed sufficient at test time" (exception: the last encoder-decoder attention layer, where one head "degrades performance by at least 13.5 BLEU points"); greedy iterative pruning "allows us to prune up to 20% and 40% of heads from WMT and BERT" without noticeable impact, after which performance "drops sharply"; head importance is the expected sensitivity I_h = E_x |∂L(x)/∂ξ_h| ("equivalent to the Taylor expansion method from Molchanov et al. 2017"), and "the important heads are determined early (but not immediately) during the training process". — [Michel et al., arXiv:1905.10650](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10650)
- Magnitude pruning is fragile at small scale: on OPT-125M at 50% sparsity, magnitude pruning raises PTB perplexity from 38.99 (dense) to 276 (SparseGPT paper), with degradation shrinking at larger sizes. — [Frantar & Alistarh, SparseGPT, arXiv:2301.00774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00774)
- HORST (2026) one-shot magnitude-prunes GPT-2 Small per tensor at 10–60% sparsity without fine-tuning, reporting the gap to dense widening at higher sparsity (numbers not captured). — [HORST, arXiv:2605.21104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21104)
- Activation patching guidance (Heimersheim & Nanda 2024): patch activations from another run rather than zeroing ("We replace activations with other activations rather than zeroing them out"); denoising (clean→corrupt) shows sufficiency, noising (corrupt→clean) shows necessity; mean ablation is "slightly more principled than zero ablating"; noising/denoising "tend to bring the model less out-of-distribution than ablation techniques"; prefer logit difference ("we personally trust logit difference (or equivalently logprob difference) the most") because probability "overemphasises effects near a threshold and suppresses effects elsewhere"; backup components can "jump into action" when one is disrupted (Hydra effect). Attribution patching is mentioned as a "fast approximation" for large models. — [Heimersheim & Nanda, arXiv:2404.15255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255)
- Gurnee et al. (Pythia 70M–6.9B): k-sparse probing (k swept 256 → 1; optimal sparse probing is NP-hard and "only feasible for smaller values of k"; mean-difference prefiltering "for the sake of computational efficiency"); "the first 25% of fully connected layers employ substantially more superposition than the rest"; higher-level contextual features are "seemingly encoded by monosemantic neurons, predominantly in middle layers"; "as models increase in size, representation sparsity increases on average" but "the smallest models seem to actually implement greater sparsity" for some simple features; ablating one French-context neuron raises loss by 8% per French sequence in 70M but only 0.2% in 6.9B. — [Gurnee et al., arXiv:2305.01610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610)
- Dohare et al. count "dead units" as ReLU units that output zero for all examples in a sample of 2,000 images and report "up to 25% of units die after 800 tasks" at step size 0.01. — [Dohare et al., arXiv:2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)

### Inferences
- Design for the user's model: for every head and every MLP neuron (a few layers × a few heads; a few hundred to a few thousand neurons), mean-ablate it and record (Δ story loss, Δ arithmetic loss) on held-out sets. Plot the two deltas against each other: neurons/heads that matter for arithmetic but not stories are the "arithmetic budget". The hypothesis predicts the no-call model has a larger such set and that the tool model's components are more uniformly story-relevant. Cost: one eval pass per component; with ~1,000 components and a ~1-minute eval this is ~hours, which can be cut by using a smaller eval set (~100k tokens) or by attribution-patching-style gradient scores (Michel's I_h) computed in one backward pass.
- A pruning *curve* ("how much can be removed before story loss rises") should use importance-ranked structured pruning (Michel's greedy procedure with gradient scores) rather than global magnitude pruning, because magnitude pruning at ~100M scale already explodes perplexity at 50% sparsity and will be worse at 1.4M. Compare the two conditions by the sparsity at which story loss rises by a fixed amount (e.g., +0.01 nats); the tool model, if it has spare capacity, should tolerate *more* removal on stories, or equivalently its removable fraction should be larger — note the opposite reading is also possible (freed capacity gets used for stories, making it *less* prunable), so this probe's sign must be interpreted alongside the scaling curve.
- Gurnee's results caution that at 70M-scale many features are in superposition in early layers; at 1.4M, "the fraction of neurons specialized for arithmetic" may be ill-defined because neurons are polysemantic. The ablation-delta scatter plot handles this gracefully (it does not assume monosemanticity); sparse probing for operand/result features (Key question 5) is the complementary tool.
- Dead-unit counts (Dohare's definition applied to GELU/ReLU MLP units on a sample of stories) are a near-free capacity indicator: a higher fraction of near-dead units in the tool model would be consistent with unused capacity.

### Gaps
- No published pruning or head-ablation curve exists for TinyStories-scale (1–10M) GPTs; the numbers above are from 60M–125M+ models.
- Gurnee et al. do not report the fraction of neurons specialized for arithmetic in any model; I found no paper quantifying that fraction at small scale.

## Key question 5: Probing for internal arithmetic and "use it or lose it" tests

### Takeaway
Linear probes for operand values and results in the residual stream are cheap at 1.4M parameters and have precedents in larger models (helix/Fourier representations of numbers); the key interpretive point is that the tool model would not *lose* arithmetic — it never had to learn to produce the answer — so the "tool-off" test measures whether the answer is nonetheless computed internally (as RETRO[OFF] and Toolformer-disabled measure for retrieval and tools).

### Cited Findings
- Kantamneni & Tegmark (ICLR 2025): in GPT-J, Pythia-6.9B and Llama-3.1-8B, numbers are represented as a generalized helix and addition is computed by rotating it (the "Clock" algorithm, previously proposed by Nanda et al.); MLP outputs, attention-head outputs and individual neuron preactivations are modelled with these helices and verified "with causal interventions"; Llama-3.1-8B reaches 98.0% on a,b ∈ [0,99]. — [arXiv:2502.00873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00873)
- Gurnee et al.'s k-sparse probing finds features that are linearly readable from few neurons; ablation effect sizes are much larger in small models (8% loss increase in 70M vs 0.2% in 6.9B for a context neuron). — [Gurnee et al., arXiv:2305.01610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610)
- RETRO without retrieval at evaluation ("Retro[OFF]") brings "limited performance degradation compared to baseline transformers" and "can be used without retrieval at evaluation and still achieve comparable performance to baseline models" (qualitative; no numbers in the text I read). — [Borgeaud et al., arXiv:2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04426v3)
- Toolformer evaluated with API calls disabled matches the plain-text-trained control on WikiText/CCNet (10.3 / 10.5 vs 10.3 / 10.5). — [Toolformer, arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1)
- Cobbe et al. train on calculator-annotated solutions with the loss on all tokens and at test time let the calculator overwrite the tokens after "="; the model therefore still learns to predict the answer digits in training. — [Cobbe et al., arXiv:2110.14168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)
- Calc-X includes calculator outputs in the loss and does not measure the models' internal arithmetic without the tool, only noting LMs "struggle with problems that require exact computations". — [Kadlčík et al., arXiv:2305.15017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15017)
- Lee et al.: a 10.6M NanoGPT learns 3-digit addition from ~2,500 reversed examples; plain format plateaus ~85% at 10k. — [Lee et al., arXiv:2307.03381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Activation-patching methodology (denoising/noising, logit-difference metric, backup heads). — [Heimersheim & Nanda, arXiv:2404.15255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255)

### Inferences
- Probe design at the user's scale: build a few thousand problems with known operands and results; record the residual stream at the position of the second operand, at "=" (or at the `</calc>` chip), and at the first answer token, in every layer; fit linear probes (ridge regression for the value, logistic for each digit) with a held-out split. Expected pattern under the hypothesis: both models encode the *operands* (the tool model needs them to write the call), but only the no-call model encodes the *result* before the answer position. Probe training is seconds on CPU; activation collection is one forward pass.
- "Use it or lose it" test: run the tool model with the calculator disabled and let it generate the digits after "=" itself. Because those positions were never loss targets, near-chance accuracy is the expected baseline and is not evidence of lost capacity; what is informative is (a) whether the result probe still reads out the sum (computed-but-not-emitted), and (b) whether a *brief* fine-tune on answer tokens (hundreds of steps) recovers arithmetic faster in the tool model than from scratch — which doubles as the transfer test of Key question 7. This is the analogue of RETRO[OFF] / Toolformer-disabled, with the caveat that those models were trained with retrieval/tool results in context and still had to predict the following text.
- A cheap causal check in the no-call model: mean-ablate the components identified by the probes or by the ablation scatter (Key question 4) and verify that arithmetic accuracy drops while story loss is flat; the same components in the tool model should have no arithmetic function. Use logit difference between the correct and a distractor digit as the metric, per Heimersheim & Nanda.

### Gaps
- No published probe study of arithmetic representations exists for models under ~100M parameters trained on mixed text + arithmetic; the helix/Clock results are from 6–8B models and modular-addition toy models. Whether a 1.4M model uses a helix, a lookup, or a digit-wise carry circuit is unknown.
- RETRO[OFF] numbers and the Toolformer-disabled downstream numbers would be the closest quantitative "tool-off" precedents; I only obtained the qualitative statements and the perplexity table.

## Key question 6: Intrinsic dimension, effective rank and Fisher-based parameter counting

### Takeaway
These are the most "capacity-flavoured" measures but the least validated as comparisons between two models at tiny scale: intrinsic dimension (d90) needs a sweep of fine-tuning runs and measures how compressible a downstream task is, effective rank of representations/weights is cheap and is a documented plasticity correlate, and Hessian/Fisher effective dimensionality is computable at 1.4M parameters but its interpretation as "used capacity" is contested.

### Cited Findings
- Aghajanyan et al. 2020: fine-tuning is reparameterized as θ_D = θ_0 + P(θ_d) with a Fastfood random projection (a dense projection for RoBERTa-Large at d = 1000 "would need 1.42 terabytes"); d90 is the smallest d reaching 90% of full fine-tuning performance and is called "necessarily crude". RoBERTa-Large reaches 90% of full fine-tuning on MRPC with ~200 parameters (SAID d90 = 207; QQP 774); BERT-Base 1608/1861 (MRPC, SAID/DID) and 8030/9295 (QQP); BERT-Large 1037/2493 and 1200/1389; RoBERTa-Base 896/1000 and 896/1389. "The intrinsic dimensionality of RoBERTa-Base monotonically decreases as we continue pre-training" (checkpoints of a 200k-step from-scratch run), and across public checkpoints "as the number of parameters increases, the intrinsic dimension of fine-tuning on MRPC decreases". Estimating d90 required ~100 fine-tuning runs over d ∈ [10, 10,000] (log scale), each with a small learning-rate search; a controlled size sweep was "computationally infeasible". — [Aghajanyan et al., arXiv:2012.13255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)
- Effective dimensionality via the Hessian (Maddox et al. 2020, "Rethinking Parameter Counting in Deep Models"): traces the idea to MacKay's Bayesian effective number of parameters, computed from the Hessian spectrum of the training loss, and finds effective dimensionality inversely related to posterior variance. — [arXiv:2003.02139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02139)
- Degeneracy-based effective parameter count (2024): defines an effective count as total parameters minus reparametrization freedoms, tied to the ranks of activation and Jacobian spaces over the training data. — [arXiv:2405.10927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10927)
- Effective rank definition used by Dohare et al.: erank(Φ) = exp(entropy of the normalized singular values) of the representation matrix, citing Roy & Vetterli 2007; "loss of plasticity is accompanied by a decrease in the average effective rank of the network", alongside rising dead-unit fraction and rising mean absolute weight. — [Dohare et al., arXiv:2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)
- Clark et al.'s EPC and Hernandez et al.'s D_T are the behavioural (loss-matched) alternatives to these structural measures (see Key question 2). — [Clark et al., arXiv:2202.01169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01169); [Hernandez et al., arXiv:2102.01293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293)

### Inferences
- At 1.4M parameters a d90 sweep is affordable (each fine-tune is minutes; a dense random projection of 1.4M × d floats is fine for d ≤ ~10^3 and Fastfood or sparse projections cover larger d), but the measure answers "how few directions are needed to adapt to task T", which Aghajanyan show *falls* with more pre-training and larger models. For the user's hypothesis the prediction is indirect: if the tool model has more general-purpose language structure, its d90 on a new language task should be *lower* than the no-call model's. Treat as exploratory; there is no precedent for using d90 to compare two equally sized pretrained models.
- Effective rank of (a) each weight matrix and (b) the residual-stream representation on a sample of story tokens is a seconds-level computation (SVD of 64×64 or 64×N matrices) and is directly analogous to Dohare's plasticity correlate. Together with dead-unit fraction and mean |weight|, it gives three cheap "is capacity in use?" indicators; the hypothesis predicts higher erank / fewer dead units in the model that uses its capacity for language, but the direction for "freed" capacity is ambiguous (unused capacity could show up as *low* rank). Report them, do not rely on them alone.
- Hessian/Fisher effective dimensionality (Maddox-style, top-k eigenvalues by Lanczos/power iteration on a 1.4M-parameter model) is feasible on CPU in minutes; but because both models have the same parameter count and the measure is data- and threshold-dependent, differences will be hard to interpret; it is better used as a secondary consistency check on the pruning curve.

### Gaps
- I found no study validating intrinsic dimension, effective rank or Fisher effective dimension as a *between-model* capacity-utilization comparison at < 10M parameters; reliability at tiny scale is unknown.
- Roy & Vetterli 2007 (effective rank) and RankMe-style representation-rank measures were not fetched directly; the definition above is as quoted by Dohare et al.

## Key question 7: Transfer and plasticity tests

### Takeaway
Fine-tuning both models on a new skill and comparing sample efficiency is the most direct behavioural test of spare capacity and has clean precedents (effective data transferred; continual-learning plasticity protocols with cheap CPU variants); the plasticity literature also supplies three cheap structural correlates (dead units, effective rank, weight norm) to report alongside.

### Cited Findings
- Hernandez et al.: D_T = extra from-scratch fine-tuning data a same-size model would need to match the pre-trained model's fine-tuning loss; D_T = k(D_F)^α(N)^β in the low-data regime; "pre-training effectively multiplies the fine-tuning dataset size"; an Alignment Forum summary notes that for text→Python, β ≈ 2α, so 100× more fine-tuning data ≈ 10× more model size. — [Hernandez et al., arXiv:2102.01293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293); [AF summary](https://www.alignmentforum.org/s/dT7CKGXwq9vt76CeX/p/85HgXZvNdTdfRJhar)
- Dohare et al. (Nature 2024 / arXiv 2306.13812): plasticity is measured as accuracy on each new task along a sequence (Continual ImageNet: binary tasks of 1,200 train / 200 test images, 2,000–5,000 tasks; Online Permuted MNIST: 800 tasks, 3×2000-unit MLP, also 100/1,000/10,000 units); "Slowly-Changing Regression" is "an even cheaper problem where we can run a single experiment on a CPU in 15 minutes"; correlates: dead units ("up to 25% of units die after 800 tasks"), falling effective rank, rising mean |weight|; continual backpropagation reinitializes a fraction ρ of low-utility units per step (example ρ = 10^-4 with 2,000 units "would mean replacing one unit in each layer after every 5 examples", maturity threshold m = 100) and "fully mitigates the loss of plasticity" on Continual ImageNet. — [Dohare et al., arXiv:2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812); [Amii summary](https://www.amii.ca/updates-insights/loss-of-plasticity)
- Pause tokens: pause-finetuning a model that was *not* pause-pretrained gives mixed results (gains on ~5 benchmarks, drops elsewhere incl. GSM8K), i.e. a capacity/compute affordance only pays off if the model was trained to use it. — [Goyal et al., arXiv:2310.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)
- Lee et al.: "fine-tuning a pretrained model results in better performance compared to training a model from scratch" for addition; 3-digit addition learned from ~2.5k reversed examples at 10.6M. — [Lee et al., arXiv:2307.03381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)
- Zhong et al. 2021 (BERT MINI 4L/256 to LARGE, 10 pretraining seeds): "when accuracy gains between sizes are small, most signals across different model sizes will be dominated by noise due to random seeds"; pretraining seeds change ~2% of individual predictions. — [Zhong et al. 2021](https://nlp.cs.berkeley.edu/pubs/Zhong-Ghosh-Klein-Steinhardt_2021_Instance_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- Transfer design at the user's scale: freeze nothing; fine-tune each pretrained model (both conditions, all seeds) on a new small language skill the pretraining data did not contain (e.g., a second story genre, a new instruction format, rhyming/story-ending prediction, or a second tool) with fine-tuning set sizes on a log grid (e.g., 100, 300, 1k, 3k, 10k examples); plot held-out fine-tuning loss vs D_F; report D_T-style "effective data" by horizontal shift. Each fine-tune is seconds–minutes; the full grid is ~an hour per condition-seed. The hypothesis predicts the tool model reaches a given loss with fewer examples.
- Also fine-tune on arithmetic answers *without* the tool (the "use it or lose it" recovery test of Key question 5): if the tool model learns to emit answers from fewer examples than a from-scratch model but more slowly than the no-call model, that quantifies how much arithmetic structure it retained from writing calls.
- Dohare's three correlates are free to compute on checkpoints and give a plasticity-side reading of the same capacity question (a network with many dead units and low effective rank adapts poorly). Because Dohare's effect appears over hundreds–thousands of tasks, single-task fine-tuning differences between the two models will be small; expect to need several seeds.

### Gaps
- No published experiment measures plasticity or transfer sample-efficiency as a function of whether a skill was offloaded to a tool; the inference from Hernandez/Dohare is by analogy.
- The Nature version of Dohare et al. could not be fetched (login redirect); quotes are from the arXiv version 2306.13812, whose numbers may differ slightly from the published one.

## Key question 8: Statistical rigor — seed variance, detectable differences, equal steps vs equal tokens

### Takeaway
Published seed spreads for small LMs are ≈0.046 PPL (0.0033 nats) at 5.5M parameters on TinyStories, ≈0.0027 nats at 50M, ≈0.005 nats at 124M, and up to 3× larger at higher learning rates or smaller batches; with 2–3 seeds per arm a difference must be roughly 1.6–2× the single-run standard deviation to be detectable, so the user's 0.1-PPL gap at matched story words is at the edge of detectability and the 0.33-PPL gap at equal steps is confounded by token count.

### Cited Findings
- Three-Phase Transformer (2026), 5.46M-parameter 4-layer d=192 TinyStories model, 5 init seeds, data seed fixed: "5-seed mean: 13.8500 PPL ± 0.046 (sample std), val_loss 2.6283 ± 0.0033", range 13.7940–13.9015, "Noise floor for any single-seed claim at this architecture and scale: 0.046 PPL std (1σ)"; a 0.02 PPL difference was judged "within seed noise". 123M on WikiText-103, 3 seeds: 16.1723 ± 0.0954 PPL vs 16.0945 ± 0.0800; paired difference +0.0778 PPL with SE 0.0929 (n=3), "statistically indistinguishable from zero". — [arXiv:2604.14430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14430)
- OpenEuroLLM scaling-law study: 50M model, 6B tokens, 5 seeds; total std across seeds 2.7·10⁻³ nats, finite-sampling contribution 6.3·10⁻⁴ on a 204,800-sequence validation set, seed-only std 2.67·10⁻³, "dominated by seed effects"; seed-to-seed variability "up to 3× larger for higher learning rate or smaller batch sizes"; standard error decreases with model size and token budget. — [arXiv:2608.28308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28308)
- modded-nanogpt (124M, FineWeb): target ~3.278 val loss "w/ up to 0.005 inter-run stddev"; record submissions must supply enough run logs for "a statistical significance level of p<0.01 that their average val loss is lower than 3.28". — [modded-nanogpt README](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt)
- Zhong et al. 2021 (BERT MINI–LARGE, 10 pretraining seeds): standard deviation of overall accuracy across pretraining seeds is ~40× smaller than the gap induced by fine-tuning seeds, yet "when accuracy gains between sizes are small, most signals across different model sizes will be dominated by noise due to random seeds". — [Zhong et al. 2021](https://nlp.cs.berkeley.edu/pubs/Zhong-Ghosh-Klein-Steinhardt_2021_Instance_paper.pdf)
- Signal and Noise (2025): 20 1B models (10 init seeds, 10 data-order seeds); checkpoint-to-checkpoint noise over the last n checkpoints is a practical proxy for seed noise (R² 0.82 / 0.86 vs seed and data-order noise); n = 9 checkpoints gives 95% confidence the sample std is within 1σ of the truth, n = 20 within 0.2σ for 34/39 benchmarks; BPB/loss metrics have higher SNR than accuracies. — [Heineman et al., arXiv:2508.13144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144)
- Pause tokens matched total tokens (200B) and noted the pause model saw only ~90% real tokens — a documented instance of the equal-tokens vs equal-real-tokens issue. — [Goyal et al., arXiv:2310.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)
- Chinchilla's loss form separates a data term B/D^β (β = 0.28) from a capacity term A/N^α, which is the standard way to convert a token-count difference into an expected loss offset (coefficients are for 70M+ models on MassiveText and must be refitted). — [Hoffmann et al., arXiv:2203.15556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)

### Inferences
- Power arithmetic: with per-run std σ and n seeds per arm, the standard error of the difference of means is σ·√(2/n): 1.0σ for n = 2, 0.82σ for n = 3, 0.63σ for n = 5. A two-sided ~95% criterion (≈2 SE) therefore needs a true gap of ≈2σ (n=2), ≈1.6σ (n=3) or ≈1.3σ (n=5). Using the 5.5M TinyStories σ ≈ 0.046 PPL (0.0033 nats) as the best available proxy, 3 seeds per arm detect ≈0.075 PPL (≈0.005 nats); 5 seeds detect ≈0.06 PPL. The user's 1.4M model is smaller and trained for fewer tokens than that reference, and OpenEuroLLM's finding that σ grows with smaller batches/higher LR suggests the user's σ could be larger; it must be measured (3–5 seeds of one baseline configuration, ~2 laptop-hours).
- The user's observed gaps: 15.66 vs 15.33 is ≈0.021 nats (≈6σ of the 5.5M reference) but is confounded by the story-token deficit; the 0.1 PPL at matched story words is ≈0.0065 nats (≈2σ) and, with one seed per arm, is not conclusive in either direction.
- Cheap variance reducers: (i) average the loss over the last ~5–10 checkpoints of each run (Signal and Noise's checkpoint-noise proxy), (ii) use a large held-out set (≥ 10⁵ story tokens) so finite-sample noise is well below seed noise, (iii) pair runs by seed (same init seed and data order for A and B) so the comparison is paired, (iv) report nats/token rather than perplexity, (v) fix the learning rate schedule to end at the same point for all runs.
- Equal steps vs equal tokens: the no-call stream has more story words per step, so at equal steps the no-call model is ahead on the data term; comparing at equal story tokens removes that but then the tool model has taken more optimizer steps and more compute. Report both, plus the identical-stream control (padding or condition C from Key question 1), and fit a local L(D_story) from the training trajectories to translate residual token mismatches into an expected loss offset.

### Gaps
- No measured seed spread exists for ~1.4M-parameter, 64-context models at 7k steps; the 5.5M TinyStories figure is the nearest published number and may understate the spread at the user's scale and batch size.
- Signal and Noise does not report absolute seed std for its smallest models, and it gives no rule for how many *training seeds* are needed; the n = 9 / n = 20 guidance is for checkpoints.

## Recommended laptop-scale design (synthesis)

### Takeaway
A sound, cheap design is a three-condition (tool / no-call / no-call-with-masked-answer), multi-width (4–5 sizes), multi-seed (3) sweep evaluated on an identical held-out plain-story set at matched story tokens, reported as story-only nats/token with seed error bars and a fitted loss-vs-parameters curve per condition; followed by cheap structural and behavioural probes (per-token-type loss, ablation scatter, operand/result probes, transfer sample-efficiency) on the checkpoints already produced.

### Cited Findings
- Loss only over real tokens, matched on tokens, deficit stated (pause tokens; RETRO). — [Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226); [Borgeaud et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04426v3)
- Tool-disabled plain-text perplexity as the language-ability check. — [Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1)
- Parameter-equivalence via loss-matched curves (EPC; RETRO constant gain; CEG). — [Clark et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01169); [Borgeaud et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.04426v3); [Davidson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07413)
- Seed spread ≈0.046 PPL / 0.0033 nats at 5.5M on TinyStories (5 seeds). — [arXiv:2604.14430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14430)
- Loss-type metrics have higher SNR; checkpoint averaging as a noise proxy. — [Heineman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144)
- Mean/resample ablation, logit-difference metric, backup-head caveat. — [Heimersheim & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255)
- Gradient-based head importance and greedy pruning curve. — [Michel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10650)
- Effective data transferred as the transfer metric; dead units / effective rank / weight norm as plasticity correlates with a 15-minute CPU protocol. — [Hernandez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293); [Dohare et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)
- 10M-scale models learn multi-digit addition from a few thousand examples. — [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)

### Inferences
- Step 0 (≈2 laptop-hours): measure the seed noise floor — 4 seeds of the current 1.4M no-call configuration, identical data order; report std of story nats/token at the final checkpoint and averaged over the last 5 checkpoints. Everything else is interpreted relative to this σ.
- Step 1, main experiment (≈18–45 laptop-hours sequential, far less with parallel runs): conditions A (tool, answer masked), B (no-call, answer in loss), C (no-call stream, answer masked); widths ≈ 0.5M, 1M, 1.4M, 2.5M, 5M (and 10M if affordable); 3 seeds, paired across conditions; identical held-out plain-story set; evaluate at matched cumulative story tokens (log them) and also at matched steps; metric = story nats/token (per-token mean), with per-token-type breakdown. Fit L = E + A/N^α per condition; report the parameter-equivalent factor with a bootstrap over seeds; also report the sign consistency across widths. B − C isolates the arithmetic burden on an identical stream; A − C isolates the chip-token cost; A − B is the headline.
- Step 2, structural probes on the Step-1 checkpoints (minutes each): dead-unit fraction, effective rank of residual representations and weight matrices, mean |weight|; mean-ablation scatter (Δ story loss vs Δ arithmetic loss per head and neuron, using a ~100k-token eval set); gradient-importance pruning curve on story loss.
- Step 3, representational probes (minutes): linear probes for operand values and the result at the "=" / chip position across layers in both models; causal check by mean-ablating the probe-identified components with a logit-difference metric.
- Step 4, behavioural transfer (≈1–2 hours per condition): fine-tune each checkpoint on (a) a new language skill and (b) arithmetic answers without the tool, over a log grid of example counts; report effective-data shifts (D_T) and the number of examples to reach a target loss.
- Reporting: every number as mean ± seed std with n; a difference is claimed only if it exceeds ≈2·σ·√(2/n); perplexities only as a convenience conversion of nats.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end study of "does offloading a skill to a tool free capacity" exists at any scale that I could find; the design above is assembled from precedents for each component, and the direction of some structural measures (effective rank, prunability) under "freed capacity" is not established.
