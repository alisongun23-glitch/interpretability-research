# Six literatures converge on the carry direction

The related-work section should be built around six bodies of work, and the paper's sharpest positioning claim is against two 2024 papers. **Quirke & Barez (ICLR 2024)** study the same 1-layer addition setting but describe the carry as a head-computed state and the MLP as a 60-key lookup they say they "haven't proven experimentally". **Kruthoff (2024)** places carrying in a set of MLP neurons, maps his model onto an XOR/AND full adder, and claims 1-layer models do not carry at all. Neither paper shows a causally used linear carry direction, attention that averages a_k and b_k, or a count-threshold parity MLP. The paper fills that gap and directly refines the gate-like framing ([Quirke & Barez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13121); [Kruthoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07993)). Several of the methodological findings have clear precedent, and the paper pushes most of them to an extreme. Logistic-probe directions were already known to be weaker interventions than difference-in-means or DAS directions (Marks & Tegmark; Li et al. ITI; CausalGym), but those papers report *reduced* effects, not the *zero* effect found here. Random-init probing baselines go back to Zhang & Bowman (2018). The class-mean failure is a concrete instance of what Li & Janson (2024) call "spoofing". The MLP's OR3 − MAJ + AND3 construction is the textbook depth-2 threshold circuit for symmetric functions, which goes back to Minnick (1961) and is treated systematically by Siu, Roychowdhury & Kailath (1995). Pre-registration has an ML home in the NeurIPS pre-registration workshops (PMLR v148), but the notes found no interpretability-specific precedent. Each citation below gives authors, year, venue/ID and URL as recorded in the research notes, and every entry the notes mark as unverified or secondhand is flagged.

## Two 2024 papers bracket the carry, and neither finds a direction

The paper's closest neighbours are three studies of small from-scratch addition transformers. **Quirke & Barez** train a 1-layer, 3-head transformer on n-digit addition with **most-significant-digit-first output**. They decompose the task into named sub-tasks: Base Add, Make Carry 1, Make Sum 9, Use Carry 1 and Use Sum 9. Each head covers a digit pair with a time offset. The carry appears as a head-level MC1 state, and the MLP is conjectured to be a key-value memory over a 2×3×10 = 60-key "trigram". The paper makes **no claim about linear directions or single neurons** ([Quirke & Barez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13121)). Its successor scales to 2–3 layers and 46 models. It formalises carry as a three-valued TriCase (0, 1, U), finds three PCA clusters in ST-head outputs, says MLP roles were "not analyzed in detail", and reports no probes or carry directions ([Quirke, Neo & Barez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02619)). **Kruthoff** is the nearest gate-like framing. In 2-layer encoder-only models on 3-digit addition, he selects 64–650 "carry" neurons by activation threshold and finds a leading SVD axis tied to carrying. In Appendix I he maps the model onto a **full adder of XOR/AND half adders**, with the final MLP as "the second XOR gate". He also states that 1-layer models do "not implement the carrying over algorithm" ([Kruthoff](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07993v2)).

Finding (1) answers Kruthoff directly. A best-unit R² of 0.30–0.40, against 1.0 for a single-sigmoid carry gate, together with 97–100% / 88–99% interchange along a label-free direction, is evidence that the carry lives in a direction rather than a neuron or neuron set. Kruthoff's negative 1-layer claim must be reconciled explicitly, because the paper's 1-layer models plainly use a carry. The notes trace the disagreement to setting: Quirke & Barez's 1-layer model handles single carries MSB-first, Kruthoff studies decimal encoder-only models, and the paper adds binary numbers LSB-first. The related-work section should name base, output order and architecture alongside each comparison. Finding (5) fills a gap the notes confirm: **no prior paper describes addition attention as averaging (a_k + b_k)/2**. The closest statements are Kruthoff's "staircase attention plus skip connection acts as an adder" and the RASP primitive whose aggregate is literally a mean over selected positions ([Weiss, Goldberg & Yahav](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06981)).

Output order explains why the paper's per-position computation is a 3-input parity. **Lee et al.** show that reversed (LSB-first) output reduces each answer digit to a local rule over two digits and the incoming carry. Under this format, 3-digit addition is learned perfectly from about 1k–4k samples, while MSB-first output plateaus a little above 85% ([Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)). In binary this local rule is exactly s_k = a_k ⊕ b_k ⊕ c_k. The length-generalisation literature (RASP-L, Abacus embeddings, positional description, NoPE, train-set priming) concerns positional bookkeeping, that is, which a_k and b_k attention retrieves. This is the same role the paper assigns to its averaging heads, which gives these mostly behavioural papers a mechanistic hook ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16028); [McLeish et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17399); [Kazemnejad et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466)). The notes found **no mechanistic study of binary addition transformers in peer-reviewed venues**.

The broader arithmetic-circuit literature supplies templates and counterpoints rather than direct competitors. Nanda et al. is the canonical full reverse-engineering of an arithmetic task, but modular addition has no carry ([Nanda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)). Zhong et al. show that the same task admits different algorithms across seeds and hyperparameters, which justifies multi-seed replication ([Zhong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17844)). In pretrained LLMs, Stolfo et al. establish the "attention routes operands, MLP computes result" pattern via causal mediation ([Stolfo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15054)). Nikankin et al. find a "bag of heuristics" built from threshold-like MLP neurons, the strongest neuron-level counterpoint to finding (1) ([Nikankin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21272)). Zhou et al. show that attention computes high-frequency, parity-like components of sums ([Zhou, Fu, Sharan & Jia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03445)). Baeumel et al. report a one-digit lookahead carry heuristic in 7B models, but the notes flag that this entry rests on search snippets ([Baeumel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19981v1)).

## Interchange success is the standard test, but the written value decides

Finding (1) belongs to the interchange-intervention tradition. Activation patching comes from causal mediation analysis ([Vig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265)). Geiger and colleagues formalised it as interchange interventions under causal abstraction, made "interchange success" the operational definition of a represented-and-used variable, and then introduced **Distributed Alignment Search (DAS)** to learn the subspace in which interchange works ([Geiger et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997); [Geiger et al. DAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536)). The paper's label-free PC1 of counterfactual differences is best framed as **an unsupervised, cheaper alternative to DAS** that reaches DAS-level interchange accuracy. Zhang & Nanda support building it from counterfactual pairs: they recommend in-distribution corruptions such as symmetric token replacement over Gaussian noise, which "produce inconsistent discovery results" ([Zhang & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16042)). Turner et al.'s ActAdd, which builds steering vectors from contrastive-pair activation differences, is the closest steering analogue ([Turner et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248)). Heimersheim & Nanda show that noising and denoising "are usually not complements", so the paper must say which direction its 97–100% and 88–99% figures measure ([Heimersheim & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15255)).

The illusion literature defines what the paper must rule out. **Makelov, Lange & Nanda** show that a subspace patch can succeed by "activating a dormant parallel pathway" that is causally disconnected on-distribution ([Makelov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030)). Wu et al.'s reply argues that these illusions are artifacts of the evaluation paradigm, and the two should be cited together ([Wu et al. reply](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12631)). Because the paper's direction is the *principal* direction of on-distribution counterfactual differences, it carries large variance by construction. That is the natural argument against dormancy, and the paper should make it explicitly. **Sutter et al.** prove that unrestricted non-linear alignment maps can map any network to any algorithm and reach 100% IIA on IOI in randomly initialised LMs ([Sutter et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08802)). This makes the paper's one-dimensional linear restriction a strength rather than a limitation.

Finding (4) has its closest formal analogue in **Li & Janson's "Optimal ablation"**, which separates *deletion* from *spoofing*. Spoofing is information inserted by the replacement value itself. The paper finds that spoofing "dominates the Δ measurements for previous ablation methods" and that "every possible value … conveys at least weak information to the model" ([Li & Janson](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09951)). Writing a class-mean carry value along a direction that downstream readers compare against other components is spoofing in its purest form. The value is correct as a marginal statistic but inconsistent with the rest of that example's residual stream, whereas interchange copies a value that was jointly consistent with a real activation. Causal scrubbing's preference for resampling over "the standard zero and mean ablations" ([Chan et al.](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN/causal-scrubbing-a-method-for-rigorously-testing)) and the Hydra effect's warning that ablation effects are not additive ([McGrath et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771)) complete the ablation-choice picture. The notes found **no paper naming the "relative readout" failure exactly**, so finding (4) is a genuine concrete mechanism for why mean-style interventions mislead.

## Probe directions classify well and intervene weakly, and here not at all

Findings (2) and (3) sit where the linear representation hypothesis meets the probing-pitfalls literature. Park, Choe & Veitch formalise "concepts are directions" via counterfactuals and show that a probe (readout) direction and a steering (intervention) direction coincide only under a non-Euclidean "causal inner product". This gives a theoretical reason why a Euclidean logistic-probe normal can miss the causal direction ([Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658)). Three empirical precedents report the same gap at smaller magnitude. **Marks & Tegmark** find that mass-mean beats logistic regression on causal effect in 7 of 8 conditions, for example 0.77 vs 0.13 normalised indirect effect on LLaMA-2-13B. They attribute this to logistic regression tilting toward a max-margin separator to avoid interference from superposed, non-orthogonal features ([Marks & Tegmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06824)). In **ITI**, shifting along the probe weight gives 34.8% True*Info, barely above a random direction (31.2%), while mass-mean shift gives 42.3% ([Li et al. ITI](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03341)). **CausalGym** ranks DAS (odds ratio about 9.95) far above probes (about 3.42) on Pythia-6.9B and states that high probe accuracy "provides no guarantee" of use ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12560)). Kumar et al. supply the theory: even when concept features alone give 100% accuracy, a probe "is likely to use non-concept features" ([Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04153)). The paper's contribution is the **extreme case**: high readout accuracy with exactly zero patching effect, in a model small enough to be fully characterised. The notes found no prior report of a zero effect. Measuring the cosine between the probe and PC1 directions would let the paper test the Marks & Tegmark superposition explanation directly.

Finding (4)'s "read relative to other components" also has a representational precedent. Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg showed that Othello board state looks nonlinear under one labelling and becomes linear under a relative one ("mine vs. theirs") ([Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941)). The broader lineage includes Toy Models of Superposition ([Elhage et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10652)), the original Othello-GPT probes ([Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382)), causally validated single directions for sentiment ([Tigges et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15154)), and probe-based neuron localisation that finding (2) argues needs causal checks ([Gurnee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610)). Engels et al.'s irreducibly multi-dimensional features are a caveat worth one sentence: one-dimensional linearity is an empirical finding about the carry, not a universal law ([Engels et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14860)).

Finding (3) replicates a classic NLP result in a mechanistic setting. **Zhang & Bowman** reported that "randomly-initialized, frozen LSTMs perform strikingly well" on auxiliary syntactic tasks ([Zhang & Bowman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10040)). Wieting & Kiela found that trained sentence encoders gain "surprisingly little" over random ones ([Wieting & Kiela](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10444)). Voita & Titov showed that probe accuracies "do not substantially favour pretrained representations over randomly initialized ones" and proposed MDL probing to separate them ([Voita & Titov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12298)). **Zhong & Andreas** give the mechanism for arithmetic: random transformers with only embeddings trained can already do decimal addition, so task-relevant structure is present at initialisation ([Zhong & Andreas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04368)). Control tasks and selectivity, the information-theoretic view of probes as measuring information present rather than use, and the decodability-is-not-usage results from amnesic, usage-based and counterfactual probing should all be cited as the conceptual ancestry of findings (2) and (3) ([Hewitt & Liang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368); [Elazar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00995); [Ravichander et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00719)). One point needs care: **CausalGym reports that DAS "finds causal effect at all timesteps, even when the model has just been initialised"**, and Sutter et al. get 100% IIA on random LMs. A random-init control on the *interchange* metric, not only on probes, is therefore what the literature demands.

## The MLP rediscovers a 1961 threshold circuit

Finding (6) has a 60-year pedigree that the paper should claim explicitly. A single sigmoid gate is the McCulloch–Pitts linear threshold element ([McCulloch & Pitts](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02478259)). Parity is not linearly separable, so no single threshold unit can compute it; the notes take this content from background knowledge rather than an opened page ([Minsky & Papert](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0262130432.json)). Symmetric functions, which depend only on the count n = a + b + c, are computed in depth 2 by count-threshold units combined with alternating signs. For three inputs, **parity = [n≥1] − [n≥2] + [n≥3]**, which is exactly the paper's **OR3 − MAJ + AND3**. Minnick's 1961 magnetic-core work synthesised symmetric functions from threshold cores with at most ⌊(n+3)/2⌋ cores ([Minnick](https://doi.org/10.1109/TEC.1961.5219146)). Siu, Roychowdhury & Kailath give the depth–size tradeoffs and devote Chapter 3 of their 1995 book to "Computing Symmetric Functions" ([Siu et al. 1991](https://doi.org/10.1109/12.106225); [Siu et al. 1995](https://openlibrary.org/isbn/0133007081.json)). Minnick's bound means cheaper constructions exist, so using the full n-unit form is a falsifiable property of the trained MLP and not a foregone conclusion. Finding (5) makes the picture coherent: attention averaging a_k and b_k delivers the count (up to scale) on one axis, which is exactly the input a count-threshold layer needs.

A reviewer will probably raise one point. **MAJ(a, b, c) is the full-adder carry-out.** The paper should state that a MAJ-like unit inside the parity construction is not the "gate-like carry neuron" that finding (1) rules out. The R² of 0.30–0.40 refers to the carry variable the model reads, which lives in a direction, and a unit that computes MAJ of the current position's inputs plays a different role. The notes do not settle this; it is a framing issue for the authors to resolve with their own data. Kruthoff's Appendix I full-adder mapping is the prior work that invites the confusion ([Kruthoff](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.07993v2)). Hänni et al. show theoretically that Boolean gates in an MLP need not be one neuron per gate ([Hänni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05451)), which supports the direction-not-neuron reading. Nikankin et al. and Hanna et al. show threshold-like MLP neurons in pretrained models ([Nikankin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21272); [Hanna et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00586)). Barak et al. explain why parity is the hard part of addition for SGD ([Barak et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08799)).

The single-sigmoid reference gate (R² = 1.0) and any margin criterion rest on the **static discipline** of digital design. MIT 6.111 states it concretely: "if VIN <= VIL then VOUT >= VOH", with noise margins separating the input and output thresholds ([MIT 6.111](https://web.mit.edu/6.111/www/f2005/tutprobs/digital_answers.html)). Agarwal & Lang's Chapter 5, "The Digital Abstraction", is the textbook home ([Agarwal & Lang](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/foundations-of-analog-and-digital-electronic-circuits/agarwal/978-1-55860-735-4)). Von Neumann's restoring organs explain why gates whose outputs are tighter than their inputs compose without error accumulation ([von Neumann](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400882618-003)). Hajnal et al. analyse unreliable and imprecise threshold gates from the complexity side ([Hajnal et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(93)90001-D)). The notes found **no source connecting the static discipline to sigmoid neural networks**, so the paper's use of it is a novel bridge, not a borrowed definition.

## Pre-registration is established in ML but absent in interpretability

Finding (7), that the predictions were pre-registered and most failed, should be presented as a methodological contribution with a clear lineage. **Nosek et al.** give the canonical rationale: pre-registration separates prediction (confirmatory) from postdiction (exploratory). The notes summarise this content from background knowledge, since the PNAS page was blocked ([Nosek et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)). ML institutionalised it through the pre-registration workshops at ICCV 2019, NeurIPS 2020 (PMLR v148, 23 Stage-2 papers) and NeurIPS 2021. These used a two-stage model in which hypotheses and protocol are reviewed before results exist. The stated rationale is that plan-first review "separates hypothesis generation from confirmation" and makes negative results publishable ([PMLR v148](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v148/); [preregister.science](https://preregister.science/)). That rationale fits a paper whose most informative results are failed predictions.

Within interpretability, the calls for this kind of rigor are explicit but unaccompanied by pre-registration practice. Lipton & Steinhardt criticise the failure to separate explanation from speculation ([Lipton & Steinhardt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03341)). **Leavitt & Morcos** use single-neuron analyses as a case study and argue for "strongly falsifiable" interpretability hypotheses. This is the most direct precedent, and the paper's pre-registered gate-neuron prediction answers their call in their own example domain ([Leavitt & Morcos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12016)). Räuker et al. call much inner-interpretability work "largely unproductive" and ask for diagnostics and benchmarking ([Räuker et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13243)). Causal scrubbing tests "precisely stated hypotheses" and seeks an "interpretability equivalent of property-based testing" ([Chan et al.](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JvZhhzycHu2Yd57RN/causal-scrubbing-a-method-for-rigorously-testing)). Zhong et al.'s Clock-vs-Pizza result explains why mechanistic predictions fail: the same task admits several learned algorithms ([Zhong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17844)). The notes found **no interpretability-specific pre-registration precedent**, but this is an absence-of-evidence result from a search the notes themselves call incomplete. The paper should say "to our knowledge" and run a targeted search of 2023–2026 mechanistic-interpretability work before claiming novelty.

## Conclusion

Read against this literature, the paper's distinctive contribution is not any single technique. It **converts three known cautions into exact, fully mechanised cases**: a probe-versus-causal-direction gap that reaches zero rather than merely shrinking, a mean-ablation hazard whose cause (relative readout) is identified rather than inferred, and a neuron-versus-direction question settled in the very setting where a gate-like full-adder story had been proposed. The related-work section should therefore position the paper as a small, fully characterised testbed in which the warnings of Marks & Tegmark, Li & Janson, Makelov et al. and Zhang & Bowman can be observed to their ends, while the MLP's arithmetic connects modern interpretability to 1960s threshold logic. The literature also implies four additions that would pre-empt reviewers. First, state whether interchange numbers are noising or denoising. Second, report interchange accuracy (not only probe accuracy) on random-init models, given CausalGym and Sutter et al. Third, show that PC1 is active on-distribution, to answer Makelov et al. Fourth, explain why the MAJ count-threshold unit is not a carry gate.

Before submission, the authors should independently verify the flagged entries. The highest-priority items are the Baeumel et al. Lookahead paper (snippet-sourced), the venues for Liu et al., Vig et al., Adebayo et al. and Räuker et al., the causal-scrubbing author names, and the exact section in Siu et al. 1995 or Muroga 1971 that states the alternating-sum parity identity. The notes name three further candidates that were not verified at all and have no citation details recorded: Hahn (2020) on self-attention's limits for parity, Chughtai et al. (2023) "A Toy Model of Universality", and the Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams PDP Vol. 1 chapter on hidden units counting active inputs. That last one would be the most apt historical precedent for count-threshold parity if it checks out.
