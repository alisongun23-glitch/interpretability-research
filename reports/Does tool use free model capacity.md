# No capacity is freed until arithmetic is paid for

Delegating arithmetic to the calculator has not freed any measurable capacity in the 1.35 M-weight story GPT, and on the present data it could not have, because the model it is compared against never learned arithmetic: the no-calls model scores **0 % on held-out addition, 3 % on subtraction and 10 % on multiplication**, so there is no arithmetic circuit whose absence in the tool model could have been redeployed to stories. The literature gives the same verdict at every scale it has looked: the only matched-data perplexity comparison in the tool-use literature (Toolformer at 6.7 B) reports **10.3 versus 10.3**, "no cost" rather than a gain, and "freed capacity" appears in print only as a conjecture in two surveys, never as a measurement ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761); [Mialon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07842)). Retrieval models are the best-measured analogue, and they show that an external store substitutes for parameters on the offloaded task (a 247 M model with a 3 B-token datastore beats the same model trained on those 3 B tokens, 13.73 versus 15.17 perplexity), not that the parameters inside were put to other uses ([kNN-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00172)). The user's gap runs the other way in any case: three tool seeds at **15.66-15.76** against one no-calls seed at **15.33** is about 0.021 nats at equal steps and about 0.0065 nats at equal story words, and the spread of the three tool seeds implies a per-run standard deviation near 0.06 perplexity, so the matched-words gap is roughly 1.5 standard errors and is explained at least as well by the tool model paying for call syntax and by call chips displacing story context inside 64-token windows as by any capacity story. A capacity budget says the stakes are modest but detectable: an addition circuit inside a language model costs a few hundred MLP neurons out of 2,048 (Nanda's one-layer, width-128 modular-addition circuit uses 433 of 512), single-digit percent of the roughly 0.8 M non-embedding weights, which under a Kaplan-style exponent of 0.077 would move story loss by about 0.01-0.03 nats, the same order as the observed gaps and two to eight times the seed noise ([Nanda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217); [Kaplan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.08361)). The question is therefore answerable on a laptop, but only in this order: first build a no-calls model that actually pays for arithmetic (upweighted answer loss, bare equation snippets, more passes), then add two controls that the published literature never had (a stories-only stream and the no-calls stream with the answer masked) so that the cost of learning arithmetic, the cost of gradient on unlearnable targets and the cost of chip tokens are separable, then run all four conditions at three seeds across the available 0.33-4.3 M widths and read freed capacity as a horizontal shift of the story-loss-versus-parameters curve, and finally look for the effect with finer instruments than aggregate perplexity: frequency-binned story loss, agreement minimal pairs built from the 4,300-word vocabulary, name and pronoun tracking probes, and ablation and probe maps of where arithmetic lives in each model. At 30 minutes per run with four runs side by side, the whole programme is about 15 laptop-hours at the current step budget.

## Every controlled precedent found "no cost", and none ever looked for a gain

The tool-use literature has measured language quality after tool training exactly once with data held fixed, and the result was equality. Toolformer fine-tuned GPT-J (6.7 B) on a CCNet subset annotated with API calls and compared it, with calls disabled, against the same model fine-tuned on the same subset without calls: **WikiText 10.3 versus 10.3, CCNet 10.5 versus 10.5**, reported at 0.1 resolution from one run with no error bars, under the caption that adding API calls "comes without a cost in terms of perplexity for language modeling without any API calls" ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)). No perplexity with calls enabled exists because marginalising over possible calls is intractable, which is also why Mialon et al. warn that tool-augmented models "might actually lose the ability to assign a probability to the next token" and why the only well-defined language metric for such a model is perplexity on text where no call fires, exactly the user's story-only measurement ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761); [Mialon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07842)). The 0.4-point worsening from base GPT-J (9.9) to both fine-tuned variants (10.3) is the cost of fine-tuning on CCNet at all, paid equally by the no-tool control, so it is distribution drift rather than tool use ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)).

The same table contains the literature's only "use it or lose it" test, and it goes the wrong way for the hypothesis. Toolformer with the calculator switched off scored **14.8 / 6.3 / 15.0 on ASDiv / SVAMP / MAWPS, against 9.6 / 5.0 / 9.3 for the matched no-tool fine-tune and 7.5 / 5.2 / 9.9 for plain GPT-J**: learning to call a calculator improved the model's own arithmetic rather than eroding it, presumably because the filtered call data is a curated arithmetic curriculum ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)). SyReLM, which trains frozen Vicuna-13B and GPT-J adapters by policy gradient against a symbolic solver, shows the same pattern: the model fine-tuned with the LM objective and run without the solver scores above the base on SVAMP and GSM8K ([SyReLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05571)). No published study at any scale shows internal arithmetic degrading because a calculator was available during training; the forgetting that is documented since 2024 is narrow fine-tuning collapse of instruction-tuned models on API data that contains no arithmetic at all, such as Llama-3-8B-Instruct falling from **79.45 to 26.38 on GSM8K and from 59.15 to 0.00 on HumanEval** after full fine-tuning on API-Bank, or Gemma-2B's MMLU dropping from 0.417 to 0.299 after function-calling fine-tuning, both repaired by mixing general text back in ([CITI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13202); [Alopex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05209)). Those results say nothing about offloading; they say that a model trained from scratch on a mixed stream, as the user's is, should keep the mixture and test general ability rather than only the tool task. The newest agent papers locate the cost of tool reliance in behaviour rather than parameters: with a code interpreter, seven proprietary models raised final accuracy on competition mathematics from 37.6 % to 43.3 % while the tool-free versions won pairwise reasoning comparisons 52.4 % to 47.6 %, because tools "reduce arithmetic errors but increase logic, assumption, and creativity errors" ([Tool-Induced Myopia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10899)). A 1.35 M story model has no deliberative reasoning policy to lose, so this strand is of limited relevance.

The calculator-training papers could not have shown freed capacity even in principle, because most of them keep the calculator's output in the loss. GSM8K's annotations are trained with "no special distinction between the annotated tokens and the rest of the solution: they are all just tokens", with the calculator only overriding sampling at test time, and Calc-X trains "with cross-entropy loss on all tokens", so those models are still supervised to compute and the calculator merely repairs their errors ([Cobbe et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168); [Calc-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15017)). ToolkenGPT freezes the whole language model and trains only tool-token embeddings, which avoids forgetting by construction rather than measuring it ([ToolkenGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11554)). PAL is prompting only, and its weak-model result is a warning for small models: text-davinci-001 fell from 26.5 % with chain-of-thought to 8.6 % with PAL, because producing a correct program cost more than the interpreter gave back ([PAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10435)). What these papers do show, repeatedly, is parameter efficiency on the tool's own task: TALM's 220 M model beats a 3 B model on Natural Questions, Calcformer-T5-L at 700 M beats T5-XL at 3 B without a calculator on five of six math sets, and Toolformer at 6.7 B beats GPT-3 at 175 B on math word problems while still losing to it on open-domain QA ([TALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12255); [Calc-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15017); [Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)). None of them holds data and compute equal and asks whether the tool model is better at something unrelated to the tool, and the places where the small tool model still loses are consistent with tools adding a skill rather than freeing general capacity.

The user's design, with the machine-written answer excluded from the loss, is the version that could show freed capacity, and it is the modern convention: ToolkenGPT replaces result tokens with `[N/A]` and ignores them in the loss, TRL's chat templates keep tool responses outside the generation block, every reinforcement-learning tool system masks interpreter output, and the one ablation that measures it, Search-R1, puts the masking gain at **0.431 versus 0.343 average exact match** for a 7 B model ([ToolkenGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11554); [Search-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)). The closest structural precedents for "tokens in the input but never targets" are pause tokens and RETRO: Goyal et al. insert `<pause>` tokens at 10 % of positions, compute the loss "ignoring any loss term that corresponds to predicting the pause tokens themselves", match on total tokens, and state that the augmented model therefore sees only about 90 % of the meaningful tokens; RETRO's objective sums log-likelihood over input tokens only and never over retrieved neighbours ([Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226); [RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)). That 90 % remark is the user's confound in mirror image, since here it is the no-calls model that sees more story words per step, and the published practice is to report loss over real tokens only, state the deficit, and compare at matched real tokens.

Retrieval is where "a smaller model with an external store matches a larger one" has actually been measured on perplexity, and its caveats transfer directly. RETRO at 7.5 B "obtains comparable performance to GPT-3 and Jurassic-1 on the Pile, despite using 25x fewer parameters", with gains that "correspond to non-retrieval models with 10x more parameters on certain datasets", largest on Wikitext103 and C4 where near-duplicates exist and smallest on Curation Corpus, and partly inflated by leakage that "Retro models exploit more strongly than baseline models" ([RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)). RETRO-fit adds retrieval to a frozen pretrained model and "ensures that when evaluated without retrieval, the original model performance is exactly maintained", which guarantees that nothing inside the base weights was redeployed ([RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)). kNN-LM gives the cleanest template for the user: hold the model fixed, vary what is offloaded, and ask how much training data the un-augmented model needs to catch up; a model trained on 100 M tokens plus a 3 B-token datastore (13.73) beat the same model trained on all 3 B tokens (15.17) because "retrieving nearest neighbors from the corpus outperforms training on it" ([kNN-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00172)). Translated to the story GPT, the retrieval question becomes how many extra arithmetic exposures the no-calls model needs to match the tool model's arithmetic, and whether story loss moves at all along the way. The surveys state the conjecture plainly and leave it open: Mialon et al. propose that by delegating rarely needed abilities to tools, "tool-augmented LMs may have better scaling laws and thus yield smaller models retaining the capabilities of their largest counterpart", and Qin et al. name "the optimal balance between internalized capabilities and external tool reliance" as the open question, neither with any measurement of parameter savings ([Mialon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07842); [Qin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08354)). A clean small-scale test is therefore a genuine contribution, not a replication, and the literature's prior for its headline number is equality within noise.

## At 1.35 M weights arithmetic would cost a few hundred neurons, and the no-calls model never bought them

Two capacity bounds bracket what arithmetic could cost. The memorisation bound comes from Allen-Zhu and Li, who find that GPT-2-style models from about 1 M to 0.5 B parameters "can and only can store 2 bits of knowledge per parameter" when each fact is seen about 1,000 times, falling to about 1 bit per parameter at 100 exposures ([Allen-Zhu and Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)). With results under 256 there are about 33,000 addition pairs, 33,000 subtraction pairs and 2,000 multiplication pairs, roughly 68,000 facts of 8 bits, about 0.54 M bits if stored as a table, which is **270 k parameters (20 % of the model) at 2 bits per parameter or 540 k (40 %) at 1 bit** (my arithmetic from the stated limits). The circuit bound is far smaller: Nanda's one-layer ReLU transformer with d_model 128, four heads of dimension 32 and a 512-unit MLP, the user's layer shape exactly, learns the full 113 × 113 modular-addition table from 30 % of the pairs with **433 of its 512 MLP neurons** devoted to five Fourier frequencies, and Quirke and Barez map five-digit addition onto three heads of a one-layer model ([Nanda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217); [Quirke and Barez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13121)). Inside a language model the circuit shares embeddings, attention and the residual stream, so its marginal cost is mostly MLP neurons, a few hundred of the 2,048 the four layers hold, which is single-digit percent of the model. The parameter count itself deserves one note: 4,300 × 128 is 0.55 M, and four blocks of 12 × 128² are 0.79 M, which together give the stated 1.35 M only if the embedding is tied, so the capacity that arithmetic competes for is about 0.8 M non-embedding weights, the quantity Kaplan's laws are written in ([Kaplan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.08361)).

The exposure count, not the bit count, is what decided the user's runs. Seven thousand steps of batch 32 at context 64 is 14.3 M tokens, about 2.3 passes over the 6.3 M-token stream and therefore about 190,000 problem presentations; if the 84,000 problems are split evenly across the three operations, each addition fact was seen about twice and each multiplication fact about thirty times (my arithmetic). Allen-Zhu and Li's regime starts at 100 exposures, and below it a model either learns a generalising rule or learns nothing, so the observed ordering of **0 % addition, 3 % subtraction, 10 % multiplication** is exactly what exposure predicts, with the extra twist that about half of the sub-256 multiplication pairs involve 0 or 1 as an operand, where the answer is a copy or a constant rather than a product; the 10 % is most plausibly those cases, and the held-out arithmetic test should stratify by trivial operands before any of it is credited as computation (inference). The silenced tool model's 0.4-3.5 % is chance (1 in 256 is 0.4 %) with the same trivial-case tail, which confirms that masking did what it was meant to do and that the tool model computes nothing internally, the direct analogue of Toolformer's disabled rows ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)). The no-calls model's 0 % on addition is below chance, which means it writes a systematically wrong number, most likely one of the operands; logging what it writes would show whether it learned "the answer is a number from this sentence" without learning the operation (inference).

The toy-model theory of capacity allocation predicts this outcome rather than a cost to stories. Scherlis et al. define a feature's capacity as the fraction of an embedding dimension it occupies, bounded in total by the dimension, and show that optimal allocation sets each feature against a global Lagrange threshold: "the most important features tend to get dedicated (monosemantic) representation, less important features are represented polysemantically in proportion to their effect on the loss, and the least important features are ignored" ([Scherlis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01892); [Elhage et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10652)). The answer tokens are about **84,000 of 6.3 M loss-bearing tokens, 1.3 % of the gradient**, so arithmetic features sit below the threshold and are dropped; the theory says the displacement runs from arithmetic to stories, not the other way, and that is what the 15.33 perplexity of the no-calls model shows. The displacement would only reverse if arithmetic's loss weight rose or the model were saturated by features of comparable importance, which is why the "pays for arithmetic" model has to be built deliberately rather than waited for. Kaplan's fit confirms that the regime is parameter-limited rather than step-limited: with the WebText constants the parameter term for a 1 M non-embedding model is about 4 nats while the step term at 7,000 steps is about 0.4 nats, a ten-to-one ratio, so freed capacity would be visible in principle at this training length if anything occupied it ([Kaplan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.08361)). TinyStories places the model at the bottom of its sweep, where the 1 M eight-layer model scored 6 / 3 / 2 on grammar / creativity / consistency and "consistency emerges when hidden size rises from 64 to 128", so many language features are already being shared or dropped, and whatever arithmetic displaces will be low-importance features whose loss contribution is small ([TinyStories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)).

The data-mixing literature does not even predict that arithmetic should cost language. At 470 M parameters, a 25 % code share improved natural-language reasoning by 3.4 % relative to text-only and stayed neutral up to 75 %, with erosion only at 90-100 % code, and the 2.8 B results were "largely consistent"; the data-mixing-law paper names "training symbolic data for prose" as its one conflict example but gives no number ([Aryabumi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10914); [Ye et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16952v2)). At 374 M, replacing text with code lowered English passivization and common-morpheme scores while raising compositional generalisation and multi-digit arithmetic, which is the one precedent that says structured data trades surface-linguistic skill for structured skill, and it points at morphology and agreement as the place to look for any capacity returned by the tool ([code pretraining study](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04556)). Interference is dramatic only when a domain is removed: Flan-T5-Base's MultiNLI fell from 81.0 % to 16.5 % "within the first 1,000 training steps" of math-only fine-tuning, while interleaving at 1:1 kept it at 86.2 % and even 15:1 kept 83.8 % ([Flan-T5 mixing](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13706v1)). Both of the user's models keep stories in the mix throughout, so this literature predicts small story differences, and a small positive transfer from arithmetic (number words, quantity talk in stories) is as plausible as a small cost.

At 10 M parameters arithmetic is learnable from thousands of examples, so the no-calls failure is about format, loss share and optimisation rather than raw capacity. Lee et al.'s 10.6 M NanoGPT reaches near-perfect three-digit addition from about 2,500 reversed-format examples and 100 % from 1,000-2,000 scratchpad examples, while plain most-significant-digit-first format "plateaus at slightly over 85 % even with 10,000 samples"; two-digit addition shows a phase transition at about 1,500 samples; and inside 1.1 M characters of Shakespeare the model keeps addition working given enough examples, though "the training perplexity on Shakespeare also tends to increase" ([Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381)). With every integer 0-255 a single token there is no digit format to reverse, but the answer sits inside prose, the parse of the word problem is part of the task, and the loss share is 1.3 %. The one published model of the user's exact shape, a four-layer, width-128 LLaMA trained from scratch on 45,151 one-step problems such as "Add 3 and 4", learned to route operands into a calculator at 99.2 % only "by 62 epochs", about 2.8 M presentations against the user's 190,000, and Nanda's circuit formed over thousands of full-batch epochs with weight decay 1 and "do[es] not grok ... without weight decay" ([Saldyt and Kambhampati](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04899); [Nanda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)). The earlier report in this series predicted that an unmasked width-128 model "will try to add if you let it", on the strength of Nanda's capacity result; the user's data shows that at 7,000 steps and 1.3 % loss share it does not, which is a correction of that prediction's timescale rather than of the mechanism. Larger models are far more sample-efficient, by almost 100× across Kaplan's range, so the 4.3 M width may learn arithmetic from the same stream where 1.35 M cannot, which matters for the sweep design below ([Kaplan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.08361)).

"Use it or lose it" is moot for the tool model, which never had the skill, but it governs any change of regime. At small scale an unexercised skill vanishes fast: Lee et al.'s 10.6 M model "forgets" one-to-three-digit addition entirely when fine-tuned on four-digit data in plain format, and the Flan-T5 collapse above happened within 1,000 steps, whereas 3-32 B models lose single-digit percentages of math items after instruction tuning, offset by 16-19 % backward transfer ([Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381); [Flan-T5 mixing](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13706v1); [Harmon et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17776v1)). If a paying model is ever switched to the tool regime mid-training, or the reverse, expect the switched-off skill to disappear within a fraction of the budget unless its data stays in the mix. Length generalisation of addition is "strongly affected by random weight initialization and training data order", which is the published reason that arithmetic accuracy, like perplexity, needs several seeds before any comparison is read ([Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09371)).

The observed story gap, read against all of this, is a null with three confounds. The equal-steps gap of 15.66-15.76 against 15.33 is 0.021 nats; the equal-story-words gap of about 0.1 perplexity is 0.0065 nats (my arithmetic). The best published seed spread for a TinyStories model is **0.046 perplexity (0.0033 nats) across five seeds of a 5.46 M model**, with a 0.02-perplexity difference judged "within seed noise", and seed variance is "up to 3× larger for higher learning rate or smaller batch sizes" ([Three-Phase Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14430); [OpenEuroLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28308)). The user's own three tool seeds span 0.10 perplexity, which for three draws implies a per-run standard deviation near 0.06, so the standard error of a three-versus-one comparison is about 0.07 perplexity and the matched-words gap is about 1.5 standard errors (my arithmetic). The first confound is token count: a seven-token `<calc> 12 + 5 = 17 </calc>` chip on each of 84,000 problems is roughly 0.6 M tokens, about a tenth of the stream, which is why the 0.33 gap at equal steps shrinks to 0.1 at equal story words. The second is context: every 64-token window that contains a call has seven fewer story tokens of conditioning before and after the problem, so "story perplexity" in windows adjacent to problems is measured under shorter effective context, and this is not removed by matching cumulative story tokens. The third is that the tool model is learning something the no-calls model is not: a reliable call-and-copy pattern is an induction-style circuit that needs two attention layers in composition, and that circuit is a cost before it is a saving ([Induction heads](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)). A fourth effect pushes the other way and did not dominate: Allen-Zhu and Li find that unlearnable "junk" tokens can cut useful capacity by up to 20× at 100 exposures, far more than their token share, and the no-calls model's answer tokens are unlearnable at two exposures, yet the no-calls model is the better story model, so whatever that gradient costs is smaller than the tool model's syntax and context costs ([Allen-Zhu and Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)).

## Measure freed capacity as a curve shift and in skills, not as one perplexity number

The headline measurement should be story-only nats per token on one identical held-out set, compared at matched story tokens, with a third condition that makes the token streams identical. Perplexity for both models is exp of the mean per-token negative log-likelihood over the same pure-story set, the same tokenizer, the same 64-token windows and stride, excluding padding and special tokens, which mirrors Toolformer's calls-disabled check and the pause-token and RETRO practice of scoring real tokens only ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761); [Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226); [RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)). The user's 207,000-token held-out set is large enough that finite-sample noise is well below seed noise; OpenEuroLLM's decomposition puts the finite-sampling contribution at 6.3 × 10⁻⁴ nats against 2.7 × 10⁻³ for seeds on a 204,800-sequence validation set ([OpenEuroLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28308)). Report nats rather than perplexity, because loss-type metrics have the higher signal-to-noise ratio (bits-per-byte lifted GSM8K's ratio from 1.2 to 7.0 and MBPP's from 2.0 to 41.8), average the last five to ten checkpoints of each run because checkpoint-to-checkpoint noise tracks seed noise with R² of 0.82-0.86, and pair runs by seed so that conditions share initialisation and data order ([Heineman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144)). Log cumulative loss-bearing story tokens per step and evaluate at matched counts, and report equal-steps numbers alongside, since the two framings are the parameter-equivalent and compute-equivalent views respectively. The cleanest fix for the stream mismatch is a condition that uses the no-calls token stream, with the answer written in prose and no chips, but masks the answer token from the loss exactly as the tool condition masks the machine span; the no-calls condition minus this one isolates the burden of learning arithmetic on an identical stream, the tool condition minus it isolates the cost of chips and longer sequences, and the stories-only stream the user already has gives the ceiling that the code-mixing studies call the 0 % control ([Aryabumi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10914)). Padding the no-calls stream with loss-masked filler tokens is the alternative, with the caveat that from-scratch models do not exploit fillers for computation without "specific, dense supervision" ([Pfau et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15758)).

Freed capacity becomes a number only through a size sweep, which the available 0.33-4.3 M widths make possible. The published versions of this idea are RETRO's baseline-versus-retrieval pairs at 132 M / 172 M up to 7.0 B / 7.5 B, where retrieval "provides a constant gain for models ranging from 150M to 7B parameters"; the effective parameter count of Clark et al., which defines the size of a dense model that matches a routed model's loss; and Davidson's compute-equivalent gain, the ratio of training compute needed without an enhancement to match the enhanced model ([RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426); [Clark et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01169); [Davidson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07413)). The recipe is to fit L_story(N) = E + A / N^α per condition in Chinchilla's form with data fixed, using non-embedding N, and report the factor by which N must grow in the no-calls condition to match the tool condition's story loss, with a bootstrap over seeds; Chinchilla's own coefficients (α = 0.34 from 400 models of 70 M to 16 B) cannot be transported to 1 M-parameter story models, so α must be fitted locally ([Hoffmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)). The sweep has a second virtue: a consistent sign across four widths is evidence even when no single width is significant, and because larger models are the more sample-efficient learners, the no-calls model may only build an arithmetic circuit at the top of the sweep, so any real shift is expected to appear there first ([Kaplan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.08361)). The embedding share rises as width falls (0.55 M of the 1.35 M model is embedding at width 128), which compresses the effective range of the sweep in non-embedding parameters and is another reason to plot against non-embedding N.

Loss decomposition by token type shows where any gap comes from before anyone argues about why. Toolformer judges a call's usefulness by the weighted loss on the five tokens after it (weights 1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2), and Quiet-STaR reports that average perplexity barely moves while "difficult tokens improve disproportionately", so an aggregate number can hide a local effect ([Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761); [Quiet-STaR](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09629v1)). The per-type table for this setting is story tokens in pure stories, problem-statement tokens, operand tokens, answer tokens (a training target only in the no-calls model, the tool-off test for the tool model), the first five tokens after a problem's answer, and story tokens ten or more tokens after a problem. A real capacity effect shows in the first and last of these; a gap driven by the five-token window is a local "recovering from the problem" effect. Frequency bins are the most sensitive story-side split: scaling model size lowers cross-entropy "almost exclusively by lowering uncertainty on the ~2,500 most frequent words" and shrinks the rare tail's share of loss, so a per-word loss table binned by training frequency (top 100, 100-1,000, 1,000-4,300) is where a few hundred redeployed neurons would show first ([Chung and Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15390)). Dialogue versus narration (inside and outside quotation marks), position in the window (tokens 48-64 minus tokens 8-16, a 64-token analogue of the induction-heads in-context-learning score), and first versus last sentence are free to compute and have no published precedent at this scale ([Induction heads](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html)).

Probing answers the question the tool-off accuracy cannot: whether the result is computed but not emitted. In GPT-J, Pythia-6.9B and Llama-3.1-8B numbers live on a generalised helix and addition is a rotation of it, verified by causal interventions, and in Pythia-70M ablating one context neuron raises loss by 8 % per sequence against 0.2 % at 6.9 B, so probes and ablations have larger, cleaner effects in small models ([Kantamneni and Tegmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00873); [Gurnee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610)). The design is a few thousand problems with known operands and results, the residual stream recorded at the second operand, at the `=` or chip position and at the first answer token in every layer, and ridge probes for the value plus logistic probes per digit with a held-out split; both models should encode the operands, since the tool model needs them to write the call, and only a paying no-calls model should encode the result before the answer position. The tool-off test the user already ran is the right one but its baseline is chance, because those positions were never targets; the informative versions are whether a result probe reads out the sum anyway, and whether a brief fine-tune on answer tokens recovers arithmetic faster in the tool model than from scratch, which is what RETRO's retrieval-off and Toolformer's disabled rows measure for their tools ([RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426); [Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)). Causal checks should patch or mean-ablate rather than zero, use logit difference between the correct and a distractor number as the metric, and expect backup components to "jump into action" when one is removed ([Heimersheim and Nanda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255)).

Pruning and ablation turn "capacity in use" into a scatter plot. Michel et al. remove heads one at a time with a gradient-based importance score, find that greedy pruning removes 20-40 % of heads "without noticeable impact" before performance "drops sharply", and that "the important heads are determined early (but not immediately) during the training process" ([Michel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10650)). For the story GPT, mean-ablating every head and every MLP neuron (16 heads and 2,048 neurons) and recording the change in story loss against the change in arithmetic loss on a 100,000-token evaluation set gives a two-axis map: components that matter for arithmetic but not stories are the arithmetic budget, the hypothesis predicts a paying model has a larger such set, and the tool model's components should be more uniformly story-relevant. Magnitude pruning is the wrong instrument at this size, since on OPT-125M it raises perplexity from 38.99 to 276 at 50 % sparsity and degrades more at smaller scale; an importance-ranked structured pruning curve, compared across conditions by the sparsity at which story loss rises by a fixed 0.01 nats, is the usable form, with the caveat that freed capacity could make a model either more prunable (unused) or less (redeployed), so its sign must be read with the scaling curve ([SparseGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00774)). Dead-unit fraction, effective rank of representations and mean absolute weight are free plasticity correlates with a documented 15-minute CPU protocol, and they belong in the table as secondary indicators whose direction under "freed capacity" is not established ([Dohare et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)).

Transfer is the most direct behavioural test of spare capacity. Hernandez et al. define effective data transferred as the extra fine-tuning data a same-size model trained from scratch would need to match a pretrained model's fine-tuning loss, with "pre-training effectively multiplies the fine-tuning dataset size"; the user's version is to fine-tune each pretrained model on a new story skill the stream never contained (a second genre, an instruction prefix, a second tool) over a log grid of 100 to 10,000 examples and report the horizontal shift, which the hypothesis predicts favours the tool model ([Hernandez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293)). The arithmetic recovery fine-tune doubles as the use-it-or-lose-it quantification: if the tool model learns to emit answers from fewer examples than a from-scratch model but more slowly than the paying model, the difference is how much arithmetic structure writing calls gave it. Pause-token fine-tuning of a model not pretrained with pauses gave mixed results, a reminder that an affordance only pays off if the model was trained to use it, so the comparison must hold the tool out of the fine-tune for all conditions ([Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)).

Skill tests for a story model of this size exist and are cheap, provided they are scored by log-probability rather than generation. The table below lists the measures with a precedent and an expected sensitivity ordering; the three with both precedent and sensitivity at 1-3 M are frequency-binned story loss, in-vocabulary agreement minimal pairs and two-candidate name and pronoun tracking, with the induction test as a structural diagnostic and judge grading as an optional check.

| Measure | What it detects | Scoring | Precedent and typical values | Cost on CPU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story loss by frequency bin, token class, position | Lexical and fluency gains, where a gap lives | Masked mean NLL per bin, nats | Gains land on the ~2,500 most frequent words ([Chung and Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15390)) | One pass over 207 k tokens |
| Agreement minimal pairs from the 4,300-word lexicon | Morphosyntax (subject-verb, determiner-noun, anaphor, irregular past, pronoun case) | Grammatical sentence gets higher summed log-prob; ties count 0.5 | BLiMP 67 paradigms × 1,000 pairs; 10 M-word causal baselines 0.63-0.70, best 0.80-0.81 ([BLiMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00582); [BabyLM 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05149)) | 1,000 forward passes per paradigm |

Three design rules make the minimal pairs honest. A word-frequency baseline scored unexpectedly well on BabyLM because "a system that returns identical logits for both sentences in a minimal pair can achieve maximal accuracy" and 22 subtasks pair permutations of the same words, so pairs must differ in exactly one token, ties must count against, and permutation pairs are out ([Elementary baselines](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.16/)). Both variants of the contrasted token must occur in training and items should be stratified by that token's training frequency, following LongTail-Swap's finding that BabyLM metrics "concentrate on the head of the word distribution" ([LongTail-Swap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04268)). Expected absolute scores are unpublished below 18 M parameters, where TinyStories-trained GPT-2 models score 57-59 on filtered BLiMP against 64-66 for a mixed natural corpus, so the user's models should be compared with each other on in-vocabulary agreement paradigms, where the binomial standard error is 1.4 points at 1,000 pairs and 3 points at 200 ([What Should Baby Models Read?](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06672)). Generation-based scoring should be avoided outright, since even 160 M to 1 B base models sit at or below chance when asked to generate the tracked location rather than score it ([entity tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18083)). TinyStories' own loss-to-grade conversion sets expectations for the judge: in the hidden-768 rows a 0.04-0.05 nat drop in evaluation loss moved GPT-4 grades by 0.3-0.6 points, so the 0.0065-nat gap in play here is invisible to grading and only the log-probability instruments can see it ([TinyStories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)).

Power sets the seed count. With per-run standard deviation σ and n seeds per arm the standard error of a difference of means is σ√(2/n), so a two-sided criterion at two standard errors needs a true gap of about 2σ with two seeds, 1.6σ with three and 1.3σ with five; at the user's implied σ of 0.06 perplexity that is roughly 0.12, 0.10 and 0.08 perplexity, and the Kaplan-style estimate of 0.01-0.03 nats (0.15-0.45 perplexity at 15.5) for a real 5-10 % capacity redeployment sits above all three, which is why the experiment can discriminate if the paying model exists (my arithmetic). Zhong et al. make the general point: "when accuracy gains between sizes are small, most signals across different model sizes will be dominated by noise due to random seeds" ([Zhong et al.](https://nlp.cs.berkeley.edu/pubs/Zhong-Ghosh-Klein-Steinhardt_2021_Instance_paper.pdf)). Every number in the writeup should be a mean with its seed standard deviation and n, with a difference claimed only beyond 2σ√(2/n).

## A 15-hour laptop design that first makes arithmetic cost something

The design has four conditions, run in the order that makes each contrast interpretable, and it starts from what the user already has: three tool seeds that fix the noise floor near 0.06 perplexity, a no-calls seed, a stories-only stream, and the arithmetic held-out tests.

| Condition | Stream | Loss | What it pays for | Key contrasts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, tool | Problems with `<calc> a op b = r </calc>`, machine span in context | All model-written tokens; machine span masked | Call syntax, operand routing, copy of the result | A − C: chip and context cost; A − B: headline |
| B, no calls | Problems with the answer in prose | All tokens including the answer | Call-free parse plus arithmetic, if it learns it | B − C: arithmetic burden on an identical stream; B − D: cost of arithmetic data |
| C, masked answer | Same stream as B | Answer token masked, as in A | Parse only; no arithmetic target, no chips | Baseline for A and B |
| D, stories only | Pure stories at matched story tokens | All tokens | Nothing but stories | Ceiling; the 0 % control |

Step 0 completes the control set at 1.35 M and costs one hour: two more no-calls seeds, three seeds of C and three of D, eight runs in two batches of four. This alone answers two questions the literature never could. D against B at matched story tokens says whether arithmetic data in the stream costs stories anything at this scale; the code-mixing precedents predict a few hundredths of a perplexity point either way ([Aryabumi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10914)). B against C says whether gradient on unlearnable answer tokens costs anything, which is the Allen-Zhu junk-data effect in a setting where the junk is structured; the existing 15.33 result suggests the cost is small, but one seed cannot say ([Allen-Zhu and Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)). A against C, which the present data approximates, is then read as the price of chips and context rather than as evidence about capacity.

Step 1 builds the paying model, and nothing after it is meaningful without it. The levers follow directly from why B failed: raise the answer token's loss weight so that it carries about a tenth of the gradient rather than 1.3 %, which moves arithmetic features above Scherlis's threshold; add bare equation snippets of the form `12 + 5 = 17` beside the word problems so that each fact is seen without the parsing load, following Lee et al.'s text-mixing recipe where plain addition "perform[s] almost perfectly with 40,000 train samples"; add weight decay, which Nanda's circuits require; and allow up to three times the steps, since Nanda's circuit formation ran for thousands of epochs and the same-shape LLaMA needed 62 passes ([Scherlis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01892); [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03381); [Nanda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217); [Saldyt and Kambhampati](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04899)). Try addition alone first, because it has the most facts per exposure and no trivial-case tail to flatter it, and define success as at least 80 % on held-out addition pairs that exclude 0 and 1 as operands, with the per-pair outputs logged so that an operand-copying failure is visible. A matrix of eight such runs is two to four hours. Whatever recipe succeeds must then be applied identically to A and C, the same snippets with calls in A, the same weights on model-written tokens, the same steps, so that the only remaining difference between A and B is who writes the answer. If no recipe reaches the criterion at 1.35 M, repeat the matrix at 4.3 M, where sample efficiency is higher; if arithmetic cannot be learned at 4.3 M either within the budget, the capacity question at this scale reduces to the chip and junk costs already measured in Step 0, and that is a publishable finding about the regime rather than a failure ([Kaplan et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2001.08361)).

Step 2 is the sweep: four conditions, three paired seeds, four widths from 0.33 M to 4.3 M, 48 runs. At the current step count the small widths take about ten minutes and the 4.3 M width about ninety, so the sweep is about seven to eight hours with four runs side by side, and roughly three times that if Step 1 settled on three times the steps (my estimate from the 30-minute figure and the parameter ratios). Evaluate every run on the identical held-out story set at matched story tokens and at matched steps, fit the per-condition curves, and report the parameter-equivalent factor with its bootstrap interval and the sign of A − B at each width. The RETRO precedent says a real offloading gain is roughly constant across sizes; the exposure argument says the gain can only appear at widths where B actually learned arithmetic, so a shift that grows with width is the expected signature if the hypothesis is true ([RETRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)).

Step 3 runs the probes on the checkpoints Step 2 already produced, minutes each: the per-token-type loss table, frequency-binned story loss, the mean-ablation scatter of story loss against arithmetic loss per head and neuron, the gradient-importance pruning curve, dead units and effective rank, the operand and result probes across layers with their causal checks, the repeated-sequence induction test, the agreement minimal pairs and the tracking probes. Step 4 is the transfer grid, one to two hours per condition: fine-tune each checkpoint on a new story skill and, separately, on arithmetic answers without the tool, over a log grid of example counts, and report effective-data shifts ([Hernandez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01293)). The whole programme is about 15 laptop-hours at the current step budget, two to three times that if the paying model needs longer runs, with Step 0 and Step 1 the only parts that must finish before the rest is worth starting.

The decision rules can be written down before the runs. The literature's prior is that A, B, C and D agree on story loss within noise, with D marginally best; that is the Toolformer result and the data-mixing result, and it would mean the tool at this scale supplies a skill rather than freeing capacity. Freed capacity is real if, and only if, four things co-occur: A beats B on story loss at widths where B passed the Step 1 criterion and not elsewhere; B's ablation scatter has a larger arithmetic-only component set than A's; the result probe reads out in B before the answer position and not in A; and A recovers arithmetic from fewer fine-tuning examples than a from-scratch model while B's story loss, not A's, is the one that moves when the arithmetic snippets are removed from the mix. If instead D beats everything by more than noise, the real cost is the arithmetic data occupying the stream, and the most a tool can ever save is the D − B gap, which bounds the hypothesis from above. If A beats C but not B, the chips are cheaper than the parse, an interesting result about format that says nothing about capacity.

## Conclusion

The most informative result the user has so far is not the 0.1-perplexity gap but the arithmetic null underneath it. It shows that at 1.35 M weights, two exposures per fact and a 1.3 % loss share, a transformer does not spend capacity on arithmetic; it ignores it, exactly as the toy-model allocation rule says it should, and the calculator is therefore not freeing anything at this budget but supplying a skill the model cannot acquire on its own. That reframes the hypothesis: a tool does not free capacity by removing a cost that was being paid, it lowers the importance of a feature class to zero, and the question that remains is which features get promoted in its place and whether they are language features worth having. Aggregate perplexity is the wrong instrument for that question even when the paying model exists, because the redeployed capacity is a few hundred neurons whose signature will be in frequent-word loss, agreement and tracking probes, and the ablation map, not in a headline number that seed noise can swallow.

The experiment is still worth running, and worth running properly, because it would be the first controlled test of the Mialon conjecture at any scale, and the synthetic setting can do what no published study could: hold the token stream identical across conditions, separate the cost of arithmetic from the cost of unlearnable targets and from the cost of chip tokens, and read every arithmetic failure per pair. The published record's silence below 100 M parameters is the opportunity. If the paying model turns out to need ten times the arithmetic exposure that the current stream provides, the compute-equivalent framing becomes the honest one: the tool's value is measured not in parameters freed but in the training data and steps the no-calls model needs to catch up, which is the kNN-LM result restated for arithmetic, and it is the result most likely to survive the seeds.
